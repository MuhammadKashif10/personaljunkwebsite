The item is one guitar. Empty the case, leave the neck whole, and keep that guitar as its own line. That is the job. A piano is not. A drum kit is not. A keyboard and an amplifier are not. A room of furniture is not.

This note is the guitar. The photo of one object and the narrowest point on the way out are the [bulky-item note](/blog/bulky-item-pickup-dubai). Whether a furniture piece can be passed on is the [donation guide](/blog/donate-furniture-dubai). Come back here when the question is one guitar, what is still in the case, and whether the neck is still the neck it had this morning.

## Make sure it is a guitar

A guitar, for this note, is one fretted instrument with a neck, a body and strings, made to be carried by one person. It may be acoustic, with a sound hole you can see. It may be electric, with pickups and a lead socket. It may be in a hard case. It may be in a soft bag. It may be bare, on a stand or on a bed. Write the shape you can see. “One acoustic, hard case, lid shut.” “One electric, soft bag, lead in the pocket.” “One guitar, no case, on a stand, strings on.”

The case is not a second guitar. If the guitar is in the case, they are one line, and you say so. If the case is empty and the guitar is beside it, they are still one line when the case belongs to that guitar. Write “case with the guitar” or “case empty, guitar beside it”. Do not book the case as a suitcase. A set of suitcases is the [suitcase note](/blog/suitcases-after-a-trip-dubai). Do not put the guitar inside a suitcase and call the result luggage.

A piano is not a guitar. Do not describe a piano, an upright, or a keyboard built into a piano cabinet as this job. This note does not confirm or refuse a piano. It stops a piano being booked under the name of a guitar. A piano is not given a URL here. If a piano is in the room, write “piano, not this note” and leave it out of these steps.

A drum kit is not a guitar. Shells, stands and cymbals, and the question of which rigid piece meets the lift, are the [drum-kit note](/blog/drum-kit-removal-dubai). Do not put a guitar in a cymbal bag and call it part of the kit. Do not follow the shell count on one instrument.

A musical keyboard and a small amplifier are two other objects. They are the [keyboard and amplifier note](/blog/keyboard-and-amplifier-removal-dubai). A computer keyboard is not a guitar and is not that pair. Do not coil a guitar lead into the amplifier’s cable bag and call the guitar part of the keyboard. If the amplifier is also leaving, it is its own line. This page does not become a band clear-out because the guitar was in the same room.

A bass, if that is the name you use for a larger fretted instrument with a longer neck, is not silently this guitar. Write “bass” and keep the same steps only if you are willing to say it is one instrument in a case, neck left whole. If you are not sure of the name, write the shape. “Four strings, solid body, longer neck” is enough. A ukulele is not a guitar. Write “ukulele” and keep it off a sentence that says guitar. This page does not open a URL for a ukulele or a bass.

A violin, a cello and any instrument that travels in a shaped case of its own are not this page if they are not a guitar. Name what you can see. Do not call a cello a guitar because both have strings. This page does not open a URL for them, and it does not confirm that they can be collected under this name.

A mixer, a set of speakers and a microphone are not a guitar. If they are leaving, they are their own lines. Do not bag a microphone inside the guitar case and then say the case holds only the guitar.

A stand is not the guitar. An X-stand, a tripod stand or a wall hanger is a second line if it is leaving. If the guitar already lifts off the stand, lift it off and write “off the stand”. Leave the stand as its own line or move it out of the photo if it is staying. A hanger that is still screwed to the wall is a fixing. Write “hanger fixed” and do not unscrew the wall. The guitar, if it already lifts off the hook, is loose. Lift it off and leave the hook in the wall. The hook is not the guitar.

A mannequin is not a guitar, and it is not given a URL here. A bookshelf, a partition and a filing cabinet are not given a URL here. If one of those is in the room, name it as what it is and keep it out of the photograph of the guitar.

If you are not sure, write the shape. “Wooden body, round hole, six strings, neck longer than the body” is a guitar. “Black slab, pickups, lead socket” is a guitar. A wrong name is worse than a plain description.

## Empty the case before you call it empty

Open the case only if it already opens by the catch or the zip it has. If the catch will not move, leave the lid shut and write “case will not open”. Do not pry it with a knife, and do not cut the zip to see inside. A case you have not opened is not an empty case. The honest line is the shut lid.

If it opens, look in the lid pocket and in the body compartment, not only at the guitar. Picks come out. A lead comes out. A strap comes out. A tuner, a capo, a slide, a pack of strings and a pencil come out. Sheet music comes out. A cable with a plug at each end comes out. This page does not book the paper. If you are keeping them, put them where you will still find them, off the photograph. If you have not decided, put them in one open pile that is not the leaving case, and write “pocket, not decided”.

Do not stuff the picks back in to make the photo look tidy. Do not bag the pocket and put the bag back in the case and call the case empty. If the small parts are leaving with the guitar, bag them and write “pocket bagged with the guitar”. You still have to be able to say the bag is not a second instrument.

Look under the neck, inside the case. A key, a card, a coin and a spare set of strings hide there. A key and a card stay with you. Anything you still need does not ride inside a case that is leaving.

The strings stay on the guitar unless they are already off. Do not cut the strings to make the neck shorter, and do not cut them to make the case shut. If a string is already broken and loose, you may wind it off by hand and bag it with the guitar. Write “broken string, bagged”. If the strings are on and in tune or not, leave them. This page does not ask you to prove the guitar plays.

A lead that already pulls out of the socket can come out. Coil it and bag it with the guitar if it is leaving, or keep it if you are keeping it. Write “lead out” or “no lead”. If the plug will not move, leave it and write “lead stuck in the socket”. Do not cut the lead. Do not open the back plate to see where the lead goes.

A battery door on an electric guitar, if it already opens by hand, may be opened. If you take the cells out, keep them with you and write “batteries out”. If the door will not open, do not pry it and do not crack the body. Write “battery door shut”. This page does not name a place to take a battery, and it does not open a sealed cavity to find one. Do not unscrew a plate that is not the battery door.

A strap button that is still on stays on. If a strap is clipped to the buttons, unclip it if the clip already opens, and bag the strap with the guitar or keep it. Write which. Do not pull a button out of the wood to make the body flatter.

Do not hose the guitar. Water on a floor has to go somewhere, and a wet body can swell. If the case smells, say so in plain words: “case empty, smells, not washed”. Do not spray a chemical into the case and then shut the lid. Do not oil the fretboard as a preparation step. This page does not say what oil to use.

A label with a name, an account or an address comes off if it already peels. If it does not peel, leave it and write “label still on”. A sticker on the case is part of the case. Do not scrape it off if it does not peel.

If you cannot see the body of the case because the guitar fills it, look in the pocket and write what you found. If there is no pocket, write “no pocket”. That is an empty check. Do not cut a window in the lid.

## A neck that stays on, and a case that will not shut

What makes one guitar a problem is the neck, the case, and a part that sticks out.

The neck stays on the body. Do not unscrew the neck to make a shorter shape. Do not take the headstock off. Do not saw the neck because the case is short. A neck that has already come off, because it was already loose in your hands, is a broken guitar. Write “neck off” and keep the neck with the body, in the case if it fits, or beside it if it does not. Do not glue it back on as a preparation step. The photo has to show the guitar you have.

A tremolo arm, if it already unscrews by hand, can come off. Bag it with the guitar and write “arm off, bagged with guitar”. If it will not turn, leave it and write “arm still on”. Do not bend it flat. Do not cut it off. The arm is part of the shape you measure if it stays on.

Try the case the way it was built to close, after the pocket is empty or after you have written why it is not. If it shuts, shut it and write “case shuts”. If it stops on the headstock, do not force the lid. Write “case will not shut, headstock proud” and leave it as it sits. Do not remove the lid by breaking the hinge. Do not stand on the lid. If the case has its own strap, you may use that strap and write “strapped with its own strap”. Do not invent a closure with tape across the strings.

A soft bag that already zips can be zipped. If the zip stops on the headstock, write “bag will not close” and do not cut the bag. A bag is not a reason to hide the length. You will measure the guitar or the closed case, and you will say which one you measured.

A crack in the body gets the words “body cracked”. Do not pick pieces out to make it look whole. A piece that has already come off goes in a bag with the guitar. Write “broken piece, bagged with guitar”. Do not tape the crack as a repair.

A loose bridge, a lifting pickup and a tuner that spins are the shape you have. Write what you can see. “Bridge loose.” “Pickup lifting.” “One tuner spins.” Do not pull the bridge off. Do not open the pickup to see the wire.

If you have already forced the neck and the joint is split, stop. Write “neck joint split” and do not press it back together.

Count after you have looked, not before. “One guitar” is a count when you can point at one. “The instruments” is not a count. A second guitar in the same room is a second line, with its own case and its own pocket. Do not add them in your head and write one number you did not check. If you move them together, count again after you move them. A left-handed guitar is still one guitar. Write “left-handed” if you can see it, so the photo matches the note. Do not take the nut off to change the hand.

## Where the guitar is standing

The guitar is awkward because of where it was left, as well as because of the case.

On a stand in a corner, lift it off if it already lifts. If a lead runs from the guitar to an amplifier, unplug the end you can see before you carry the guitar away. The amplifier, if it is staying, stays plugged into the wall or unplugged as its own decision. The amplifier, if it is leaving, is the keyboard note’s kind of object only when it is that small amplifier with a keyboard. A guitar amplifier on its own is its own line. Write “amplifier, own line” and do not open its case. This page does not become the keyboard note because a lead was plugged in.

On a bed or a sofa, move the covers you are keeping, then look under the guitar. A pick and a lead hide in the fold. Those come out.

Under a bed, do not drag it out by the headstock. If it slides by hand, slide it and write “slid out”. If a drawer blocks it and the drawer is staying, do not pull the drawer off its runners. Write “under the bed, not pulled out” and photograph it where it is.

On a wall hook, lift it off if it lifts. If a lock or a cable tie holds it to the hook, and the tie already comes off by hand, take the tie off and write “tie off”. If you would have to cut a lock that is not yours, stop. Write “locked to the hook” and leave it. This page does not cut a lock, and it does not decide who owns the guitar.

In a shared corridor, do not leave the guitar there the night before to “get it out of the room”. The place you photograph is the place it leaves from. A case you have not opened does not belong on a landing.

Beside a pile of packing, the cartons stay on the [guide to sorting packing material](/blog/packing-material-disposal-dubai) if that is the pile. Do not call the guitar a carton. Do not hide the guitar inside a carton and call the result boxes. A guitar used as a weight on a stack of magazines is still a guitar. The magazines are not this page. A magazine file is the [magazine-file note](/blog/magazine-file-holder-dubai). Do not put the guitar in the file.

In a child’s room, the room is not the guitar. A whole room of children’s furniture stays on the [children’s room guide](/blog/childrens-room-clearance-dubai) if that is the job. One guitar is this note. A cot is not given a URL here. Do not call the guitar a cot because it was in that room.

## One guitar, not a piano

The donation guide already says what has to be true before a furniture piece is passed on, and what to do when it is not. This page does not say a guitar can be given away, and it does not say it cannot. It does not name a recipient, and it does not invent a rule for instruments. If someone else is collecting the guitar, arrange that handover yourself. Keep it out of the leaving photos until they have collected it. A message that says “someone might want the guitar” is not a handover. If the guitar is leaving because it will not be passed on, the steps on this page are the preparation. The donation guide is not a second name for the case.

The drum-kit note keeps the shells and the stands. The keyboard note keeps the keyboard and the small amplifier. A piano stays unnamed as a job. Do not send a message that says “the music stuff” and expect those notes to guess which one object you mean.

You can book [a normal junk item](/services/junk-removal) once the case is empty or the shut lid is named, the neck is still whole or the break is written, and the guitar is its own line. That page is one visit for the things you have already decided are going. It is not a request to take a neck off, open a body, or collect a piano under this name.

When the guitar leaves with other furniture from the same home, you can [take it with the other furniture](/services/furniture-removal) and still keep it as its own line. That page is the furniture leaving the home. It is not a request to call the guitar a sofa, a wardrobe or a piano. Beds and wardrobes stay on that page as furniture. This guitar does not become a bed because it was on one.

The picks, the lead and the sheet music stay off this note once they are out, unless you have bagged them with the guitar and said so. The business removes items. It does not buy them, sell them or say what a guitar is worth.

A ceiling fan is not this page. Do not book a fan by calling it a guitar.

## Measure the guitar as it will travel

Measure after you have emptied the case, or after you have written why you could not, and not with a guess.

If the guitar will travel in the case, and the case shuts, measure the case: length, width and depth, including a handle that sticks out. Say “measured in the case”. If the case will not shut, measure the guitar itself, from the end of the headstock to the end of the body, and the depth including a bridge or an arm that sticks out. Write “case open, measured the guitar”. If there is no case, measure the guitar the same way. A strap left on can change the depth. Say whether the strap is on.

Then look at the opening it has to pass. The useful figures are the clear height and clear width of the narrowest door, with the door as open as it goes. A guitar under a bed still has to pass that door if it is coming out. A stand, a chair or a cable can change the gap. Move what you are keeping, then measure if the gap changed.

If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. This note does not keep a standard length for a guitar, a case or a doorway.

Write one sentence you measured. “Case shut, longer than the gap beside the bed” or “bare guitar, not yet compared with the door” or “not measured”. Do not write “it will fit” if you have not compared the figures. Do not write “it will not fit” from memory of a different door.

Weight is not something to guess. A case you cannot lift because it is still full of other instruments is not a weight, and it is not one guitar. Empty it first, or write why you could not. If the guitar is awkward only because of the length, write “long, carry by the case or the neck”. Do not write a number you did not weigh. Do not write a crew size. This note does not keep one.

The wider method for one object and the narrowest point on the way out stays with the bulky-item note if the corridor and the lift are the question. Stay on this page for the pocket, the neck and the count. Do not send two descriptions that disagree about whether the case was empty.

## One line in the booking

Say, in one message:

- one guitar, acoustic or electric or the shape, and where it stands
- case empty, or case will not open, and what you believe is inside
- case shuts, case will not shut, or no case
- neck whole, or neck off, or neck joint split
- lead out, lead stuck, or no lead
- batteries out, battery door shut, or no battery door
- the length and the widths you measured, or “not measured”
- that a piano is not included
- anything else leaving from the same room, as its own line, including a stand, a drum kit or an amplifier if that object is going

Do not write “the music room” as the only words if the room also holds a piano, a drum kit and a keyboard you have not decided about. Do not write “case empty” if you have not opened it. Do not ask for a neck to be cut because the figure is longer than the gap. The figure is the report. A cut is not a step on this page.

If you have not looked in the case, you are early. Open it if the catch already moves. If the catch will not move, the shut case is the honest line. Send that.

On the morning, put the guitar where the photos put it. Check a lead has not gone back in the pocket overnight. Check a neck you said was whole is still on. A pick dropped back inside is not an empty case. Take it out before you treat the old note as current.

Point at the body and the neck as the guitar. Point at the case as the case of that guitar. Point at a piano, a drum or a keyboard only if that object is its own line. The useful record is the sentence from the first check: one guitar, case emptied or the lid named, neck left whole or the break written, kept as its own line, written in plain words.
