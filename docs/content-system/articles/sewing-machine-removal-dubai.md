A route for electronics is not. A list of what a household load can include is not. A table the machine sits on is not.

## Make sure it is a sewing machine

A sewing machine, for this note, is one freestanding machine made to stitch cloth, with a needle, a bed, and a way of holding a bobbin. It may sit in a hard case. It may sit in a soft cover. It may sit bare on a table, with the case beside it. It may be a domestic machine with a handwheel you can see. It may be a small machine with a plug. Write the shape you can see. “One machine, hard case, lid shut, foot pedal beside it.” “One machine, soft cover on, no case.” “One machine, bare, needle still in, no plug visible.”

The case is not a second machine. If the machine is in the case, they are one line, and you say so. If the case is empty and the machine is beside it, they are still one line when the case belongs to that machine. Write “case with the machine” or “case empty, machine beside it”. Do not book the case as a suitcase. A set of suitcases is the [suitcase note](/blog/suitcases-after-a-trip-dubai). Do not put the machine inside a suitcase and call the result luggage.

A vacuum cleaner is not a sewing machine. A vacuum has a dust compartment, a hose and sometimes a battery door. That machine is the [vacuum note](/blog/vacuum-cleaner-removal-dubai). Do not empty a dust bin and call it a bobbin area. Do not follow the hose steps on a sewing machine.

A pedestal fan is not a sewing machine. A fan that will not fold, standing on its own base, is the [pedestal-fan note](/blog/pedestal-fan-removal-dubai). A ceiling fan is not this page. Do not book a fan by calling it a sewing machine.

A dehumidifier is not a sewing machine. A unit with a tank in a closed room is the [dehumidifier note](/blog/dehumidifier-removal-dubai). Do not tip water out of a sewing machine. There is no tank on this page.

One microwave in an office pantry is not this machine. That unit is the [pantry-microwave note](/blog/office-pantry-microwave-dubai). A home iron, a garment steamer and a kettle are small appliances on a wider list. Name them as what they are and keep them off these steps. Do not bag an iron inside the sewing case to save a line.

An overlocker, if that is the name on the machine, is not this sewing machine. Write the name you can see and keep it off these steps. This page does not open a second URL for it. A serger you cannot name stays as “machine, not identified” until you can point at a needle and a bobbin area. A wrong name is worse than a plain description.

A computer, a printer and a monitor are not this machine. One monitor from a home desk stays on its own note. Do not unplug a screen to make a sewing machine look clearer, unless the plug is in the way of a photograph of the machine and you already know that plug.

A cabinet the machine drops into is furniture. If the cabinet is leaving, it is its own line, and you do not call the cabinet a sewing machine. If the machine will not lift out of the cabinet by the movement it was built with, write “in a cabinet, not lifted out” and do not saw the cabinet. This page does not unscrew a hinge to free a machine, and it does not give a sewing cabinet its own URL.

A mannequin, a dress form and a magazine file are not this machine. A mannequin is not given a URL here. A filing cabinet, a bookshelf and a partition are not given a URL here. If one of those is in the room, name it as what it is and keep it out of the photograph of the machine.

If you are not sure, write the shape. “Box with a needle, a handwheel and a flat bed” is enough. “Black case, lid shut, weight at one end” is enough when you have not opened it yet. Do not guess a brand you cannot see.

## Empty the bobbin area before you call the case empty

Start with the case, if there is one, because that is where small things hide.

Open the lid only if it already opens by the catch or the zip it has. If the catch will not move, leave the lid shut and write “case will not open”. Do not pry it with a knife, and do not cut the zip to see inside. A case you have not opened is not an empty case. The honest line is the shut lid.

If it opens, look in the tray, not only at the machine. Thread spools come out. Bobbins come out. A seam ripper, a pack of needles, a pair of scissors, a pin and a measuring tape come out. A manual comes out. This page does not book the paper, and it does not tell you which pile a spool belongs in. If you are keeping them, put them where you will still find them, off the photograph. If you have not decided, put them in one open pile that is not the leaving case, and write “notions, not decided”.

Do not stuff the spools back in to make the photo look tidy. Do not bag the notions and put the bag back in the case and call the case empty. The bag is a second object if it is leaving on its own. If the notions are leaving with the machine, the bag stays with the machine and you write “notions bagged with the machine”. You still have to be able to say the bag is not a second machine.

Look under the machine, inside the case, not only in the tray. A foot, a screwdriver, a spare bulb and a key hide under the bed. A key stays with you. Anything you still need does not ride inside a case that is leaving.

Then the machine itself, if you can lift it out or if it is already bare. The bobbin area is the part under the needle where the bobbin sits. If a cover already slides or lifts by hand, lift it and take the bobbin out. Write “bobbin out” or “no bobbin inside”. If the cover will not move, leave it and write “bobbin cover shut”. Do not lever it with a knife. Do not unscrew the hook to make the bed look empty.

A needle that already lifts out of the clamp, by the screw or the lever the machine has, can come out. Bag it with the machine. Write “needle out, bagged with the machine”. If the clamp will not move, leave the needle and write “needle still in”. Do not snap the needle off. Do not cut it with scissors. A loose needle on the floor is not a preparation step. If one is already on the floor, pick it up and bag it with the machine, and write “loose needle, bagged”.

A presser foot that already lifts off by the lever it has can come off. Keep it with the machine. Write “foot off, with the machine”. If it will not move, leave it and write “foot still on”. Do not bend it to make the machine shorter.

Pins in a cushion are not the machine. The cushion comes out of the case if it was in the case. If you are keeping the pins, they stay with you, off the photograph. If you have decided the cushion is going, it is its own small line or it is bagged with the machine, and you say which. Do not leave a cushion of pins loose in the case and call the case empty.

Do not hose the machine. Water on a table has to go somewhere, and a wet machine is heavier and can stain the top. If the bed is sticky, say so in plain words: “bed sticky, not washed”. Do not spray a chemical into the bobbin area and then shut the case. Do not oil the machine as a preparation step. This page does not say what oil to use, and it does not name a place to take a bottle of oil. If a bottle is in the case, it comes out. If you do not know what the bottle is, write “bottle, not identified” and do not pour it into the sink.

A label with a name, an account or an address comes off if it already peels. If it does not peel, leave it and write “label still on”. Do not scrape a table to remove a sticker that is on the table rather than on the case.

If you cannot see the bobbin area because the machine will not come out of the case, do not cut a window in the case. Write “bobbin area not seen” and what you believe is still in the case. That machine stays out of any sentence that says it is empty.

## A case that will not close, and a machine that will not lift out

What makes one machine a problem is the case, the cord, and whether the machine will lift out of the thing it sits in.

Try the lid or the zip the way it was built to close, after the tray is empty or after you have written why it is not. If it shuts, shut it and write “case shuts”. If it stops on a spool, the spool is not out. Go back one step. If the lid is bent and will not sit, write “case will not shut” and leave it as it sits. Do not remove the lid by breaking the hinge. Do not stand on the lid to flatten it. Do not tape the lid shut as a repair. Tape is not a hinge. If you use a strap the case already has, write “strapped with its own strap”. Do not invent a closure.

A soft cover that already pulls off can come off for the photo of the needle, then go back on if that is how the machine will travel. Write “soft cover on” or “soft cover off, with the machine”. Do not cut the cover to make a shorter shape.

The foot pedal is not the machine. It is a second line if it is leaving, and you say “pedal with the machine” so it is not lost. Unplug the pedal from the machine if the plug already pulls out by hand. Coil the lead and keep it with the pedal. If the lead is fixed into the machine and you cannot see a plug, write “pedal lead fixed” and do not cut it. Do not pull the lead out of the casing. A cut lead is a different object from the machine you started with.

Find the power plug, if there is one. Unplug it from the socket you can see. Coil that lead and keep it with the machine. Write “unplugged”. If you cannot see a plug, write “lead, no plug visible” and do not cut the lead. A machine with a lead you cannot trace is still this note. It is an honest line.

A battery door, if the machine has one and it already opens by hand, may be opened. If you take the cells out, keep them with you and write “batteries out”. If the door will not open, do not pry it and do not crack the case. Write “battery door shut”. This page does not name a place to take a battery, and it does not open a sealed housing to find one.

A handwheel that already turns can be left as it is. Write “handwheel turns” or “handwheel stuck”. Do not take the wheel off to make the machine narrower. Do not open the side panel to see the motor. The motor stays closed. Write “case of the machine closed” for the housing, so that sentence is not confused with the carrying case.

If the machine sits in a cabinet and already lifts out on the hinges it has, lift it out, set it where it cannot slide, and write “lifted out of the cabinet”. The cabinet, if it is staying, is not this line. The cabinet, if it is leaving, is a second line. Do not unscrew the cabinet into the machine to make one object. If the lift will not move, leave the machine where it is and write “will not lift out”. Do not saw the table top.

A foot that has already come off the machine goes in a bag with the machine. Write “foot off, bagged with machine”. Do not glue it back on as a preparation step.

If you have already forced a panel and the housing is split, stop. Write “housing split” and do not press it back together. The photo has to show the machine you have.

Count after you have looked, not before. “One sewing machine” is a count when you can point at one. “The sewing things” is not a count. A second machine in the same room is a second line, with its own case and its own bobbin check. Do not add them in your head and write one number you did not check. If you move them together, count again after you move them.

## Where the machine is standing

The machine is awkward because of where it was left, as well as because of the case.

On a table, move it only if it already lifts and you can hold it. If a cable runs under the machine, follow the cable to both ends before you pull. A lead you are keeping stays with the lamp or the computer it serves. Write “cable under the machine” if you cannot free it without unplugging something you have not identified. Do not cut the lead to get the machine off the table.

In a cupboard, on a high shelf, do not drag it down by the cord. Lift what you can hold, or leave it and write “on the high shelf, not brought down”. A machine that falls and splits the case is a different shape from the one you were going to measure.

Wedged between a table and a wall, do not kick it out. If it slides by hand, slide it and write “slid out”. If the table has to move first and the table is staying, do not drag the table off its feet to reach a case. Write “behind the table, not pulled out” and photograph it where it is.

In a shared corridor or a bin room, do not leave the machine there the night before to “get it out of the flat”. The place you photograph is the place it leaves from. A case you have not opened does not belong on a landing.

Beside a pile of cloth, the cloth is not the machine. If you are keeping the cloth, move it off the photograph. If you have not decided, write “cloth, not decided” and keep it out of the case. This page does not book clothes. Clothes are not given a URL here.

A machine used as a weight on a pile of cartons is still a sewing machine only if that is the object. The cartons stay on the [guide to sorting packing material](/blog/packing-material-disposal-dubai). Do not call the machine a carton because it was on the pile. Do not hide the machine inside a carton and call the result boxes.

## Measure the machine as it will travel

Measure after you have emptied the case, or after you have written why you could not, and not with a guess.

If the machine will travel in the case, and the case shuts, measure the case: height, width and depth, including a handle or a foot that sticks out. Say “measured in the case”. If the case will not shut, measure the shape you actually have, and write “case open in the measure”. If the machine is bare, measure the machine, including the handwheel and any foot that sticks out. Say “measured bare”. If the pedal is a second line, measure it on its own, including the lead only if the lead is fixed and will not coil. A coiled lead is not a width.

Then look at the opening it has to pass. The useful figures are the clear height and clear width of the narrowest door, with the door as open as it goes. A machine on a high shelf still has to pass that door if it is coming out. A table, a chair or a cable can change the gap. Move what you are keeping, then measure if the gap changed.

If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. This note does not keep a standard size for a sewing machine, a case or a doorway.

Write one sentence you measured. “Case shut, handle wider than the gap beside the table” or “bare machine, not yet compared with the door” or “not measured”. Do not write “it will fit” if you have not compared the figures. Do not write “it will not fit” from memory of a different door.

Weight is not something to guess. A case you cannot lift because it is still full of notions is not a weight. Empty it first, or write why you could not. If the empty case is awkward because of the shape, write “awkward to carry”. Do not write a number you did not weigh.

The wider method for one object and the narrowest point on the way out stays with the [bulky-item note](/blog/bulky-item-pickup-dubai) if the corridor and the lift are the question. Stay on this page for the bobbin area, the case and the count. Do not send two descriptions that disagree about whether the case was empty.

## One line in the booking

Say, in one message:

- one sewing machine, in a case or bare, and where it stands
- bobbin area empty, bobbin cover shut, or bobbin area not seen
- case shuts, case will not shut, or no case
- housing closed, or housing split
- pedal with the machine, or no pedal
- unplugged, or lead with no plug visible
- batteries out, or battery door shut, or no battery door
- the height and the widths you measured, or “not measured”
- anything else leaving from the same room, as its own line, including a cabinet if the cabinet is going

Do not write “the cupboard” as the only words if the room also holds a vacuum, an iron and a pile of cloth you have not decided about. Do not write “case empty” if you have not opened it. Do not ask for a handwheel to be cut because the figure is larger than the gap. The figure is the report. A cut is not a step on this page.

If you have not looked in the case, you are early. Open it if the catch already moves. If the catch will not move, the shut case is the honest line. Send that.

On the morning, put the machine where the photos put it. Check a spool has not gone back in overnight. Check a needle you removed is still bagged with the machine, not loose on the table. A pin dropped back inside is not an empty case. Take it out before you treat the old note as current.

Point at the machine as the machine. Point at the pedal only if it is its own line with the machine. Point at a cabinet, a vacuum or a pile of cloth only if that object is its own line. The useful record is the sentence from the first check: one sewing machine, bobbin area emptied or the cover named, case shut or the reason written, housing left closed, kept as its own line, written in plain words.
