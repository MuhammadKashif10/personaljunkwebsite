The items are a stack of waiting-room chairs. Count them, say they are a stack, and leave the desks, the reception counter and the task chairs at the workstations off this note. A booking that says “the chairs” will be read as every seat on the floor. This note is the waiting-room stack only.

Chairs that leave with a dining table stay on the [dining-table guide](/blog/dining-table-removal-dubai). Dining chairs that leave while the table stays are the [dining-chairs note](/blog/dining-chairs-without-the-table-dubai). Tall stools that will not stack are the [bar-stool note](/blog/bar-stools-that-will-not-stack-dubai). A floor of desks, task chairs and cabinets is the [office-furniture guide](/blog/office-furniture-removal-dubai). A shop being closed, with its own asset list, is the [shop-clearance guide](/blog/shop-clearance-dubai). Come back here when the only seats in the question are this counted stack from a waiting room.

## Count the stack before you name it

Stand in front of the chairs and count the ones that are leaving. Write the number. “Fourteen waiting-room chairs, stacked in the reception” is a count. “The seating” is not. If some chairs in the same room are staying, count those too and write “staying”. A photo of a pile does not replace the number. Count them, then photograph them.

Look at whether they are one model. Identical backs, identical legs, identical colour. If they match, write “one model”. If two shapes are in the same pile, split the count: “ten with arms, four without”. Do not write one number for two shapes. The person quoting the job needs the split, because a mixed pile is not one stack.

Write the room. A reception, a clinic waiting room, a lobby inside the office, a corridor used as a waiting space. “Waiting room, tenth floor” is a name. “Downstairs” is not. If the stack has already been moved to a store, say so: “stack now in the store, was in reception”. Do not describe them as still in reception if you moved them yesterday.

Look at how they are stacked. Nested, one on another, or standing in a row that someone has called a stack. If they nest, write “nested” and say whether the pile is stable. If they are only leaning, write “leaning, not nested”. Do not push a leaning row into a tighter pile to make the photo look smaller, and then describe a stack you have not checked. If a chair is cracked, leave it out of the nest and write “one cracked, not in the stack”.

If the chairs are still in use in the waiting room, and the stack does not exist yet, you are early. This page is the stack that is leaving. Write “still in use, not stacked” and do not book a pile you have not made. Making the stack means placing chairs that already nest, by hand, in the room. It does not mean forcing chairs that will not nest.

## Say what kind of chair is in the stack

A waiting-room chair, for this page, is a side chair from that room: a seat, a back, and legs or a sled, often with or without arms, often meant to sit in a row. Write the words you can see. “Plastic shell, metal legs” is a description. “Chair” is not.

Look at the feet. Glides, wheels, or a sled. Write which. If a glide is missing, write “glide missing” and say which corner as you face the chair: front left, front right, back left, back right. If a chair rocks, write “rocks” and say which one in the count, or “several rock” if you have checked more than one. Do not check only the top chair and then describe the stack as sound.

Look at the arms. If some have arms and some do not, you already split the count. If an arm is loose, write “arm loose”. If an arm cap is split, write “arm split”. Do not snap an arm off to make the chair narrower. The width with the arms on is the report.

Look at the linking clips, if you can see them. Some waiting-room chairs have a small bracket that joins one chair to the next. If the clips are already undone and in your hand, bag them and write “clips off, bagged”. If they are still joining a row, write “still linked” and count the row as it stands. Do not break a clip to separate a row. If you can release a clip with your fingers, and you already do that when you move these chairs, you may release it and write “clips off, by hand”. If you need a tool, leave the row linked.

A writing tablet on an arm, if one chair has one, is part of that chair. Write “tablet arm” and say whether it folds by hand. If it folds, fold it and write “tablet folded”. If it does not, leave it and write “tablet fixed”. Do not saw the tablet off.

Upholstery is part of the description. Cloth, vinyl, or a hard shell with no pad. If a pad is torn, write “pad torn” and say which chair if you can, or “several torn” if that is what you see. Do not tape a tear and then describe the seat as whole. Do not remove a pad. A loose pad that is already off stays with that chair. Write “pad off, with chair”.

## How the stack meets the doorway

Walk the path the stack has to take, or the path one chair has to take if the stack will be broken back into single chairs for the door. Open each door as far as it opens. The clear width is the gap you can see. A mat in the opening is part of the width until you move the mat.

Measure one chair: width, depth and height, including the arms and the back. Then measure the stack as it stands: the height of the pile and the width of the widest chair. If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. This page does not keep a standard door size.

If the stack is taller than the door, say so. “Stack is taller than the reception door” is a useful sentence if that is what you can see. Do not solve it by cutting a leg. The choice is to carry fewer chairs at a time, which you describe by the single-chair size, or to say the route is not clear. Write which. Do not assume a stack that fits the room will fit the door.

Name the door that limits the chairs. If a second door is tighter, name that one too. A stack that leaves the waiting room and stops at the lift lobby has not left. If you have already been given a lift or loading note, add it. If you have not, write “no lift instruction yet”. Do not invent a lift size. Do not write a building rule you have not been given.

If other people use the waiting room, do not leave a tall stack in the doorway overnight. Leave the stack in the room, counted, with a clear path around it if the room is still in use. If the room is closed, say “room closed”. A stack in a live corridor is not the gap you photographed.

A goods lift, a passenger lift and a stair are different routes. Write the one you have been told to use. If you have been told nothing, write that. Do not choose a stair because the stack looks light. A count of chairs is not a weight. This page does not keep a weight for a chair.

## What already comes off, and what stays on

Feet that unscrew by hand may come off if you already do that when you stack these chairs. Bag the feet with the chair they came from and write “feet off, by hand”. If you need a spanner you do not already use, leave the feet on. Do not saw a leg to shorten a chair. Do not remove every foot “to be helpful” and then lose which chair they belong to.

A loose screw in the seat stays with that chair. Bag it and write “screw bagged, with chair”. Do not start a dismantling job. Furniture that was built to come apart, where the screws are the work, stays on the [dismantling guide](/blog/furniture-dismantling-dubai). A waiting-room chair that already stands as a chair is not that job. Do not take the seat off the frame to make the stack flatter.

A wheel that comes off in your hand is a fact. Bag it with the chair and write “wheel off”. Do not pull the other wheels to match. If the chair is a sled with no wheels, do not describe it as a wheel chair because a neighbour in the stack has wheels. Split the count.

Look under the seat for a label, a number or a sticker the office uses. If you can read it, write the words. If the sticker says the chair belongs to a named company or a landlord, that is a reason to ask before it leaves. A worn seat is not permission. The person who may release the stack writes that release. This page does not interpret a contract.

If a chair is broken into pieces already, count the pieces with that chair and write “frame and seat separated”. Keep the pieces together. Do not add the pieces to a rubbish sack and drop the sack out of the count. The chair is still a chair in the number, marked as separated.

Cleanliness is not a test on this page. Write “soiled seat” if you can see it. Do not claim a chair is clean if you have not looked. Do not soak a stack to improve the photo. Water on a stack is a condition. Write “stack wet” if that is what you see, and say why if you know. If you do not know, write “wet, cause not known”.

## What is not this stack

A task chair at a desk, with a gas lift and wheels meant for a workstation, is not a waiting-room chair. The office-furniture guide keeps the desks and those task chairs. An office chair is not given its own URL. Name a task chair on the office-furniture list if the floor is being cleared, or leave it. Do not add it to this stack because it is also a chair. Do not retitle a task chair as a waiting-room chair to use this note.

A dining chair from a home, or a chair leaving a dining table, is the dining guide that matches it. A bar stool is the bar-stool note. A high chair is not this stack. An armchair in a reception, if it is one lounge chair and not part of the identical run, is its own line. Do not hide one armchair inside the stack count.

A restaurant floor of banquet chairs is not this stack. That job stays blocked as its own article. This page does not give a banquet room a method. If the seats are identical chairs in an office or clinic waiting room, you are in the right place. If they are a dining room of a restaurant, you are not. Write the room honestly. Do not call a banquet room a waiting room to use this note.

A reception counter, a coffee table, a magazine rack and a plant are not the chairs. If they are leaving, they have their own lines on the office-furniture guide or on the shop guide if the site is a shop. A whole shop is the shop-clearance guide. Use this page when the stack is the question, including when the shop guide is also open for the rest of the unit. Do not turn this note into the shop.

A whole office floor is the [office clearance](/services/office-clearance) around a working floor, and the office-furniture guide for the inventory. Use those when the desks are in the job. Use this page for the stack. The stack can be one line inside a larger office booking. It is still counted here, in its own words, so it is not lost inside “chairs”.

A bench fixed to a wall or to the floor is not a stack of chairs. If you can see bolts into the building, write “fixed” and leave it off this note. Do not unbolt a bench to make a stack. A row of loose chairs that happen to face the same way is this page if they are the waiting-room seats and they are leaving. A fixed beam with seats moulded into it is not.

Chairs you are moving to another branch to keep using are not a collection. This note is for chairs that are leaving the property. If another office will use them, say that to the person who is moving them. Do not use a disposal booking as a way to relocate a stack you still want.

A single chair from a home, with no stack and no waiting room, is not this page. One chair does not become a stack because this URL exists. Leave a single household chair on its own line, or on the furniture page that already matches it. Do not pad this count.

## Photos of the stack, not the floor

Use it only as a reminder that task chairs at desks are a different group. Your own photos still have to show the waiting-room stack, with the number visible.

Send these views.

- One chair out of the stack, from the side, so the back, the arms and the feet are visible.
- The feet: glides, wheels, or a sled.
- A torn pad, a missing glide, a loose arm or a separated seat, if you can see one.
- The doorway the stack or the single chair has to pass, open as far as it goes.

Add the room name and the floor. Add the number leaving and the number staying. Add “one model” or the split. Add “nested” or “leaning”. Add the single-chair size and the stack height, or “not measured”. Add a lift or loading note you have already been given.

If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Take the photos in the light the room already has. If that light does not work, say so. A photo of the whole floor, with the waiting room as a corner, does not show a missing glide.

## One line in the booking

You can [clear the chair stack](/services/furniture-removal) once the number is written, the room is named, and the note says the desks are not in this count. That page is the furniture leaving the room. It is not a request to unbolt a fixed bench, and it is not a request to clear a restaurant floor.

If the rest of an office is leaving in the same visit, say so as separate lines on the office-furniture guide. Do not hide a desk, a task chair or a box of cables inside the waiting-room count. Those lines are not this stack.

Say, in one message:

- waiting-room chairs, the room, and the floor
- the number leaving, and the number staying
- one model, or the split
- nested, leaning, or still linked
- arms, tablets, glides or wheels, if you can see them
- a loose arm, a missing glide, a torn pad or a separated seat, if you can see it
- single-chair size and stack height, and the clear width of the door that limits them, or “not measured”
- anything that is not this stack: task chairs, a reception counter, banquet chairs, bar stools, a fixed bench

Do not write “the chairs” as the only words. Do not write “nested” if the row is only leaning. Do not ask for a leg to be cut because the stack is taller than the door. The number and the door are the report.

If the chairs are still in use and not stacked, you are early. Stack only the ones that already nest by hand, or book them as a counted row and say they are not nested. Send the chairs you can stand in front of and count.

A second stack in another waiting room is a second count, with its own room name. Do not add the numbers in your head and send one total unless you also send both room names and both photos. A total without a room is the wrong booking.

If a written asset list says the stack stays with the building, leave it off the note. The person who may release it is the person who writes that release. This page does not decide ownership from the colour of the chairs.

The office-furniture guide remains the inventory when the floor is the job. The shop-clearance guide remains the asset list when the site is a shop. This page is the stack. Use it so the waiting-room chairs are counted on their own, and so a task chair at a desk is not renamed to join them.
