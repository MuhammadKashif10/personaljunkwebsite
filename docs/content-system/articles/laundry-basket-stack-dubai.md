The item is a stack of laundry baskets. Empty each one, try the nest it already has, and keep the stack as its own line. That is the job. A drying rack on the balcony is not. An ironing board behind a door is not. A pile of cardboard from a move is not. The clothes that were in the baskets are not.

This note is the baskets. What a household bin and a chute are for is the [bins guide](/blog/what-dubai-bins-wont-take). Cartons, film, foam and timber from a move stay on the [guide to sorting packing material](/blog/packing-material-disposal-dubai). Come back here when the question is the stack of baskets, what is still inside them, and whether they will sit inside one another.

## Make sure it is a laundry basket

A laundry basket, for this note, is a container made to hold clothes on the way to a wash or on the way back. It may be a rigid plastic tub with holes. It may be a hamper with a lid and a fabric liner. It may be a folding frame with a cloth bag. It may be a set of two or three that were sold to nest. Write which ones you have, and how many. “Four plastic tubs, holes in the sides, two handles each.” “One hamper, lid on, liner still in.” “Two folding frames, bags still clipped on.”

A plastic crate left after an unpack is not a laundry basket. A crate is a packing box with a lid or open sides, and the [plastic-crate note](/blog/plastic-crates-after-unpacking-dubai) is that stack. Do not call a crate a basket because both are plastic and both can be stacked. Do not call a basket a crate because you want one note for every plastic container in the room.

A drying rack is not a basket. A rack is a frame for wet clothes, with wings, a scissor or tiers. The [drying-rack note](/blog/drying-rack-removal-dubai) is that frame, the fold and the railing. A basket standing beside the rack is this page when the basket is what is leaving. Do not put the rack inside a basket and call the pair one item.

An ironing board is not a basket. A board has a long top, a cover and legs. The [ironing-board note](/blog/ironing-board-removal-dubai) is that board and the door it was blocking. A basket in the same gap is its own line once you have decided it is leaving. Do not describe the gap as “the baskets” if the thing stopping the door is the board.

A soft bag, a tote or a sack of clothes is not a basket. Write “bag of clothes” and keep it off these steps. This page does not tell you where clothes go. If the clothes are leaving, they are not renamed as a basket. If they are staying, they leave the photograph.

A pedal bin, a bathroom bin or a swing bin is not a laundry basket. It is a bin. Empty it if you are discarding it, and name it as a bin. Do not follow the nest steps below as if a foot pedal were a laundry handle.

A basket that is screwed to a wall, or built into a cupboard as a pull-out frame on rails, is a fitting. Write “fixed basket, staying” and do not unscrew the cupboard. This note starts when the basket already stands on the floor, or when it lifts off a rail by the movement it was built with. If it will not lift off, leave it and write “will not lift off the rail”.

If you are not sure, write the shape you can see. “Plastic tub, oval, two handles, holes in the sides” is enough. A wrong name is worse than a plain description.

## Empty each basket before you stack it

Take one basket off the stack. Look at the bottom, not only at the top. A sock, a peg, a belt, a coin, a bottle of detergent, a packet you do not recognise. Those are not the basket.

Clothes come out. Wet clothes come out. A damp towel comes out. This page does not book the clothes, and it does not tell you which container they belong in. If you are keeping them, put them where you will still find them, off the photograph. If you have not decided, put them in one open pile that is not the leaving stack, and write “clothes, not decided”. A basket that still holds a wash is not an empty basket, even if you plan to deal with the wash later.

Do not roll the clothes into the basket to make the photo look tidy. Do not bag the clothes and then put the bag back in the basket and call that empty. The bag is a second object. The basket is the shell.

A liner in a hamper comes out if it already lifts out. Shake it. Write “liner out” or “liner kept”. If the liner is clipped and the clips already open by hand, open them. If a clip will not move, leave the liner in and write “liner stuck”. Do not cut the liner out to make the hamper look empty. Do not cut the cloth to make a smaller load.

A lid comes off if it already lifts or unclips by hand. Write “lid off” and keep the lid with that basket, not in a pile of lids you can no longer match. If the lid will not come off, do not lever it with a knife. Write “lid stuck” and “inside not fully seen”. That basket stays out of any sentence that says the stack is empty.

Look under a false bottom if the basket has one that already lifts. If it does not lift, leave it and write “base not lifted”. Do not drill a hole to see underneath.

A bottle, a pod container or a box of powder is not the basket. If you know it is ordinary washing product you are already discarding, it can leave as its own small line, named. If you do not know what the bottle is, it does not go back into the basket and it does not get hidden under a lid. Leave it out and write “bottle, not identified”. This page does not tell you to pour it away.

Do not hose the baskets to make them look cleaner. Water in a flat has to go somewhere, and a wet stack is heavier and still the same shape. If a basket smells, say so in plain words: “empty, smells, not washed”. Do not spray a chemical into a lidded hamper and then shut the lid.

Keys, a card, a letter and a charger come out and stay with you. Anything you still need does not ride inside a basket that is leaving.

## The nest that will not close

What makes a stack of baskets a problem is the nest. They were often sold to sit inside one another. When the handles, the rims or the sizes no longer allow that, the stack is as tall as the baskets are, and forcing them cracks a rim or snaps a handle.

Try one pair at a time, by hand, the way you would put them away.

If a smaller basket already drops inside a larger one without you standing on it, nest that pair and write “these two nest”. If it stops halfway, stop. Write “will not nest” and leave them separate. Do not stand on the rim. Do not hit the inner basket with your hand to drive it down. A stuck pair stays stuck.

Handles are the usual reason. A handle that already folds flat by the hinge it has can be folded before you try the nest. Write “handles folded”. A handle that sticks up, and will not fold, is included in the shape. Write “handles up, will not fold”. Do not saw a handle off to make the basket shorter or to make it fit inside the next one. A cut handle is a sharp edge, not a nested stack.

Different brands often look the same and do not nest. Write “two sizes, will not nest” if that is what you see. Do not file a rim, and do not heat the plastic to soften it. This note does not include a repair.

A cracked rim gets the words “rim cracked”. Do not pull the cracked piece off so the photo looks clean, unless it has already come away in your hand. A piece that has already come off goes in a bag with that basket. Write “broken piece, bagged with basket”. Do not glue it as a preparation step.

A folding basket with a cloth bag closes only if the frame already folds by the catch it was built with. If it folds, fold it and write “frame folded, bag still on” or “frame folded, bag off”. If the catch will not move, leave the frame open and write “frame will not fold”. Do not bend the tubes. Do not cut the cloth to collapse the frame.

A hamper with a lid that hinges shut can be closed if the hinge still works. If the lid is bent and will not sit, write “lid will not shut” and leave it as it sits. Do not remove the lid by breaking the hinge.

If you have already forced a nest and a basket is split, stop. Write “split” and do not press it back together. The photo has to show the shape the stack actually has.

Count after you have tried the nest, not before. “Four baskets: two nested, two will not nest” is a count. “A stack” is not a count. If you cannot see the bottom of the pile, you do not know the number. Take off what you can lift, count those, and write “lower ones not counted” for the rest.

## Where the stack is standing

The stack is awkward because of where it was left, as well as because of the nest.

Behind a door, with an ironing board, the board is the other note. Move the baskets only if they move without forcing the board. If the door hits the baskets, write “door hits the stack” and do not keep pushing. A basket that is the only thing in that gap, with no board, is still this note. Say where it stands.

On a balcony, beside a drying rack, the rack is the other note. A basket tied to the railing with a loose strap can be untied. Write “strap off”. A chain and a padlock are not a loose strap. If you do not have the key, write “locked to the railing” and stop. Do not cut a chain, and do not ask for the railing to be cut. A basket screwed to the balcony is fixed. Write “fixed” and do not unscrew the building.

In a laundry cupboard beside a washer or a dryer, the machine is its own line if it is leaving, with its own disconnection. Do not strap a basket to a machine to make one item. Do not pull a machine out to reach a basket if you cannot see the hoses. Write “basket behind the machine, not pulled out” and photograph it where it is.

Under a shelf, or on top of a cupboard, do not drag the stack down by one handle. Lift what you can hold, or leave it and write “on the high shelf, not brought down”. A basket that falls and splits is a different shape from the one you were going to measure.

Nested already, and stuck, is the same rule as a pair that will not nest. Do not kick them apart. If they will not come apart by hand, measure them as they are and write “stuck nested, left together”.

A basket used as a bin for bathroom rubbish is still a laundry basket only if that is the object. The rubbish comes out. Do not book a basket of mixed bathroom waste as an empty basket. If you do not know what is in it, write “contents not identified” and do not tip it into another basket.

In a shared corridor or a bin room, do not leave the stack there the night before to “get it out of the flat”. The place you photograph is the place it leaves from. A stack you have not counted does not belong on a landing.

## One stack, not the laundry

A second stack in another room is a second line, with its own count and its own nest. Do not add them together in your head and write one number you did not check. If you do combine them in one place, count again after you move them.

The bins guide already says what a chute is for. A rigid basket does not become chute waste because it is plastic, and it does not become acceptable in a chute because you have emptied it. Stay on that guide for the container question. Stay on this page for the stack. Do not post a basket into a chute to test the fit.

The packing guide already sorts cartons, film, foam and timber. A basket is not a carton. Do not put flattened cardboard inside the baskets and call the result packing. Do not hide the baskets inside a carton and call the result boxes. If both are leaving, they are two lines.

A project pile from a clear-out or a refit is a different load. If the baskets are sitting in that pile, [leave the stack out of the project sacks](/services/waste-removal) and say in the message that the baskets are a separate line. That page is the project load. The baskets are not part of it because they were in the same room.

The clothes, the pegs and the detergent stay off this note. A wider household load that happens to include the baskets, once each basket is empty or honestly marked, can still use these steps one basket at a time. The list of what else the home holds stays on the acceptance pages that already own that list. This note stays the stack.

Do not put tools, a paint tin, food waste or a battery inside a basket and shut a stuck lid. If the lid will not open, the basket had to be empty before you stopped trying. If you already cannot open it, say what you believe is inside, and do not add more.

A basket you are giving to someone who still wants it is not a collection. Arrange that handover yourself. Keep it out of the leaving photos until they have collected it. A message that says “someone might want it” is not a handover.

The business removes items. It does not buy them, sell them or say what a basket is worth.

A ceiling fan, a wall fan and a fan fixed on the balcony are not this page. Do not book a fan by calling it a laundry basket.

## Measure the stack as it will travel

Measure after you have tried the nest, not before, and not with a guess.

If two baskets nested, measure that pair as one shape: height, and the width and depth of the outer basket, including handles that still stick out. Then measure each basket that would not nest, the same way. If a handle folds, fold it before you measure, and say that you did. If it does not fold, include it.

Then look at the opening the stack has to pass. The useful figures are the clear height and clear width of the narrowest door, with the door as open as it goes. A basket on a high shelf still has to pass that door if it is coming out. A machine, a rack or a board can change the gap. Move what you are keeping, then measure if the gap changed.

If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. This note does not keep a standard size for a basket or for a laundry door.

Write one sentence you measured. “Four baskets, two nested, outer one wider than the cupboard door” or “handles up, not yet compared with the door” or “not measured”. Do not write “it will fit” if you have not compared the figures. Do not write “it will not fit” from memory of a different door.

Weight is not something to guess. A basket you cannot lift because it is still full of wet clothes is not a weight. Empty it first. If a nested pair is awkward for one person, write “awkward to lift”. Do not write a number you did not weigh.

The wider method for one object and the narrowest point on the way out stays with the [bulky-item note](/blog/bulky-item-pickup-dubai) if the corridor and the lift are the question. Stay on this page for the nest, the contents and the count. Do not send two descriptions that disagree about whether the handles folded.

## Photos of the baskets, not the machines

The picture is a washer and a dryer in a utility room, with a sink beside them. It does not show a laundry basket, a handle, a lid, a nest or a stack. Use it only as a reminder that the machines are a different line. Your own photos still have to show this stack.

Send these views.

- The whole stack, in the shape it will leave in, nested or separate.
- One basket from above, empty, so the bottom is visible, or the words “lid stuck” and what you believe is still in it.
- A handle that will not fold, or a rim that is cracked, close enough to see it.
- The count, written, if it is not obvious from one photo.
- The door or the cupboard only if that opening is why the stack is stuck.
- The clothes only if they are still in a basket, so the photo matches the note.

Add where the stack stands. Add the sentence about the nest. Add a lift or loading note if you have been given one. If you have not, write “no lift instruction yet”. Do not invent a lift size, and do not write a building rule you have not been given.

## One line in the booking

You can book [a trash pickup for the baskets](/services/junk-removal) once each basket is empty or the stuck lid is named, the nest has been tried, and the count is a number. That page is a single pickup or a mixed household load. It is not a request to saw a handle, force a nest, or sort the clothes for you.

Say, in one message:

- a stack of laundry baskets, how many, and where it stands
- empty, or which basket still has a stuck lid and what you believe is inside
- which ones nested, and which ones will not
- handles folded, or handles up
- the height and the widths you measured, or “not measured”
- anything else leaving from the same gap, as its own line
- that it is not a drying rack, not an ironing board, and not a carton

Do not write “laundry” as the only word if the room also holds a machine, a rack and a wash you have not decided about. Do not write “they nest” if you have not tried the pair. Do not ask for a handle to be cut because the figure is larger than the gap. The figure is the report. A cut is not a step on this page.

If you have not emptied the top basket, you are early. Empty the ones you can open. If a lid will not move, the stuck lid is the honest line. Send that.

On the morning, put the stack where the photos put it. Check a wash has not gone back in overnight. Check a lid you removed is still with its basket. A towel dropped back inside is not an empty basket. Take it out before you treat the old count as current.

Point at the shells as the baskets. Point at a rack, a board or a pile of clothes only if that object is its own line. The useful record is the sentence from the first check: a stack of laundry baskets, emptied or the stuck lid named, the nest tried, kept as its own line, written in plain words.
