The new furniture is in the room, and the wrap it came in is now bigger than the furniture. Foam sheet, bubble wrap, stretch film, corner guards and tape. Before any of it is called rubbish, unroll it once, take out what was taped inside it, and bag it so it cannot spread across the flat. The wrap is the job on this page. The sofa is not.

## Check whether the delivery team takes it

Look at the delivery message or the receipt for a line about packaging. Some delivery teams take the wrap away when they leave. Some unwrap in the corridor and take everything. Some leave it all in the room. The answer is in what they wrote, not in what usually happens.

If the note says the team takes the packaging, and they have not yet left, ask them before they go. Point at the pile. If they agree, it is theirs. If they have already gone and the note said they would take it, write down what the note said and the day, and ask the seller about it before you book anyone else. Do not bag it into a junk load while that question is open. The same wrap should not be collected twice.

If the note says nothing, or says the packaging stays, the wrap is yours. Write “retailer did not take packaging” in your note. That one line saves a question later.

A verbal “they normally take it” is not a promise you can rely on. If there is no sentence, write “not stated”.

## Keep it if the piece arrived damaged

Look at the furniture before you get rid of a single sheet. Check the fabric, the corners, the legs, any glass and any mirror. Open the drawers and the doors. If something is scratched, torn, cracked or missing, stop.

A damaged delivery is a question for the seller, and the wrap may be part of the answer. A tear in the foam over a torn arm, or a crushed corner guard over a dented corner, shows where the damage happened. Some sellers ask to see the packaging, or ask for it to be kept until they have looked. This note does not say what any seller requires, and it does not interpret a return policy. It only says: do not throw away the evidence before you know whether you need it.

Photograph the damage and the wrap that was over it, before you move either. Keep the wrap that covered the damaged part in its own bag, labelled “kept for the seller”. Write the date you reported the damage, and what the seller said. If the seller says the packaging can go, write that down, with the day.

If the piece is going back, the seller may want it rewrapped. Keep enough foam and film to do that, and keep it apart from the pile that is leaving. A piece that has to be returned with no wrap left is a harder return.

Until the seller has answered, the wrap from a damaged piece is not part of the pile on this page. Leave it out of the photos you send for a pickup, and leave it out of the bag count. Write “one bag kept for a damage report” so nobody collects it by mistake.

If nothing is damaged, say so in your note: “furniture checked, no damage”. Then carry on.

## Unroll it before you bag it

Furniture is wrapped in layers. A sofa may have foam sheet against the fabric, stretch film over that, and cardboard corners taped on top. A table may have foam pads on each corner and film around the legs. A wardrobe may arrive in panels, each in its own foam sleeve.

Things are taped inside those layers on purpose. The small bag of feet for the sofa. The bolts for the bed. The allen key. The spare buttons and the fabric care card. The shelf pins for the wardrobe. The remote for a recliner. The warranty card. The glass top for a coffee table, in its own sleeve, wrapped separately.

Before any wrap goes in a bag, unroll each piece once on a clear patch of floor. Run your hand along it. Look inside every sleeve and every folded corner. Shake the film out. It takes a few minutes, and it is the only way to be sure that nothing you need is leaving with the rubbish.

Put everything you find on the furniture it belongs to, or in one box labelled with the piece. Write “wrap checked, parts removed” in your note when you have done it.

If the furniture has not been assembled yet, stop. Do not throw away any wrap until the piece is built and you know every part is out. A missing bolt is easy to find in a pile of foam on the floor. It is hard to find in a bag that has already gone.

## Name what is in the pile

Delivery wrap is not one material, and the person reading your booking will picture the pile from your words. Sort it into these groups, and say which ones you have.

Foam sheet. Thin, soft white or grey sheet that comes on a roll or in loose lengths. It is light and takes up a lot of room. It is not the same as the hard white blocks that come with an appliance.

Bubble wrap. Clear sheet with air pockets. Often around glass tops, mirrors and legs.

Stretch film. Thin clear plastic wound tight around the piece. Once cut, it tangles. Roll it into a ball as you take it off.

Corner and edge guards. Shaped pieces of foam, plastic or cardboard that sit on corners and edges. Some are hard foam. Some are folded board.

Tape and straps. Plastic strapping bands, packing tape, labels.

Cardboard. A carton, an inner sleeve, a board sheet under the piece. If there is a lot of board, the packing-material guide has the method for flattening it and the test for board that is wet or dirty.

Write the groups you have in plain words. “Foam sheet, stretch film, bubble wrap from one sofa and one coffee table, plus four cardboard corners.” That is a description. “Packaging” is not.

## Keep it small, and keep it in one place

Soft wrap expands. A pile that looked like one armful in the delivery team’s hands fills a corner of the room once it has been cut off.

Fold foam sheet, do not crumple it. Lay it flat, fold it in half, and keep folding until it is a square you can hold. Stack the squares. Folded foam sheet takes a fraction of the room of loose foam.

Roll bubble wrap into a cylinder and tie it, or tape it once. Do not pop it to make it smaller. It takes time and does not save much space.

Squeeze stretch film into a ball as you take it off. Put the balls in one bag.

Put corner guards in one bag or one box.

Cut plastic strapping into short pieces before it goes into a bag. Long straps spring out and catch on things.

Use clear bags if you have them, so the contents can be seen. If the bags are black, write on each one what is inside: “foam sheet”, “film”. Tie each bag. A bag of loose film left open in a corridor is a bag that blows open.

Do not overfill a bag until it splits. Do not tape several bags into one large bundle that nobody can lift by the neck. Bags that can be lifted one at a time are easier for everyone.

If you are keeping some of the wrap for a later move, put that in a separate bag and keep it out of the photos. Write “keeping one bag of bubble wrap”. The rest is the pile.

## What is not the wrap

The furniture is not the wrap. It stays.

The old furniture that the delivery replaced is not the wrap either. If an old sofa or an old bed is also leaving, it is its own line. The [old-sofa note](/blog/old-sofa-before-new-delivery-dubai) and the delivery-day note cover the order of the two visits and what happens to the old piece. The wrap can leave on the same visit. On the booking, it is still a separate line from the furniture.

Wrap that was on the old furniture, put on by you to protect it while it waited, is part of that old piece. Leave it on the old piece unless the people carrying it ask for it to come off. Do not mix it into the new delivery’s pile.

The hard white blocks from an appliance are the polystyrene note. If a fridge and a sofa arrived on the same day, you have two piles. Keep the blocks and beads out of the foam-sheet bags. They behave differently, and the beads spread.

A wooden pallet or a timber crate is not wrap. If a piece arrived on one, it is its own line. Do not break it up to fit it into bags.

Fabric samples, a care booklet, an invoice and a manual are not wrap. Put them with the furniture or with your papers.

A mattress bag is a special case. A new mattress often comes in a thick plastic sleeve. The sleeve is wrap. The old mattress it replaced is not. The [mattress guide](/blog/mattress-disposal-dubai) keeps the old mattress. If you are putting the old mattress into the new sleeve to carry it out, write that: “old mattress, in the new mattress bag”. Do not describe the old mattress as wrap.

## Do not leave it in the corridor

Delivery wrap often ends up in the corridor outside the flat, because that is where the delivery team unwrapped the piece. It is not a storage place.

Bring it inside. A pile of foam and film in a shared corridor blocks the way for neighbours, can block a fire door, and spreads when a door opens and closes. Light film and foam move with any draft.

Do not leave bags by the bin room or the service door to see whether someone takes them. Do not push foam down a chute. Soft wrap can jam a chute, and the building may not accept it there. This page does not set out a building’s rules for packaging, and it does not say what a recycling container in your building will take. Ask the building or read the packing-material guide, which covers sorting packaging by material before you ask.

If the building has given you an instruction about delivery packaging, follow it, and write it in your note. If it has not, write “no building instruction about packaging”. Do not invent one.

## How much is it, really

Wrap is light. Volume is what matters. A single sofa can leave enough foam sheet and film to fill several large bags. A bedroom set in flat panels can leave more.

Count the bags once the pile is bagged. Write the number. “Five large bags, tied, plus one bundle of cardboard corners.” If some wrap is still loose, write that too: “two loose foam sheets, folded”.

If you have not bagged it yet, photograph the pile with something of known size next to it, such as a door or a chair, and write “not bagged yet, about the height of the chair”. Do not describe a pile as small because it is light.

The person reading the booking is deciding how much space the visit needs. A number of bags is the best answer you can give.

## One line in the booking

You can [collect the wrap with the junk](/services/junk-removal) once it is checked, sorted and bagged. That page is a single pickup or a mixed household load. It is not a request to sort a pile that still has bolts in it, and it is not a recycling promise.

If the old furniture is leaving on the same visit, the [furniture removal](/services/furniture-removal) page is the old piece. Name it as its own line. The wrap is a second line.

Before the visit, use the [before-the-crew-arrives checklist](/blog/before-the-crew-arrives-dubai) for the route to the door. Keep the bags inside the flat, together, out of the walkway.

Say, in one message:

- wrap from a furniture delivery, and which pieces it came from
- whether the delivery team took any packaging, or “not stated”
- wrap checked, parts removed
- the groups: foam sheet, bubble wrap, stretch film, corner guards, straps, cardboard
- the number of bags, and anything still loose
- anything that is not wrap: a pallet, a crate, polystyrene blocks, old furniture, an old mattress
- the floor and any lift or loading note

Do not write “some packaging”. Do not write “a few bags” without a number. The wrap leaves once the parts are out of it and the bags are counted.
