The item is one plastic playhouse in a villa garden. Empty it, name how it meets the ground, and measure the path from where it stands to the gate. That is the job. A trampoline is not. A slide is not. A children’s room indoors is not. A patio set is not.

This note is that one hollow house. A trampoline, with its mat, frame and anchors, is the [trampoline guide](/blog/trampoline-removal-dubai). A playpen indoors, whose fold has stopped, is the [playpen note](/blog/playpen-removal-dubai). A whole children’s room, with more than this house leaving, is the [children’s-room guide](/blog/childrens-room-clearance-dubai). Chairs and a table on the patio are the [garden-furniture guide](/blog/garden-furniture-disposal-dubai). Come back here when the question is one plastic house on the lawn or the tiles, what is inside it, and whether the path to the gate will take it.

## Make sure it is a plastic playhouse

A plastic playhouse, for this note, is one hollow shell a child can stand or crawl inside. It has a roof, or what is left of a roof. It has a doorway, a window opening, or both. It may have a floor. It may sit open at the bottom. It may be one moulded piece, or several panels clipped together. Write the shape you can see. “One pink shell, door flap, floor, on the back lawn.” “Panels, roof lifts, no floor, on the side tiles.” “One house, porch roof cracked, standing against the boundary wall.”

The shell is one line when the panels still belong to this house. A loose shutter that came off this house stays with this house. A slide that was bought as a separate toy is not this house. A slide is not given a URL here. Write “slide beside it, separate” if a slide is leaving, and do not bolt the two descriptions together. A swing frame is not this house either. A swing frame is not given a URL here. Name it as a swing if that is what it is.

A trampoline is not a playhouse. A trampoline has a mat, a frame and, often, springs or bands and a net. The trampoline guide is that structure. Do not call a house a trampoline because both are in the garden and both are large. If both are leaving, they are two lines. Do not photograph the house through the trampoline net and call them one play area.

A playpen is not this house. A playpen is an indoor pen, mesh or panels, and the playpen note is the fold. Do not follow playpen steps on a shell that has a roof and stands in a garden. Do not follow these steps on a pen that folds in a bedroom.

A children’s room indoors is not this house. The children’s-room guide is the order for that room: what is kept, what is a cot, what is a bunk, what is a toy. A cot and a bunk are not given a URL from this page. This page starts when the object is outside, and it is a plastic house.

Garden furniture is not this house. A plastic table and chairs on the same patio are the garden-furniture guide. A sun-bleached chair is not a wall of the house. If the chair is leaving, it is its own line. Do not stack the chairs inside the house and call the result one item. Hidden chairs fall out when the shell is lifted, and they change the weight.

A timber shed, a pergola, a gazebo and a fence are not this house. They are not given a URL here. If the object has timber posts in the ground, or a roof of wood rather than a plastic shell, stop and name what you can see. Do not unscrew a post to make a playhouse out of a shed.

An above-ground pool frame is not this house. It is not given a URL here. A paddling pool that is only a ring of plastic, with no walls a child walks through, is not this house either. It is not given a URL here. Write what you see, and leave these steps.

If you are not sure, write the material and the opening. “Plastic walls, a door, a child can step inside, back garden” is enough. “Metal frame with a mat” is a trampoline. A wrong name is worse than a plain description.

## Empty it before anyone moves it

The house is heavier than the plastic looks, because of what is inside it. Toys, a small chair, cushions, a box of sand, a watering can, a pool of water from the last rain. All of that comes out before the house is described as ready.

Take out every toy you can reach. Put the toys you are keeping somewhere that is not inside the shell. If a bag of toys is also leaving, it is its own line, written as toys, not as the house. This page does not tell you where toys go, and it does not open a note for them.

Look for sand. A tray of sand, a bucket, or sand that has been swept into the corners. If the sand is still inside the house, you can gather it into a bag you can point at, and write “sand inside, gathered”. If the sand has already been dumped as a pile on the lawn, that pile is not the house. The [soil and pots guide](/blog/soil-sand-pots-disposal-dubai) is a pile of sand or soil. Do not kick the sand across the grass to make the photo tidy. Do not tip it into a storm drain.

Tip out standing water if you can do that by lifting a corner that already moves, or by letting it run out of the doorway onto the ground it already sits on. Write “water emptied” or “water still inside, too heavy to tip”. Do not hose the house. A wet shell is heavier, and a hose left running is a second problem. If the floor is damp, say “floor damp, not washed”.

Check the roof void and any window box. A ball, a shoe and a set of keys sit in the places a child can reach and an adult forgets. Anything you still need does not ride inside a house that is leaving.

Do not cut a window out to make the emptying easier. A cut panel is a sharp edge, and it does not make the shell smaller in a way this note uses. If a panel is already split, write “panel split” and say which face. Leave the split as it is.

If two houses are leaving, they are two lines. Do not nest a small shell inside a large one and write one size. A shell inside a shell looks like one house in a wide photo. Count them apart, on the ground, before you put one inside the other for the camera.

## How it meets the ground

What makes this house awkward is not only its size. It is how it sits.

A house that only rests on tiles, with no pegs and no bolts, is freestanding. Write “sitting on tiles, no fixing seen”. You may look under the edge if the edge already lifts with one hand. If it does not lift, do not lever it. Write “will not lift, fixing not seen”.

A house held by tent pegs or by straps you can see, and that unclip or pull out by hand, may be released that way. Write “pegs out, by hand” and keep the pegs with the house, in one bag. If a peg bends and stays in the ground, stop. Write “one peg stuck”. Do not hammer it sideways until the tile cracks.

A bolt into a slab, a bracket into a wall, or a post set in concrete is a different fact. Write “bolted” or “post in concrete” and leave it. Do not break the slab. Do not dig the post out. On-site work that means excavation, a broken footing or a repair to the ground is not a step on this page. The trampoline guide makes the same split for a frame and its anchors. This page is the plastic house. Show the bolt. Do not hide it under a mat to make the house look loose.

Sandbags, bricks or a water weight on the floor are not the house. Lift them off if you can do that without spilling, and write what they are. A full water weight is heavy. If you cannot empty it by a cap that already opens, write “weight still full, cap stuck” and leave it in the photo. Do not stab the weight to drain it.

If the house belongs to a landlord, a developer or the community, and it is not clearly yours, leave it off the booking until the release is in writing. A collection is not permission to take a structure you do not own. Write “on inventory, waiting” or “released in writing”. This note does not decide ownership at the gate.

Look at the ground you would expose. Dead grass, a stain, a peg hole, a tile that is already cracked. Write what you can see. Removing the house does not include laying new turf, replacing tiles or filling a hole. If that work is needed, it is a separate decision, and it is not booked by calling the house a garden clearance.

## The path from the lawn to the gate

A hollow house at the back of a lawn is a long carry. The useful measurement is not a shop width. It is the path from where the house stands to the gate a vehicle can reach.

Walk that path before you move the house. Name it in plain words. “Back lawn, side passage, front gate.” “On the tiles by the pool, then three steps, then the side gate.” If a side gate is locked and you do not have the key, write “side gate locked”. Do not climb a wall with a panel to avoid the lock.

Measure the tightest point on that path. A side passage between the villa wall and the boundary. A gate leaf. A turn at a condenser or a bin store. Write the clear width, or write “not measured”. Do not invent a gate size. This note does not keep a standard gate, and it does not keep a standard playhouse size.

If the shell is wider than that gap, it does not pass while it is still a shell. Write “wider than the side passage” or “wider than the gate” only if you have compared the two. If you have not, write both figures, or write “not compared”. Do not drag the house to the gate to test the sentence. A shell that jams in a passage blocks the only way out, and a split panel leaves sharp plastic on the route.

If you are checking a municipal collection, the eligibility, the item list and the staging instructions stay on [the municipal bulky-waste guide](/blog/dubai-municipality-bulky-waste). This page does not add a rule about what that service will take from a garden, and it does not tell you to drag the house to the street to find out. A house that sits too far from the gate for you to move it unaided is a carrying fact. Write the distance in steps you have walked, or write “not walked”. Keep the house where it stands until the route, and the collection, are the next thing that happens.

Do not leave the house in the drive overnight so it is nearer the road. A shell in a shared drive is in the way. The municipal guide is where staging instructions live. This note does not copy them, and it does not invent a kerb rule.

The [villa-garden guide](/blog/villa-garden-waste-removal-dubai) is the walk around every zone when the job is cuttings, pots and furniture as well as this house. Stay on that guide for the zones. Come back here for the shell. A photo of bagged fronds, with the house behind a bush, describes the wrong object.

The [bulky-item note](/blog/bulky-item-pickup-dubai) is the photo and the route for one awkward object. Use it for the way out. Stay on this page for the plastic, the ground and the fact that this is a house and not a trampoline.

## Panels that already come apart

Some of these houses were sold as panels. A roof may lift off clips. A wall may unclip from the floor. A door flap may already be off its pin. If a joint opens by hand, the way you already open it to store the house, you may open it. Write “roof off, by hand” or “walls unclipped”. Keep every panel on the lawn where you can count them. Do not carry one wall to the gate and leave the rest for later without saying so.

If a clip is stiff, brittle or split, stop. Write “clip stuck” or “clip broken”. Do not saw a panel to make it narrower than the gate. Do not stamp on a roof to flatten it. A house that leaves whole, because it will not come apart, is an honest line. “Will not come apart” is more useful than a pile of jagged pieces.

Count the panels after they are apart, if you took them apart. A roof, four walls and a floor are six pieces, not one, once they are separated. Write the count. Bag the clips and the plastic bolts. Tape the bag to the largest panel, or put it inside the roof and keep the roof with the walls. A house that arrives as four walls and a missing roof clip has become a different job.

The dismantling guide for furniture is the [furniture dismantling guide](/blog/furniture-dismantling-dubai). A playhouse whose clips already open by hand is not that furniture job. Do not start on a glued joint, a bolted slab or a timber post because a wardrobe page talks about screws.

If the house is one moulded shell with no clips, write “one shell, no panels”. Do not cut a doorway wider. The doorway it has is the doorway you describe.

Sun-brittle plastic cracks when it is flexed. If a wall already flexes and crazes, write “brittle, cracks when flexed” and do not keep flexing it to prove it folds. It does not fold. That sentence is the warning.

## One house, not the garden

A playhouse that has sat at the end of the lawn for years is one object. The villa-garden guide is how you separate cuttings, soil, pots and outdoor furniture across the plot. The garden-furniture guide is the table and the chairs. This page is the house. Do not book “the garden” because the house is the thing you are tired of.

If the trampoline and the house are both leaving, list them as two lines. The trampoline guide keeps the mat and the anchors. This page keeps the shell. A request that says “the play area” will be read as whichever structure is in the first photo. Say the house.

If the only other things leaving are ordinary outdoor items you have already named, they can travel in the same visit once the house is its own line. Ask for [a pickup for this house](/services/junk-removal) and put the house in the same message as that line. Do not use that sentence to mean a trampoline booking, a load of branches, or a whole-villa clear-out. Those stay on their own pages.

A hose by the tap is not this house. A grill on the patio is not this house. Name them if they are leaving, on their own notes, and keep them out of the photograph that is meant to show the shell.

Do not promise the house will be stacked flat on a vehicle. You do not know the load. You know the shell as it sits, or the panels as you counted them. The wide photo and the words “path to the gate” are the report.

## Photos of the house, not the lawn

![Weathered outdoor chairs and a table beside stacked cushions and empty pots on a patio.](media:garden-furniture-disposal-dubai-cover)

The photo above is outdoor chairs, a table, cushions and pots. It is not this house. It does not show a plastic shell, a door flap, a peg or a gate. Use it only as a reminder that the thing in the frame has to be the thing you named. Your own photo has to show the house.

Take the photos the request needs.

- One wide photo with the house and the ground around it, so a trampoline or a patio set in the same garden is visible if it is staying or leaving.
- The doorway and one wall, close enough to see a split, a missing shutter or a clip.
- The ground contact: tiles with no fixing, pegs, a bolt, or a note that the underside was not seen.
- The tightest point on the path to the gate, with the house still where it stands, not jammed in the gap.
- The panels laid out and counted, only if you have already taken them apart by hand.

Do not send a photo of the lawn from the upstairs window and write “playhouse at the back” with no view of the walls. A house behind a bush is invisible. Move the camera, or move a branch aside, until the shell is the object in the frame.

If sand or toys are still inside, include them. A photo of an empty-looking doorway, when a bucket of sand is in the back corner, describes half the job.

If you have two houses, photograph them apart, then say the count.

## One line in the booking

Say, in one message:

- one plastic playhouse, the shape, and where it stands
- emptied, or what is still inside: toys, sand, water
- sitting free, pegs out, bolted, or underside not seen
- one shell, or panels unclipped, with the count
- the path to the gate, and the tightest width, or “not measured”
- that it is not a trampoline, not a slide, and not a shed
- anything else leaving from the same garden, as its own line

The eligibility and the staging for a municipal collection stay on [the municipal bulky-waste guide](/blog/dubai-municipality-bulky-waste). Ask for [a pickup for this house](/services/junk-removal) when the house is the piece you want taken and you are not waiting on that other route. The trampoline guide stays the frame and the mat. The garden-furniture guide stays the patio set. The children’s-room guide stays the indoor room.

Do not write “the garden” as the only words. Do not write “it will come apart” if the clips have not opened. Do not ask for the shell to be cut down because a gate would then close. A cut is not a step on this page.

If you have not looked inside, you are early. Empty what you can reach. If a bolt holds it to a slab, that bolt is the honest line. Send that. If the object is a trampoline, a slide, or a timber shed, you are on the wrong note. Name it and leave these steps.

On the morning, look at the doorway again. A toy put back overnight is still part of the line. Take it out before you treat yesterday’s photo as current. Point at the house as the house. Point at a trampoline only if the trampoline is its own line.

The useful record is one plastic playhouse, emptied or the contents named, ground contact written, path to the gate measured or marked “not measured”, kept as its own line, written in plain words.
