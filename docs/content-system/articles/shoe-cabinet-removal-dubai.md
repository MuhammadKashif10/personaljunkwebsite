The item is one shoe cabinet in a narrow hall. It has doors or a lid, it is full of shoes, and it stands in the tightest part of the flat. Empty it, measure it where it stands, and say whether it is fixed to the wall. That is the job. The rest of the hall is not.

This note is the cabinet. A freestanding coat stand in the same entry is the [coat-stand note](/blog/coat-stand-removal-dubai). A whole flat of furniture is the [apartment furniture guide](/blog/apartment-furniture-removal-dubai). A piece that was built to come apart, and that you have already agreed to take apart, is the [dismantling guide](/blog/furniture-dismantling-dubai). A glazed cabinet that displays objects, with loose glass shelves, is the [glass display-cabinet note](/blog/glass-display-cabinet-removal-dubai). ## Make sure it is a shoe cabinet

A shoe cabinet, for this note, is a piece of furniture whose job is to hold shoes. It may be a low cupboard with doors, a taller cupboard with shelves, a bench with a hinged lid, or a narrow cabinet that stands against the hall wall. Write which one you have, in plain words. “Low cupboard, two doors, three shelves.” “Bench with a lid.” “Tall cabinet, one door.”

A coat stand is not this cabinet. A stand has hooks and no shelves of shoes. If the object in the hall is a pole with hooks, use the coat-stand note. Do not call a stand a cabinet because it is in the same corner.

A built-in cupboard that is part of the flat is not this cabinet. If the door is the same as the other doors in the hall, and the box is a recess in the wall, it stays with the building. Write “built-in cupboard, staying”. Do not take the door off to make a piece of furniture out of a recess.

A wardrobe is a wardrobe, even when it stands in the entry and holds shoes on the bottom. The [wardrobe guide](/blog/wardrobe-removal-dubai) keeps it. A chest of drawers is not this page either. It is not given a URL here. Name it as a chest if that is what it is, and keep it off this note.

A console table with a bowl of keys is not a shoe cabinet. The [console-table note](/blog/console-table-removal-dubai) is that table. A mirror on a hook is not a shoe cabinet. A bench with no storage, that you sit on and that holds nothing inside, is a bench. Name it as its own line if it is leaving. Do not group the hall and call the group one cabinet.

If you are not sure, photograph the front with the doors open and write “not sure it is a shoe cabinet”. That is a better line than a wrong name.

## Empty it before anyone moves it

The cabinet is heavier than it looks because of what is inside it. Shoes, shoe boxes, a spare pair still in a bag, an umbrella, a folded jacket, a set of keys, a small box of documents. All of that comes out before the cabinet is described as ready.

Open every door and every drawer. Lift the lid if it is a bench. Look on the top, behind the doors, and on the floor of the cabinet. Run a hand along the back of each shelf. People keep passports, spare keys, jewellery and cash in a shoe cabinet because it is by the door. Find those before the cabinet leaves.

Take the shoes out. Count the pairs if you can, or write “shoes out, not counted”. Put the shoes you are keeping in a box or a bag that stays in the flat, out of the photos of the cabinet. Shoes are not this cabinet. This page does not tell you where shoes go, and it does not open a separate note for them. If a bag of shoes is also leaving, it is its own line, written as shoes, not as the cabinet.

Take out boxes, bags, umbrellas and papers. If a paper matters, it stays with you. Write “cabinet emptied” only when you have looked in every compartment. If one door will not open, write “one door stuck, not emptied”. Do not force that door to finish the sentence.

Do not leave the shoes inside to save a trip. A cabinet that leaves full will spill on the stairs, and the weight is the shoes, not the wood. Do not fill the empty cabinet with other rubbish to save space. Hidden contents fall out on the route, and they change what the load is.

## The hall is the problem

The cabinet was put in the hall because the hall was the only place left. Many entries are a short corridor. The front door is at one end. A cupboard, a kitchen opening or the coat stand is along one side. The cabinet fills the width that remains.

Measure the cabinet where it stands, before you drag it into the doorway to “see if it fits”. Width across the front, height from the floor to the top, and depth from the wall to the front face. If a door handle or a hinge sticks out, include it in the depth. If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. This note does not keep a standard size for a shoe cabinet.

Then look at the hall, not at a catalogue. The useful figures are the clear width of the hall beside the cabinet, and the clear width and height of the front door when it is open as far as it goes. If a door closer, a light or a detector sits in that opening, say so. Write “door closer above the frame” if you can see one. If you have not been given a lift size or a loading rule, write “no lift instruction yet”. Do not write a building rule you have not been given.

A cabinet that is deeper than the clear gap beside it cannot be turned in the hall. That is the fact to write down. “Depth greater than the gap beside it” or “turns in the hall” or “not tried”. Do not tip it onto its face in the hall to test the turn. A tipped cabinet blocks the only door, and a glass door meets the floor.

If the coat stand is still in the corner, it is in the way of this measurement. The stand is its own note. If the stand is staying, measure the cabinet with the stand still there, and say “coat stand staying”. If the stand is also leaving, it is a second line. Do not write “the hall” as one item.

Shoes left on the floor, a mat, and a pile of bags are part of the route. Move them before you measure the door, or measure with them in place and accept the narrower figure. A width taken in an emptied doorway, when the doorway will not be empty, is the wrong width.

## Doors, shelves and a mirror

Many of these cabinets have doors that lift off, shelves that slide out, and a mirror on the inside of a door or on the front. Decide what comes off by hand, and leave the rest on.

A door that lifts off pins you can see, with no tool, may come off. Lift it straight up, the way it was hung. Lay it flat against a wall, glass inward if the door has glass, with a towel between the glass and the wall. Write “door off, by hand, with cabinet”. Keep the pins with the cabinet if they come out. If the door does not lift, leave it on. Write “doors left on”.

A shelf that slides out with no screws may come out. Stack loose shelves flat, not on edge, and keep them with the cabinet. Write how many. “Two shelves out.” If a shelf is screwed or pinned and you do not already undo that fixing by hand, leave it in. Write “shelves left in”.

A mirror that is a separate panel, held by clips you can turn by hand, may come off. Wrap nothing tightly across the glass. Stand the mirror with the glass toward a wall and a towel between, or leave it on the door. Write “mirror off” or “mirror left on”. If the mirror is glued or screwed and you would need a tool you do not use on it, leave it. A cracked mirror is named, not hidden. Write “crack on the lower door” if that is what you see. Do not tape over the crack to make the photo look tidy.

Loose glass, a broken clip, or small fragments on the floor of the cabinet come out. Sweep them into a rigid box, not a soft bag, and keep the box with the cabinet. Write “loose fragments, in a box”. Do not put glass in with the shoes you are keeping.

This note does not become a mirror guide, and it does not become the glazed display cabinet. If the piece is a cabinet of glass shelves for objects, and shoes were never the point, stop and use the glass display-cabinet note. If it is a shoe cabinet that happens to have one glass door, stay here and name the glass.

Do not saw a door, a shelf or a side to make the cabinet shorter. Do not take the back off. A cabinet that has been broken down into boards is a different job, and it is the dismantling guide only when taking it apart was the agreed method. If you have not agreed that, leave it standing.

## Fixed to the wall, or standing free

Some shoe cabinets stand on the floor and are only held by their weight. Some are screwed to the wall so they cannot tip in a narrow hall, especially a tall one. Some have a bracket you cannot see until you look behind the top.

Look. A bracket, a strap, a screw through the back into the wall, or a fixing into the skirting. Write what you can see. “Two screws through the back, into the wall.” “No fixing seen.” “Could not see the back.”

If a screw is one you already turn by hand or with a screwdriver you already use on it, and the cabinet is yours to release, you may take that screw out. Have someone, or a hand, steady the cabinet while the last screw comes out. A tall cabinet tips forward when the top is released. Write “released, by hand”.

If the fixing needs a tool you do not have, or you are not sure the cabinet is yours to take off the wall, leave it. Write “still fixed”. Do not lever the back off the wall. Do not pull the cabinet forward against a screw that is still in. The holes, and who fills them, are a question for the person who owns the wall. This note does not decide that, and it does not describe a repair.

In a furnished rental, the cabinet may be on the inventory. Check the inventory before you book. If it lists a shoe cabinet, a hall cabinet or a shoe bench, it stays unless the landlord or the agent says, in writing, that it may go. This note does not interpret the inventory. Write “on the inventory, staying” or “written release, leaving”, and keep the release with your papers, not inside the cabinet.

In a shared flat, the cabinet may belong to someone who has already left. The [note on furniture a flatmate left](/blog/flatmate-left-furniture-dubai) covers whose it is. Use that note before you send this cabinet away on someone else’s behalf.

If the cabinet is yours, write “mine, leaving”.

## What else shares the hall

The cabinet is one line. The other things in the hall are other lines, or they stay.

The coat stand, if it is leaving, is the coat-stand note. The floor lamp, if it is the tall object, is the [floor-lamp note](/blog/floor-lamp-taller-than-doorway-dubai). A clothes rail on wheels is not given a URL here. Name it as a clothes rail on the apartment furniture list if it is leaving.

A shoe rack that is only wire or plastic shelves, with no doors and no box, is not this cabinet. It is a rack. If it is leaving, write “shoe rack” as its own line. Do not call it a cabinet to use this page. If it comes apart by hand into flat shelves, say so. Do not bend it until it breaks.

A doormat, a key bowl, an umbrella bucket and a mirror on a hook are each their own line if they are leaving. They are small. They are not the cabinet, and they are not a reason to describe the job as “the hall”.

Bags of shoes you removed from the cabinet stay out of the cabinet’s photos if they are staying. If they are leaving, they are a second line, and this page still does not tell you a route for the shoes. Say what the bag is, and do not hide it inside the cabinet.

If the whole flat is being cleared, the apartment furniture guide is the inventory. The cabinet can be one line on that list. Keep the line in the words this note uses, so the hall cabinet is not lost inside “furniture”.

## One line in the booking

You can [add the cabinet to the junk](/services/junk-removal) once it is empty, named, and either standing free or marked as still fixed. That page is a single pickup or a mixed household load. It is not a request to unscrew a fixing you have left in place, to take a built-in cupboard out of the wall, or to repair the holes.

If other furniture is leaving on the same visit, the [furniture leaving the flat](/services/furniture-removal) is those pieces. Name the cabinet as its own line beside them. The phrase for the furniture page stays on that page. This note does not compete with it.

Before the visit, the cabinet should be empty and standing where it is, or moved a short step out from the wall if you have already released it and the hall allows that step. Do not lay it across the doorway. Do not stand it in the shared corridor to wait. Leave it inside.

Say, in one message:

- one shoe cabinet, and its shape: low cupboard, tall cupboard, or bench with a lid
- emptied, or which door could not be opened
- width, height and depth, or “not measured”
- the clear gap in the hall and the front door, or “not measured”
- doors and shelves off by hand, or left on
- any glass, crack or loose fragments
- standing free, released by hand, or still fixed
- on an inventory or not, and any written release
- anything else in the hall that is leaving, as its own line

Do not write “shoe cabinet” as the only words. Do not write “small item” because the hall is small. Do not ask for the cabinet to be cut down. The empty cabinet, the hall, and whether it is fixed are the report.
