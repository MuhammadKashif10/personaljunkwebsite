The television has already left. What remains is the long low cabinet it used to sit on. Name that cabinet, empty it, and write “TV already gone” on the same note. A booking that says “the TV unit” will be read as the cabinet and the screen together. This note is the cabinet that is left.

A unit that still has the screen on it, or that is fixed to the wall, a niche or the building, stays on the [media-wall guide](/blog/media-wall-unit-removal-dubai). Come back here when the screen is already gone and the open question is the sideboard. The screen itself, whether it works and which route it takes, stays on the [TV guide](/blog/tv-disposal-dubai). A cabinet that was built to come apart, and whose screws are the job, stays on the [dismantling guide](/blog/furniture-dismantling-dubai). A glazed display cabinet, with loose glass shelves as the risk, is the [glass-cabinet note](/blog/glass-display-cabinet-removal-dubai). A sideboard that leaves with a dining table, as one set, stays on the [dining-table guide](/blog/dining-table-removal-dubai). This page is the sideboard on its own, after the television has gone. A narrow table in a hallway, with no screen and no long run of doors, is the [console-table note](/blog/console-table-removal-dubai).

## Name the cabinet the screen left

Stand in front of the piece and say what you can see. A sideboard is long, low and standing on the floor. It may have doors, drawers, or both. It may have short legs, a plinth, or sit flat. Write the room. “Sideboard in the living room, TV already gone” is a name. “The unit” is not.

If the cabinet is still holding a television, you are early for this page. Take the screen off this note and follow the TV guide for the set. When the screen has left, come back and describe the cabinet that remains. Do not book both as one object because they used to live together.

Look at how the cabinet meets the wall. If it only touches the wall because it was pushed there, write “freestanding”. If you can see screws, battens or a silicone line fixing it to the wall, a niche or the building, write “fixed” and stop this note. A fixed cabinet is the media-wall question, or a question for the person who may release the fitting. Do not pull it off the wall to make this page fit. Do not unscrew a bracket you have not been told to release.

Measure the length, the height and the depth as it stands. The length is the figure that surprises people. A cabinet that looks ordinary in the room can be longer than the clear width of the door. Write the three figures, or write “not measured” if you have no tape. Do not invent the numbers. This page does not keep a standard door size.

If two cabinets stand side by side and were bought as a pair, count two. “Left cabinet” and “right cabinet” are names. “One sideboard” is wrong if you can see a join between two carcases. If they are screwed to each other and the screws will not turn with your fingers, write “two cabinets, still joined” and measure the pair as it stands. Do not split them with a tool you do not already use for ordinary furniture care.

A top that is stone, glass or a separate timber slab is part of the description. Write the material you can see. If the top lifts off when you try it with your hands, and it was only resting, you may lift it off, set it against a wall you can protect, and write “top off, was resting”. If it is fixed, leave it. Do not prise a glued top. A glass top that is loose is a glass line of its own: write “glass top, loose” and keep it with the cabinet in the note. Do not carry the glass across the flat to “get it out of the way” unless you already have a safe place for it in the same room.

Look at the feet. If one foot is missing, write “foot missing” and say which corner as you face the cabinet: front left, front right, back left, back right. If the cabinet rocks, write “rocks”. Do not pack a book under a corner and then describe the cabinet as level.

## Empty it before you call it empty

Open each door and each drawer. Take out what you are keeping. A remote, a cable, a game console, a stack of plates, a folder. If it is staying, it leaves the photograph. If it is leaving and it is not the cabinet, name it as its own line or take it out of the shot. This page does not require a washed cabinet. It requires an empty one, or an honest note that a drawer is still full.

A cable that belonged to the television is not the cabinet. If the screen has gone and a power lead, an HDMI lead or a wall bracket is still on the floor, name those parts. “Bracket still on the wall” is a fact about the wall, not a fact about the sideboard. Leave the bracket unless the person who may release it has said it goes. Do not unscrew a wall plug on this page. Coil a loose lead that is leaving, bag it, and write “lead from the old screen, bagged”. Do not hide the bag inside a drawer and then say the cabinet is empty.

Look behind the cabinet if you can move it a little without dragging a foot that is already loose. Dust, a dropped remote, a child’s toy. If you are keeping them, they leave the photograph. If the cabinet will not move because it is heavy or the floor would mark, write “not pulled out” and say you have not seen the back. Do not claim the back is clear if you have not looked.

A drawer that will not open is still a drawer. Write “drawer stuck” and say which one: top left, top right, middle, bottom. Do not hit it. Do not take the back off the cabinet to reach it. If you can see the contents through a gap, say what you can see. If you cannot, write “contents unseen”. A booking that says the cabinet is empty, when a drawer is shut and stuck, is the wrong booking.

Look inside for glass shelves, a loose divider, or a metal rack that lifts out. If a shelf lifts out by hand, take it out, set it with the cabinet, and write “shelf out”. If it is fixed, write “shelf fixed”. The general question of taking furniture apart is the dismantling guide. A sideboard that already stands as one cabinet is not that job. Do not take the back panel off to make the carcase lighter.

A mirror on a door, or a glass door, changes the carry. Write “mirror door” or “glass door” and say which door. Do not take the mirror off the door. If the glass is cracked, write “glass cracked” and say which panel. Do not tape over a crack and then describe the door as sound. A glazed display cabinet, where the glass shelves are the whole risk, is the glass-cabinet note. A sideboard with one glass door, and a solid carcase, can stay on this page if you have named the glass. If you are unsure which page fits, look at the object: a tall cabinet built to show objects behind glass is the display cabinet. A long low cabinet the television used to sit on is this page.

## Doors, hardware and what already comes off

Close each door and feel the hinge. If a hinge is loose, write “hinge loose” and say which door. If a door is already off, keep it with the cabinet and write “door off”. Do not lean the door on a sofa in another room and then forget it. A door that will not close is still part of the cabinet. Write “door will not close”.

Handles and knobs stay unless they are already in your hand. If a knob has come off, bag it and write “knob off, bagged” with the door it came from. Do not unscrew the other knobs to make the front flatter. A key, if the cabinet locks, stays with the cabinet or is named. Write “key with cabinet” or “key staying”. A locked door you cannot open is “locked, key missing” or “locked, key here”. Do not force the lock.

A cable hatch, a hole in the back, or a grommet where the television lead used to pass is part of the description. Write “hole in the back for a lead”. Do not cover it with a story about the old screen. The hole tells the person reading the note that this cabinet held a screen. It does not put the screen back on the booking.

If the back panel is loose, write “back loose”. Do not staple it, nail it, or peel it off. A loose back can catch on a door frame. Saying so is more useful than a repair you have not finished.

Legs that unscrew by hand, and that you can turn with your fingers, may come off if you already do that for ordinary care of this cabinet. Bag the legs with the cabinet and write “legs off, by hand”. If you need a spanner you do not already have, leave the legs on. Do not saw a leg to shorten the cabinet. The length and the height are the report. They are not a reason to cut the piece.

A plinth that is part of the carcase stays. If a clip-on plinth is already detached, keep it with the cabinet and write “plinth off”. Do not snap a plinth to make the cabinet sit lower.

Look along the top for felt pads, a glass mat, or rings from the television stand. If a mat is leaving, bag it or name it. If it is staying, it leaves the photograph. A mark on the top is part of the description if you can see it. Write what you see. Do not write a condition you have not looked at.

## The length against the doorway

Walk the path the cabinet has to take. Open each door as far as it opens. The clear width is the gap you can see, not the width of the door while it is shut. A rug in the opening is part of the width until you move the rug. Move what you are keeping, then look again.

The sideboard is long. The figure that matters is the length, and the height as it will travel. If the top is off and resting, measure the carcase and the top as two pieces and say so. If the top is on, measure the cabinet whole. A shorter chest in the same room does not prove this cabinet will pass.

Name the door that limits the cabinet. If a second door is tighter, name that one too. A cabinet that leaves the living room and stops at the front door has not left. If you have already been given a lift instruction, add it: which lift, and the note you were given. If you have no lift note, write “no lift instruction yet”. Do not invent a lift size. Do not write a building rule you have not been given.

If the cabinet has to turn in a corridor, say so. “Turn at the hall, cabinet is longer than the hall is wide” is a useful sentence if that is what you can see. Do not solve it by cutting the cabinet. If you cannot see a way through, write “route not clear” and name the pinch. The person quoting the job needs the pinch, not a guess that it will be fine.

The media-wall guide remains the unit when the screen, the brackets and a wall fixing are still the question. Use this page when the screen is gone and the sideboard is the piece that is leaving. A floor lamp, a picture or a trolley in another room is not this cabinet. Leave those on their own notes if they have them. Do not add them to the sideboard.

If other people use the corridor, do not leave the cabinet there overnight as a way to save the living room. The corridor is not the gap you photographed. Leave the cabinet in the room until the collection, empty, with the doors shut if they shut.

## What is not this cabinet

A television that is still in the flat is not this booking, even if it used to sit on this cabinet. If the screen is leaving too, it has its own line on the TV guide. If the screen has already gone to another address, write “TV already gone” and do not describe the screen as if it were still here. A wall bracket left behind is not the sideboard.

A media wall that is screwed into a niche, or that includes a bridge and tall side towers, is the media-wall guide. Do not call that run a sideboard because the screen has been lifted off one shelf. If the only piece left is one long low cabinet and it stands on the floor, you are on this page. If you can still see a wall fixing, you are not.

A glass display cabinet is not this sideboard. Tall glazed doors and loose glass shelves are that note. A bookshelf is not this page either. Open shelves, with no doors and no television history, are not this cabinet. Name it on its own line if it is leaving, or leave it. Do not retitle a bookcase as a sideboard to use this note.

A chest of drawers in a bedroom is not this cabinet. The bedroom list keeps a chest. A pair of tables beside a bed is the bedside note, not a short sideboard. A dining table that is leaving, with this cabinet as a matching piece in the same set, stays on the dining-table guide. Use this page when the table is staying, or when there is no table in the job, and the cabinet is the piece the screen left behind.

A cabinet you are moving to another address to keep using is not a collection. This note is for a sideboard that is leaving the property. If someone is taking it to a new home to keep, say that to the person who is moving it. Do not use a disposal booking as a way to relocate a cabinet you still want.

If a tenancy list or a written note says the cabinet stays with the property, leave it off the booking. A scratched top is not permission. The person who may release it writes that release. This page does not interpret a contract.

A fixed shelf, a plaster niche or a panel that is part of the wall is not a sideboard. Leave it. Do not book joinery as a freestanding cabinet because it looks like furniture from the doorway.

## One line in the booking

You can [take the sideboard](/services/furniture-removal) once the cabinet is named, the screen is marked as already gone, and the drawers are empty or the stuck one is written down. That page is the furniture leaving the room. It is not a request to release a wall fixing, and it is not a request to saw the cabinet down to the doorway.

If other household items are leaving in the same visit, and they are not this cabinet, say so as separate lines. Do not hide a television, a sofa or a bag of cables inside the sideboard count. Those lines are not this cabinet.

Say, in one message:

- sideboard, and the room
- freestanding, or fixed — and if it is fixed, you are on the wrong page
- length, height and depth, and the clear width of the doorway that limits it
- doors, drawers, a glass door or a mirror, if you can see them
- shelves out, or shelves fixed
- a stuck drawer, a missing foot, a loose hinge or a loose back, if you can see it
- TV already gone
- anything that is not this cabinet: a screen that is still here, a wall bracket, a dining table, a glass display cabinet

Do not write “the TV unit” as the only words. Do not write “TV already gone” if the screen is still on the cabinet. Do not ask for the cabinet to be cut because the length is larger than the gap. The figure is the report.

If the drawers are still full, you are early. Empty them, or name what is still inside. Send the cabinet you can stand in front of and check.

The media-wall page remains the unit under a screen, and a unit that is fixed. The TV page remains the screen. The dismantling page remains furniture that was built to come apart. The glass-cabinet page remains a glazed display cabinet. The dining-table page remains a sideboard that leaves with the table. This page remains the sideboard the television left behind.

## The morning the cabinet leaves

Look at the top. It should be clear. If someone has put the remote, a plant or a box back on it, take those off and correct the note. If a drawer you emptied has been filled again, empty it again or say what is now inside.

Look at the wall. If the screen is back on the cabinet, this note is no longer true. Stop and follow the TV guide for the set, or take the set off this booking and write the new sentence. If a bracket you had named has been removed by someone else, say so. Do not leave “bracket still on the wall” in a message that is no longer what you can see.

Open the door to the gap you measured. A box in the opening changes the width. Move it before you treat the old figure as current. If you have taken the top off since you measured, measure the carcase and the top again, or say that the earlier figure was the cabinet whole.

Point at the cabinet as the cabinet. Point at a television only if it has its own line. Point at a dining table, a glass display cabinet or a chest of drawers only if those have their own lines. Leave a bookshelf off this note.

Do not stand the cabinet in a shared corridor the night before. If the only place it can wait is the room it is already in, leave it there, clear of the doorway, empty, with the doors shut if they shut.

When it has gone, look at the floor where it stood. A shelf you meant to include is still a shelf if it is leaning on the wall. A cable you meant to keep is a problem only if it left inside a drawer. The useful record is the sentence from the first note: the sideboard that was leaving, the screen already gone, the length written beside the doorway’s clear width, and the drawers empty or the stuck one named.
