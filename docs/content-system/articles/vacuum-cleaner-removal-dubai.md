The item is one vacuum cleaner from a cupboard. It has a hose or a wand, it holds dust, and it may have a battery or a cord. Empty the dust or say you did not, decide which parts go with it, and keep the machine out of a rubbish sack. That is the job. A cupboard full of other gadgets is not.

This note is one vacuum. A list of unlike machines, each with its own disconnection, is the [old-appliance guide](/blog/old-appliance-removal-dubai). What a household load can include, including a vacuum that rides with other items, is the [acceptance list](/blog/what-we-take-dubai). What a household bin and a chute are for is the [bins guide](/blog/what-dubai-bins-wont-take). Where a small appliance can be taken is already set out in the [e-waste guide](/blog/e-waste-disposal-dubai). Come back here when the question is one vacuum that has been living in a cupboard, and whether it is about to be bagged with the kitchen rubbish.

## Make sure it is one vacuum

A vacuum, for this note, is a machine whose job is to pick up dust. It may be an upright, a cylinder with a hose, a stick, or a small robot that sits on a dock. Write which one. “Upright, with a hose on the back.” “Cylinder, hose and wand.” “Stick, one battery.” “Robot, with its dock.”

A fan is not a vacuum. A pedestal fan that will not fold is the [pedestal-fan note](/blog/pedestal-fan-removal-dubai). A desk fan is not that note. A ceiling fan is not confirmed on this page. If the object is a ceiling fan, say “ceiling fan” and stop. Do not describe a fan as a vacuum because both come out of a cupboard.

A carpet washer, a steam cleaner or a machine with a tank of water and detergent is not this vacuum. It holds liquid. Write what it is, and do not empty a chemical tank under these steps. This note does not tell you where detergent goes.

A dehumidifier is a different machine, with a water tank. The [dehumidifier note](/blog/dehumidifier-removal-dubai) is that tank. An office microwave is the [pantry-microwave note](/blog/office-pantry-microwave-dubai). Neither is a vacuum, even if it sits on the same shelf.

If you are not sure, write the name from the label and “not sure it is a vacuum”. A label you can read is better than a guess.

## Get it out of the cupboard

The cupboard is why this machine is awkward. It was pushed to the back, the hose is hooked on a shelf, and the door does not open fully because a broom is in the way.

Take out what is in front of it, if those things are yours to move. A broom, a bucket, a box of bags, a drying rack. Put them where they are not the vacuum. If one of them is also leaving, it is its own line. Do not call the contents of the cupboard “the vacuum”.

Pull the machine out only when the cord is unplugged, or when you can see that it has no cord to the wall. If the plug is behind it and you cannot reach the socket without dragging a machine that may still be switched on, do not drag it. Move what blocks your hand. If you still cannot see the plug, write “plug not seen” and stop pulling. Do not cut the cord to get the machine out.

If the hose or the wand is caught on a shelf, unhook it. Do not yank the machine against a hose that is looped over a bracket. If the machine is wedged and will not come forward, write “stuck in the cupboard” and photograph it there. Do not dismantle the cupboard. The cupboard stays with the flat unless it is a freestanding piece you have already decided is leaving, and that piece is not this note.

A robot that lives on a dock comes off the dock. Lift it if it lifts. If it is locked to the dock, write “on the dock, not released” and do not lever it off. The dock is part of this machine if it is leaving with it. Write “dock leaving” or “dock staying”.

## Unplug it, and decide the battery

Switch the machine off at its own switch, then at the wall if it is plugged in. Unplug the wall end first, then the machine end if the cord detaches. Coil the cord loosely. Use the machine’s own clip if it has one. Do not wrap the cord tightly around the handle, and do not tape it across a cracked casing.

Some vacuums have a cord that does not come off. That cord stays with the machine. Write “cord fixed, coiled”.

Some have a battery that slides off a release you already use. If it comes off by that release, you may take it off. Keep it with the machine, not loose in a drawer, and write “battery off, with vacuum”. Do not open the pack. Do not pierce it, bend it or tape over a swollen case to hide it. If the pack is swollen, cracked or hot, leave it on or beside the machine where it already is, and write “battery damaged, not opened”. This note does not give a route for a loose battery. The bins guide and the e-waste guide already mark that boundary. Do not put a loose battery in a sack.

If the battery does not come off by the release you already use, leave it in. Write “battery left in”. A robot’s battery is often inside. Leave it inside. Write “robot, battery not removed”.

A spare battery in the same cupboard is a second item. Write “spare battery” only to say it is there. Do not add it to this machine’s bag as if it were a tool. If you are not sending it, put it back where you keep it, out of the photos.

The charger, if there is one, is its own small lead. Decide whether it goes with the machine. Write “charger leaving” or “charger kept”. A charger you will use with a replacement is not spare because this machine is leaving.

## The dust

A vacuum leaves a mess when it is tipped, because the dust compartment is rarely empty. That is the part to deal with before the machine waits by the door.

Open the bin, the bag hatch or the cylinder the way you already empty it at home. If the dust is ordinary household dust, empty it into a bag you can tie, close the bag, and put that bag with ordinary household rubbish you are already disposing of in the way you usually do. Write “dust emptied”. Put the empty compartment back on the machine so the opening is closed.

If you do not know what the dust is, or it smells of something other than ordinary dust, do not empty it and do not sniff it to decide. Close the compartment and write “dust not emptied”. Leave the machine upright so the compartment stays shut.

Do not wash a filter, a hose or a motor to make the photo look cleaner. Water in a motor is a different problem from dust. If the compartment is already wet, write “compartment wet” and leave it closed.

A full bag that lifts out may come out. Tie it or tape it closed and keep it out of the machine if you have emptied the reason for the machine to hold it. If the bag tears, put the torn bag inside another bag and tie that. Write “bag removed” or “bag left in”.

Do not tip the machine to knock the dust out on the floor of the hall. You would then be sweeping the cupboard into the route.

## The hose, the wand and the tools

What makes one vacuum larger than the cupboard it came from is the hose, the wand and the floor head, once they are no longer clipped on.

Take off any part that comes off by the release you already use. A hose that unclips, a wand that pulls apart, a floor head that clicks off. Lay those parts beside the machine. Write what came off. “Hose off, wand in two pieces, floor head off.”

If a part does not release, leave it on. Write “hose left on”. Do not cut the hose to make it shorter. Do not saw the wand. A cut hose is not a smaller vacuum. It is a sharp edge and a machine that can no longer be described simply.

Small tools, the brush and the crevice piece, live in a clip or a lid. Take them out so they are not lost in the cupboard, and decide. If they are leaving, put them in one small bag and tape the bag to the machine, not across a crack. Write “tools with vacuum”. If you are keeping them for another machine, they stay out of the photos. Write “tools kept”.

A spare filter still in its packet is not the machine. If it is leaving, it is a small extra line. If it is staying, put it away.

Count the parts that are leaving with the machine, in one sentence. “Machine, hose, wand, floor head, one bag of tools.” A person reading that can see the volume. “Vacuum and bits” cannot.

Keep the parts with the machine. A hose left in the cupboard is a hose that is still in the flat after you think the job is done.

## Keep the machine out of a sack

A vacuum does not go in a black sack with kitchen rubbish. It does not go down a chute. It does not get left by the bin room to see who takes it. It is a machine with a motor, a hose and sometimes a battery. Bagging it hides the hose, the dust and the battery from the person who has to carry it.

The bins guide is where the question of a household container and a chute is already answered, including for small appliances. This note does not add a rule, and it does not repeat a list of drop-off points. Those details sit in that guide and in the e-waste guide. Read them if you are deciding between taking the machine yourself and having it collected. If you take it yourself, empty it and close it first, using the steps above, so it does not spill on the way.

If the machine is sitting in a room of bags from a clear-out or a refit, lift it out of that pile. If those bags are leaving as a project load, [leave the machine out of the project sacks](/services/waste-removal) and say in the message that the vacuum is a separate line. That page is the project load. The vacuum is not part of it because it was in the same room.

If someone wants the machine and it works, arrange that handover yourself. Keep it out of the leaving photos until they have collected it. A message that says “I might take it” is not a handover.

The business removes items. It does not buy them, sell them or say what a vacuum is worth.

## One machine, not the cupboard

The cupboard often holds more than the vacuum. A second, older vacuum. A handheld. A box of old bags and belts. A steam mop. A fan. An iron. A heap of cloths.

Decide what is leaving. If only one vacuum is leaving, the rest stays in the cupboard or is put away, not piled on the machine. If some of the rest is leaving, each thing is its own line. “One cylinder vacuum. One handheld. One iron.” Three lines.

A second vacuum is a second line, with its own dust and its own hose. Do not strap two machines together. Do not put a handheld inside the cylinder’s compartment.

A box of small kitchen gadgets, irons and hair dryers, leaving as part of a wider clear-out, belongs on the acceptance list as that wider load. This note stays the one vacuum from the cupboard. You can still use these steps on each vacuum in a larger load, one at a time, so each one is empty and named. The list of what else the load may include stays on the acceptance page.

If several unlike appliances are on the same booking, a fridge, a washer and this vacuum, the old-appliance guide is the register. Keep this vacuum off that register’s “white goods” line. It is still one line of its own, prepared here.

Do not put tools, batteries, detergent or general rubbish inside the dust compartment to save space. Close the compartment empty, or leave it closed and marked if you did not empty it.

## Photos of the machine, not the room

![A sofa, stacked boxes and an appliance beside an open doorway in an apartment living room.](media:what-we-take-cover)

The picture is a sofa, stacked boxes and an appliance beside an open doorway. It does not show a vacuum cleaner, a hose, a dust compartment, a battery or a cupboard. Use it only as a reminder that the machine should stand with its parts beside it, not buried in a sack. Your own photos still have to show this vacuum.

Send these views.

- The whole machine, out of the cupboard, standing.
- The dust compartment, closed, and a note that it was emptied or was not.
- The hose, wand, floor head and tools that are leaving, laid beside it.
- The cord, coiled, or the battery, on the machine or beside it, not opened.
- Any crack, split hose or damaged battery, in the light you have.
- The cupboard only if the machine is still stuck and you need to show that.

Add the room and the floor. Add “dust emptied” or “dust not emptied”. Add a lift or loading note if you have been given one. If you have not, write “no lift instruction yet”. Do not invent a lift size, and do not write a building rule you have not been given.

Weight is not something to guess. A cylinder with a full compartment is heavier than an empty stick. Write “still heavy” if that is what you found when you lifted it. Do not write a number you did not weigh. If you have no tape, write “not measured”. The useful size is the machine with the hose and the wand as they will travel, not the size of the cupboard.

## One line in the booking

You can book [a small rubbish pickup](/services/junk-removal) once the machine is out of the cupboard, the dust is dealt with or marked, and the parts are counted. That page is a single pickup or a mixed household load. It is not a request to cut a cord, open a battery, or sort a cupboard you have not emptied.

If the vacuum is one of several machines, say so, and use the old-appliance guide for the others. The vacuum remains its own line.

Say, in one message:

- one vacuum, and its kind: upright, cylinder, stick or robot
- out of the cupboard, or “stuck in the cupboard”
- unplugged, cord coiled or “plug not seen”
- battery off and with the machine, left in, or “battery damaged, not opened”
- dust emptied, or “dust not emptied”
- the parts leaving with it: hose, wand, floor head, tools, dock, charger
- any crack, split hose or wet compartment
- anything else from the cupboard that is leaving, as its own line

Do not write “small appliance” as the only words. Do not write “it can go in a bag”. Do not put the machine in a sack because the sacks are already going. One vacuum is one line.
