The item is one freestanding noticeboard. Take the pins, the clips and the paper off, or name what is still on it. Say whether the feet already fold, and leave any screw in the wall where it is. That is the job. A floor of desks is not. A school closure window is not. A magazine file is not. A picture in a frame is not.

This note is the board. Desks, task chairs, cabinets and loose partitions on a workplace floor are the [office furniture guide](/blog/office-furniture-removal-dubai). A school office during a fixed closure, with assets, records and access still to freeze, is the [school-office clearance guide](/blog/school-office-clearance-dubai). Come back here when the question is one board that already stands on its own feet or on a wheeled stand, what is still pinned to it, and whether it leaves whole.

## Make sure it is a freestanding noticeboard

A noticeboard, for this note, is a board made to hold notices, and it already stands without the wall holding it up. It may be cork, fabric, a magnetic sheet or a plain board with a clip rail. It may sit on two feet, on a tripod, or on a wheeled stand. It may be wood, metal or a light frame with a pin surface. Write the shape you can see, and how many. “One cork board, two feet, reception, pins still in.” “One wheeled stand, magnetic face, corridor, paper still clipped.” “One fabric board, folded flat, empty.”

A floor of desks is not a noticeboard. The office furniture guide is that inventory: what stays in use, what is released, and how each group leaves. A board standing beside a desk is this page when the board is what is leaving. Do not call the desk a noticeboard because a notice was pinned near it. Do not call the board a desk because you want one note for the room.

A school closure is not this board. The school-office guide is the authority, the records, the IT and the window when pupils are off the route. A single board a school has already released, standing free in an office that is in scope, can still use these steps. Do not paste a safeguarding checklist onto one cork rectangle, and do not treat a classroom wall as this page because a notice was on it.

A magazine file is not a noticeboard. A holder that keeps magazines or loose sheets upright is the [magazine-file note](/blog/magazine-file-holder-dubai). Paper leaning in a box is that note. Paper pinned to a face is this one. Do not tip the magazines out and call the empty box a board. Do not unpin a board and call the pins a file.

A framed picture is not a noticeboard. One rigid frame, with the glazing still in it, is the [framed-picture note](/blog/oversized-framed-picture-removal-dubai). A print behind glass is that page. A cork face you can push a pin into is this page. Do not take the glass out of a frame to make it look like a board, and do not book a picture by calling it a notice.

A glass partition, a fabric screen on a floor track, or a panel clamped between desks is not a noticeboard. A partition is not given a URL here. If you can see a track, a foot bolted to the slab, or a clamp that joins two screens, write “screen, not a board” and stop. Do not follow the steps below as if a glazed run were a cork face.

A filing cabinet is not a noticeboard. A cabinet has drawers and a carcass. It is not given a URL here. It stays with the office furniture guide, which already treats a cabinet as its own line. A notice taped to a drawer does not turn the cabinet into this page. Leave the cabinet on its own line.

A bookshelf is not a noticeboard. A shelf has shelves and uprights, and it is not given a URL here. If the object is a bookcase, name it as a bookcase and keep it off these steps. Books can leave as their own line if you have already decided they are going. They are not renamed as notices.

A mannequin is not a noticeboard. It is not given a URL here. If one is in the room, name it as a mannequin and keep it out of the photograph of the board.

A whiteboard that is screwed to the wall, or a pinboard on a picture rail, is a fitting. Write “fixed, staying” and do not unscrew the wall. This note starts when the board already stands on feet or on a stand, or when it lifts off a hook by the movement it was built with. If it will not lift off, leave it and write “will not lift off”. The slot for a board that is still screwed to the wall is to skip it.

A flipchart pad on an easel is not this board unless the object you are booking is the freestanding notice surface, not the pad. If the thing leaving is a pad of paper and the easel is staying, write “pad only” and do not book the easel as a noticeboard. If both are leaving and the easel is a wheeled notice stand, they are still two lines until you have looked at each one. Do not fold the pad into the frame and call that one object.

If you are not sure, write the shape. “Cork face, silver frame, two splayed feet, pins in the corners” is enough. A wrong name is worse than a plain description.

## Take the pins and the paper off

Set the board where you can see the face, if it already stands or already lifts. If it does not stand, leave it where it is and work from the side you can reach. Do not lay it face down on a desk to speed the clear, unless you have already checked the desk is staying and you can lift the board back up.

Pins come out. Drawing pins, map pins and the short nails some cork still holds. Put them in one small tin or one envelope that stays with you, not in a pocket of a jacket that is also leaving. A pin left in the cork is a pin in a hand later. Write “pins out” when you have looked at the face, the edges and the back. If a pin will not come, write “pin stuck” and do not dig it out with a knife.

Clips and magnets come off. A clip that already slides off the rail can come off. Keep the clips with the board if they belong to it, in a bag you can point at, and write “clips bagged with board”. If a clip is riveted, leave it and write “clip fixed”. Magnets come off the face. A magnet you are keeping stays with you. Do not leave a handful of magnets on the floor.

Paper comes off. Notices, timetables, photos, children’s drawings, name lists and a sheet with a phone number. This page does not decide which paper a school or an office must keep. If the sheet has a name, an account, a pupil or a staff list, it is a records decision. Put it with the person who already owns that decision, off the photograph. If you have not decided, put it in one open pile that is not the leaving board, and write “paper, not decided”. A board that still holds a term’s notices is not an empty board.

Do not pin the paper back on to make the photo look tidy. Do not bag the notices and then pin the bag to the cork and call that empty. The bag is a second object. The board is the face.

Look at the back, not only at the front. A spare pin, a key, a lanyard and a folded letter hide behind the frame or under a foot. Those come out and stay with you. Anything you still need does not ride on a board that is leaving.

Already-shredded paper in its own sacks is not a noticeboard. That paper is the [shredded-paper note](/blog/shredded-paper-out-of-furniture-load-dubai). Do not tip a sack onto the cork, and do not call the board a sack because both can hold paper. If shreds are stuck in the pin holes, write “shreds stuck” and do not wash them out.

Do not hose the board. Water on a cork face has to go somewhere, and a wet board is heavier and can stain the floor. If it smells, say so in plain words: “empty, smells, not washed”. Do not spray a chemical into the fabric and then stand it back in a corridor.

A label with a name, a room number or an asset sticker comes off if it already peels. If it does not peel, leave it and write “label still on”. Do not scrape a wall to remove a sticker that is on the wall rather than on the board. The wall’s surface is not this job.

If you cannot see the back because the board is too wide to turn in the space it stands in, do not cut a window in the face. Write “back not seen” and what you believe is still on it. That board stays out of any sentence that says it is empty.

## A board that will not stand, or that is still fixed

What makes one board a problem is how it stands, and whether anything still holds it to a wall, a desk or the floor.

If it already stands on its own feet, write “stands free”. If it falls when you let go, do not prop it with a chair and call that standing. Write “will not stand” and leave it on its edge, or flat, in the shape it will leave in. Do not glue a foot back on as a preparation step. A foot that has already come off goes in a bag with that board. Write “foot off, bagged with board”.

A foot or a tray that already folds by the hinge it has can be folded. Write “feet folded”. If the hinge will not move, leave it open and write “feet will not fold”. Do not stand on the hinge, and do not saw a leg off to make the board shorter.

Wheels that already turn can stay on. Write “wheels on”. A brake that already clicks can be set so the stand does not roll while you photograph it. If a wheel has come off, bag it with the board. Do not fit a different wheel from another stand so the photo looks complete.

A back strut that already unscrews by hand can come off if that is how the stand was built to travel. Keep the strut with the board. If it will not turn, leave it and write “strut stuck”. Do not lever it with a screwdriver you are using as a chisel. The [dismantling guide](/blog/furniture-dismantling-dubai) is furniture that was built to come apart. Stay on this page when the only question is whether this one board’s feet already fold. Do not take the face out of the frame to make a carry easier.

A wooden frame with a split corner gets the words “corner split”. Do not pull the split piece off so the photo looks clean, unless it has already come away in your hand. A piece that has already come off goes in the bag with the board.

Screws into a wall are a fixing. If you can see screw heads, plugs or a rail, do not unscrew them. Write “screwed to the wall” and leave the board where it is. This note is the board only when the board is already loose. A board that is still screwed to the wall is skipped. Do not drill the plaster out, and do not cut the rail.

A hook the board already lifts off is not a screw. Lift it off, write “lifted off the hook”, and leave the hook in the wall. The hook is not the board.

A clamp under a desktop, if it already releases by the knob it has, can be released. Write “clamp off”. If the knob will not turn, leave it and write “clamp stuck”. Do not saw the clamp, and do not cut the desk edge to free it. The desk is its own line if the desk is leaving. The [standing-desk note](/blog/home-standing-desk-removal-dubai) is one desk in a home. A workplace desk stays on the [office desk guide](/blog/office-desk-removal-dubai). Do not unscrew a board into a desk to make a single object.

If you have already forced a panel and the board is split, stop. Write “split” and do not press it back together. The photo has to show the shape the board actually has.

Count after you have looked, not before. “One board” is a count when you can point at one. “The notices” is not a count. A second board in the same room is a second line, with its own pin check. Do not add them in your head and write one number you did not check. If you move them together, count again after you move them.

## Where the board is standing

The board is awkward because of where it was left, as well as because of what is pinned to it.

In a reception, in front of a counter that is staying, move the board only if it already rolls or already lifts. If a cable runs behind the frame, follow the cable to both ends before you pull. A lead you are keeping stays with the machine it serves. Write “cable behind the board” if you cannot free it without unplugging something you have not identified. Do not cut the lead to get the board out of the corner.

Behind a desk, do not kick it out. If it slides by hand, slide it and write “slid out”. If the desk has to move first and the desk is staying, do not drag the desk off its feet to reach a board. Write “behind the desk, not pulled out” and photograph it where it is.

In a corridor that staff still use, do not leave the board there the night before to “get it out of the office”. The place you photograph is the place it leaves from. A board you have not cleared does not belong on a landing or in a lift lobby.

In a classroom or a staff room, the school-office guide still owns the closure. If that room is outside the written scope, leave the board and write “room not in scope”. If the room is in scope and the board already stands free, these steps still apply to that one board. Do not take a board down from a teaching wall because the office next door is being cleared.

On a high shelf, or leaning on a cupboard, do not drag it down by one pin. Lift what you can hold, or leave it and write “on the high shelf, not brought down”. A board that falls and splits is a different shape from the one you were going to measure.

Beside a printer, a monitor or a box of leads, those machines are their own lines if they are leaving. Do not hang the board on a printer and call the pair one item. Do not bag a screen behind the cork. This page does not unplug a screen to make a board look clearer, unless the plug is in the way of a photograph of the board and you already know that plug.

A board used as a lid on a box of mixed waste is still a noticeboard only if that is the object. The box is a second line. Do not book a board over mixed waste as an empty face. If you do not know what is under it, write “under the board, not identified” and do not tip the box into another container.

In a home, a board in a study or a child’s room follows the same pin check. It is not a reason to open a whole-flat inventory. If the only thing leaving from that room is the board, say so. If a desk, a holder and a picture are also leaving, each one is its own line.

## One board, not the office

The office furniture guide already says what a workplace floor includes. Stay on that guide for the desks, the chairs and the cabinets. Stay on this page for one board. Do not send two descriptions that disagree about whether the board was still screwed to the wall.

The school-office guide already says what a closure window includes. A board the school has not released stays there. This page does not decide a safeguarding question, a records retention rule, or who may enter a room. If the board is sitting inside a pile the school has already listed, take it out of that pile’s description and say it is its own line.

A whole office, with the dock, the hours and the desks, is [the office the board is leaving from](/services/office-clearance) when the floor is the booking. That page is the office. It is not a request to unscrew one cork face, and it is not a new name for a single board in a home.

You can [take the board with the furniture](/services/furniture-removal) when the board is the thing leaving and the desks are staying, or when both leave and you have named them apart. That page is the furniture leaving the room. It is not a request to clear a school, empty a filing cabinet, or release a glazed partition.

A wider household load that happens to include the board, once the pins are out or honestly marked, can still use these steps. The mixed pickup is [the other household items leaving with it](/services/junk-removal). That page is one visit for the things you have already decided are going. It is not a second name for the office floor.

The notices, the photos and the name lists stay off this note once they are off the face. A board you are giving to someone who still wants it is not a collection. Arrange that handover yourself. Keep it out of the leaving photos until they have collected it. A message that says “someone might want the notices” is not a handover.

The business removes items. It does not buy them, sell them or say what a board is worth.

A ceiling fan, a wall fan and a light over the board are not this page. Do not book a fan by calling it a noticeboard.

## Measure the board as it will travel

Measure after you have cleared the face, or after you have written why you could not, and not with a guess.

Height, width and depth, including a foot, a wheel or a tray that sticks out. If the feet are folded, measure the board in that folded shape, and say the feet are folded. If the feet will not fold, include them. If the board will not stand, measure it in the position it will leave in, and say “measured on its edge”.

Then look at the opening it has to pass. The useful figures are the clear height and clear width of the narrowest door, with the door as open as it goes. A board on a wheeled stand still has to pass that door if it is coming out. A desk, a chair or a cable can change the gap. Move what you are keeping, then measure if the gap changed.

If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. This note does not keep a standard size for a noticeboard or for an office door.

Write one sentence you measured. “One board, feet wider than the gap beside the desk” or “stands free, not yet compared with the door” or “not measured”. Do not write “it will fit” if you have not compared the figures. Do not write “it will not fit” from memory of a different door.

Weight is not something to guess. A board you cannot lift because it is still on a tall stand is not a weight. If the empty board is awkward because of the shape, write “awkward to carry”. Do not write a number you did not weigh.

The wider method for one object and the narrowest point on the way out stays with the [bulky-item note](/blog/bulky-item-pickup-dubai) if the corridor and the lift are the question. Stay on this page for the pins, the fixing and the count. Do not send two descriptions that disagree about whether the face was clear.

## One line in the booking

Say, in one message:

- one freestanding noticeboard, the face, and where it stands
- pins and paper off, or what is still on it and why you stopped
- stands free, will not stand, feet folded, or screwed to the wall
- the height and the widths you measured, or “not measured”
- anything else leaving from the same room, as its own line, including a desk if the desk is going
- that it is not a filing cabinet, not a partition, and not a framed picture

Do not write “the office” as the only words if the room also holds desks, a monitor and paper you have not decided about. Do not write “it is empty” if you have not seen the face and the back. Do not ask for a foot to be sawn off because the figure is larger than the gap. The figure is the report. A cut is not a step on this page.

If you have not cleared the board, you are early. Take the pins out if they already lift. If a foot or a clamp will not move, that stuck part is the honest line. Send that. If the board is still screwed to the wall, you are on the wrong note. Leave it, and write “screwed to the wall”.

On the morning, put the board where the photos put it. Check a notice has not gone back on overnight. Check a foot you removed is still with the board. A timetable pinned back on is not a clear face. Take it off before you treat the old note as current.

Point at the face as the board. Point at a desk, a cabinet or a pile of notices only if that object is its own line. The useful record is the sentence from the first check: one freestanding noticeboard, pins and paper off or the reason written, feet folded or the reason written, wall left alone, kept as its own line, written in plain words.
