The item is one console table in a hallway. Empty the top and any drawer, measure the table where it stands, and keep that table as its own line. That is the job. A coffee table is not. A sideboard is not. A unit built around a television is not. A shoe cabinet is not. A coat stand is not.

This note is the narrow table against the hall wall. A coat stand with hooks, standing in the same entry, is the [coat-stand note](/blog/coat-stand-removal-dubai). A cabinet that holds shoes in that hall is the [shoe-cabinet note](/blog/shoe-cabinet-removal-dubai). A long low cabinet in a living room, with the television already gone, is the [sideboard note](/blog/sideboard-after-tv-removed-dubai). A unit that still carries a screen, or that is fixed into a niche, is the [media-wall guide](/blog/media-wall-unit-removal-dubai). Come back here when the question is one narrow table in a hall, what is on it, and whether it will pass the hall it stands in.

## Make sure it is a console table

A console table, for this note, is a narrow table that stands against a wall in a hall or an entry. It is deeper than a picture rail and shallower than a sideboard. People put keys, post, a lamp or a bowl on it. It may have one drawer. It may have two. It may have an open shelf underneath. It may be a simple top on four legs, with nothing under it but air. Write the shape you can see. “Narrow table, one drawer, against the hall wall.” “Open shelf below, no drawer, beside the front door.” “Two drawers, short legs, under a mirror.”

The top is part of the table. The drawer, if it belongs to this table, is part of the table. A lamp that only sits on the top is not part of the table. A mirror hung on the wall above the table is not part of the table. Write “lamp on top” or “mirror above, on the wall” so those objects stay their own lines.

A coffee table is not this table. A coffee table sits away from the wall, in front of a seat, and is usually lower and deeper. A coffee table is not given a URL here. Do not call a hall table a coffee table because both have a top. Do not move a coffee table into the hall and follow these steps. If the only table leaving is the one in front of the sofa, name it as a coffee table and stop. This note starts when the table is the narrow one against the hall wall.

A sideboard is not this table. A sideboard is long and low, and it usually stands in a living room or a dining room, not in the tight width of a hall. The sideboard note is that cabinet, and it starts after the television has already gone. Do not shorten a sideboard in the description and call it a console. If you can stand beside it and the piece is still longer than the hall is wide, you are probably looking at a sideboard, or at a piece that will not turn. Write the length. Let the length decide the name.

A media wall, a TV stand and a low unit that exists to hold a screen are not this table. The media-wall guide is that unit, including a simple console that sits under a television. If the screen is still on the piece, or the piece is in the living room because of the screen, stay on that guide. A hallway table with no screen, and no bracket for a screen, is this note. Do not send a photo of a television and call the table under it a hall table.

A shoe cabinet is not this table. A shoe cabinet has doors, a lid or shelves, and its job is to hold shoes. The shoe-cabinet note is that piece. A console table may have a pair of shoes tucked under it. The shoes are not a cabinet, and the table is not a cabinet because the shoes are there. Take the shoes out of the photograph of the table, or name them as shoes. Do not book the table as a shoe cabinet to save a second line.

A coat stand is not this table. A stand has hooks and no top for keys, or a top so small it only stops the pole. The coat-stand note is the hooks and the front door. If both the stand and the table are leaving, they are two lines. Do not lean the stand on the table and call them one hall piece.

A dining table is a dining table, even if it has been pushed against a wall while a room is being painted. The [dining-table guide](/blog/dining-table-removal-dubai) keeps it. A pair of bedside tables is the [bedside-table note](/blog/bedside-tables-only-dubai). Those tables belong beside a bed. Do not rename them as a console because one of them has been carried into the hall already.

A bench you sit on, with no top that holds keys, is a bench. A bench is not given a URL here. Name it as a bench if it is leaving. A shelf that is screwed to the wall as part of the flat, with no legs and no back of its own, is not this table. Write “fixed shelf, staying” if it belongs to the building. Do not unscrew it to make a table out of a wall.

A glazed display cabinet, even a narrow one in a hall, is the [glass display-cabinet note](/blog/glass-display-cabinet-removal-dubai). Glass shelves and a glazed door take it off this page. A table with a glass top and no glazed door can stay here if it is still the narrow hall table. Write “glass top” and do not treat the glass as a second item unless it already lifts off.

If you are not sure, write the shape and the room. “Narrow wood top, one drawer, hall, no screen” is enough. “Long cabinet, living room, TV already gone” is a sideboard, and you should leave this note. A wrong name is worse than a plain description.

## Empty the top and the drawer

The table is heavier, and more important, because of what people leave on it. Keys, a building access card, post, a wallet, a spare remote, a bowl of coins, a lamp, a charger, a small box of medicines. All of that comes off before the table is described as ready.

Lift everything off the top. Put what you are keeping on a chair or a shelf that is not in the photograph. Check under a tray and inside a bowl. People drop a key under a stack of letters. Write “top cleared” when you have looked, not when the top looks tidy from the door.

Open the drawer if it opens. Take out papers, cables, a passport, a set of keys, a pen pot. If a paper matters, it stays with you. Write “drawer emptied”. If the drawer will not open, write “drawer stuck, not emptied”. Do not force it with a screwdriver to finish the sentence. A stuck drawer is a fact. It is not a failure of the note.

If there are two drawers, open both. Write “one drawer stuck” if only one moves. Do not assume the stuck one is empty because the other one was.

Look on the lower shelf, if there is one. Shoes, a bag, a folded umbrella, a box of cables. Take them out. A lower shelf that still holds a bag is not an empty table. Write “shelf cleared” or “no shelf”.

Do not fill the empty drawer with other rubbish so the table carries it out. Hidden contents fall on the stairs, and they change what the load is. Do not put the lamp inside the drawer and call the lamp part of the table. If the lamp is leaving, it is its own line. A floor lamp that is taller than the doorway is the [floor-lamp note](/blog/floor-lamp-taller-than-doorway-dubai). A small table lamp is not given a URL here. Name it as a lamp, and say whether it is staying or leaving.

Do not wash the table as a preparation step. Water on a veneer top, or on a drawer that has swelled, makes the piece worse to carry. If it is dusty, say “dusty, not washed”. If a ring from a cup has marked the top, leave the mark. The mark is not the job.

A mirror on the wall above the table stays on the wall unless you have already decided the mirror is leaving, and you can lift it off a hook by hand. A mirror is not given a URL here. Do not unscrew a mirror from the wall to make the table photo cleaner. Write “mirror above, staying” or “mirror lifts off the hook, set aside”. If the mirror is cracked, write “mirror cracked” and do not lift it.

If the table belongs to someone else in the flat, ask before you empty it. Their post and their keys are not yours to move into a bag. Empty only what you may touch. If a set of keys on the table is not yours, write “keys not mine, still on table” and leave them. The table is not ready until those keys are off.

## The hall is the problem

The table was put in the hall because the hall had a wall and nowhere else did. Many entries are a short corridor. The front door is at one end. A shoe cabinet, a coat stand or a cupboard door is along one side. The table fills the depth that remains.

The useful fact is not a catalogue depth. It is the clear gap between the front of this table and the opposite wall, or the opposite door. If that gap is smaller than the depth of the table, the table cannot be turned in the hall. Write “depth greater than the gap beside it”, “turns in the hall”, or “not tried”. Do not tip the table onto its top to test the turn. A tipped table blocks the only door, and a glass top meets the floor.

The front door is the second limit. Open it as far as it opens. A closer arm, a chain or a viewer can sit in the opening. Write what you can see. “Door closer above the frame.” “Chain on the lock side.” If you have not been given a lift size or a loading rule, write “no lift instruction yet”. Do not write a building rule you have not been given.

A table that stands behind the door, so the door only opens part way, has been living in that reduced opening. Write “door opens onto the table”. Do not take the door off its hinges to prove a point. If the table has to move a short step along the wall before the door will open fully, and you can do that without the legs catching, you may move it that step and write “moved one step along the wall”. If a leg catches the skirting, stop and write “not moved”.

Do not stand the table in the shared corridor so it is ready. A table in a corridor is in the way. Keep it inside until the collection is the next thing that happens. The [bulky-item note](/blog/bulky-item-pickup-dubai) is the photo and the route for one awkward object. Use it for the way out. Stay on this page for the drawer, the hall and the fact that this is a narrow table and not a sideboard.

If the route is stairs with no lift, say so. The [walk-up stairs guide](/blog/walk-up-stairs-no-lift-dubai) is the flights. A light-looking table can still be an awkward width on a landing. Do not start the carry yourself to prove it fits.

A shoe cabinet beside the table is the shoe-cabinet note if that cabinet is leaving. A coat stand beside the table is the coat-stand note if the stand is leaving. Name them. A photo of the hall with three pieces and the words “console table” describes a hall, not a table.

## Measure the table where it stands

Measure before you drag the table into the doorway to see if it fits.

- Length, along the wall.
- Depth, from the wall to the front edge, including a drawer pull if the pull sticks out.
- Height, from the floor to the top. If a lamp is still on it, take the lamp off first. Do not add the lamp to the height.

If a leg splays wider than the top, the widest point is the legs. Measure that width and say “legs wider than the top”. If the lower shelf sticks out past the legs, measure to the shelf.

Then look at the hall. The clear gap beside the table. The clear width and height of the front door when it is open as far as it goes. If another door comes first, such as a glass door between the entry and the living room, measure that one too. Name the tighter opening.

If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. A guess written as a measurement will be trusted. This note does not keep a standard door size, and it does not keep a standard size for this kind of table.

A table that is longer than the clear door width has to be turned, or it does not leave in that orientation. Write “longer than the door, has to turn” only if you have compared the two figures. If you have not compared them, write the two figures, or write “not compared”.

Check the ceiling only if the table is tall enough that a turn would lift the top toward it. Some entries have a lowered ceiling or a bulkhead. Write “low ceiling in the entry” if the top of the table, once tilted, would meet it. Do not tilt the table to test that sentence.

## What already comes off, and what stays on

Some of these tables were built in parts. A leg may bolt on. A drawer may lift out once it is open past a stop. A lower shelf may sit on pegs. This is the same kind of fact the [dismantling guide](/blog/furniture-dismantling-dubai) decides on a larger piece. On a hall table it is often simpler, and it is often unnecessary.

If a drawer already lifts out by the way you empty it, you may lift it out. Write “drawer out, by hand”. Keep the drawer with the table. Stand it on its base, or lay it inside the frame if it fits without force. Do not leave the drawer in another room.

If a leg unscrews by hand, and you already do that when you move the table, you may unscrew it. Write “legs off, by hand”. Bag the bolts and tape the bag to the frame, or put the bag in the drawer and keep the drawer with the frame. If a thread is stiff, stop. Write “legs do not unscrew”. Do not use a wrench you do not normally use on this table. A stripped thread or a split leg is a worse table than a whole one.

Do not saw a leg off to shorten the table. Do not snap a stretcher. Do not remove the top from the frame with a knife. If the table will only leave whole, that is the report. The dismantling guide is for furniture whose screws are the work. A hall table whose drawer already lifts out is not that job.

A glass top that already lifts off, and that you already lift off to clean under it, may come off. Lean it against a wall, glass facing the wall, away from the route, and write “glass top off, leaning”. If the glass is fixed, leave it. Write “glass fixed”. If it is cracked, write “glass cracked, left on” and do not lift it.

Keep every small part with the table. Bolts, pegs, a loose pull, a felt pad from a foot. One bag. Write “parts bagged, with table”. A table that arrives as a top, three legs and a missing bolt has become a different job from the one you described.

If the only joint you can see is a glued corner, leave it. Write “corners glued, left whole”. Do not soak a joint, and do not hit it with a hammer to see if it was meant to come apart.

## Is it fixed to the wall?

Look at the back, as far as you can see it without dragging the table out. Some hall tables have a bracket, a strap or a screw that ties them to the wall so they do not tip. This is common where children use the hall. It may have been added after the table was bought.

If you can see a strap and it unclips or unties by hand, you may release it. Write “strap off, by hand”. If you can see a screw and you do not own the wall, leave the screw. Write “screwed to the wall, left”. Do not pull the table forward to break a bracket you have not looked at. A bracket that tears out of plaster is a repair, and this note does not include a repair.

If you cannot see the back because the table is tight to the wall, write “back not seen”. Do not assume it is free. A table that has stood in one place for years may be held by one screw and still look loose from the front.

If the table is on a landlord’s inventory, leave it off the booking until the release is in writing. A collection is not permission to take a piece you do not own. Write “on inventory, waiting” or “released in writing”. This note does not interpret a tenancy. It asks you to say which of those two sentences is true.

A table that was built into a recess, with the wall as its back, is not this note. Write “built into the wall, staying”. The shoe-cabinet note makes the same distinction for a hall cupboard that is part of the flat. Do not take the doors off a recess and call the result a console table.

## One table, not the hall

A console table that has taken the only free wall in an entry is one object. A whole flat of furniture is the [apartment furniture guide](/blog/apartment-furniture-removal-dubai). Stay on that guide for the list of rooms. Come back here for the table. Do not book “the hall” because the table is the thing in the way of the door.

If the coat stand, the shoe cabinet and the table are all leaving, list them as three lines. The coat-stand note and the shoe-cabinet note keep those pieces. This page keeps the table. A request that says “the entry” will be read as everything in it. Say the table.

If the only other things leaving are ordinary household items, they can travel in the same visit once you have named the table. Ask for [furniture removal for a single piece](/services/furniture-removal) when the table is the piece, with or without other furniture you have already named. Do not use that sentence to mean a sofa booking, a whole-home clear-out, or a coffee table. Those stay on their own pages. The furniture page keeps the transaction. This note does not take that page’s head wording as its own target.

A full room, every cupboard, and the balcony as well, is a clearance. This page does not become that clearance because a hall table is in the pile. Name the room on the clearance page you are actually using, and name the table here so it is not lost inside the word “contents”.

Do not promise the table will be turned on the vehicle. You do not know the load. You know the table as it stands in the hall. The figures, or the honest gap, are the report.

Take the photos the request needs.

Do not send a close-up of a lamp and nothing else. Do not send a photo of the living-room sideboard and write “and the hall table” with no view of the hall. A table behind an open door is invisible. Move the camera until the table is the object in the frame.

If the glass is cracked, include the crack. A photo of a tidy top, when a cracked glass top is facing the wall, describes half the job.

If you have two tables, photograph them apart, then say the count. A pair of bedside tables is not this page. Two hall tables, if both are truly hall tables, are two lines.

## One line in the booking

Say, in one message:

- one console table, the shape, and that it is in the hall
- top cleared, drawer emptied, drawer stuck, or no drawer
- length, depth and height, or “not measured”
- the gap beside it and the front door, or “not measured”
- drawer out or legs off by hand, or left whole
- glass top off, glass fixed, or no glass
- standing free, strap off by hand, or still fixed
- that it is not a coffee table, not a sideboard, and not a shoe cabinet
- anything else leaving from the same hall, as its own line

Ask for [furniture removal for a single piece](/services/furniture-removal) when the table is the piece you want taken. The coat-stand note and the shoe-cabinet note stay the other two pieces in that entry. The sideboard note stays the long cabinet after the screen has gone. The media-wall guide stays the unit that still belongs to a screen.

Do not write “the hall” as the only words. Do not write “it is small” if you have not measured it. Do not ask for a leg to be sawn off because a door would then close. A cut is not a step on this page.

If you have not opened the drawer, you are early. Open it if it already moves. If it will not move, that stuck drawer is the honest line. Send that. If the object is a coffee table, a sideboard, or a unit under a television, you are on the wrong note. Name the piece you actually have and leave these steps.

On the morning, look at the top again. A set of keys put back overnight is still part of the line. Clear them before you treat yesterday’s photo as current. Point at the table as the table. Point at a shoe cabinet only if the cabinet is its own line.

The useful record is one hallway console table, top and drawer emptied or the reason written, measured where it stands, kept as its own line, written in plain words.
