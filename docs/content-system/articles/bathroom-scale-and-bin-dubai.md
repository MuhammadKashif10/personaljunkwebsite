The items are a bathroom scale and a small bin, both standing free. Empty the bin, leave the scale’s case closed, and keep the two as two lines. That is the job. A toilet is not. A sink is not. A handover of the whole flat is not. A project pile from a clear-out is not.

This note is those two objects. The list of what a household load can include is the [acceptance guide](/blog/what-we-take-dubai). The order of a move-out, with the rooms and the deadline, is the [end-of-tenancy guide](/blog/end-of-tenancy-clearance-dubai). Come back here when the question is the scale on the bathroom floor and the small bin beside it, and what is still in the bin.

## Make sure it is a scale and a bin

A bathroom scale, for this note, is a freestanding scale made to stand on a floor and weigh a person. It may be a flat digital pad. It may be a dial you can see without a battery. It may be glass on top of a plastic base, or metal, or a soft mat over a sensor. Write which one you have. “Flat pad, glass top, no dial.” “Round dial, needle, no plug.” “Pad, display dark, battery door shut.”

A small bin, for this note, is a freestanding bin that stands on the floor or in a corner, with a lid or without one. It may be a pedal bin. It may be a swing bin. It may be an open tub. Write the shape and say it is the bin, not the scale. “Pedal bin, lid on, liner in.” “Open tub, no lid, empty.” “Swing lid, will not close.”

They are two lines even if they leave on the same visit. Do not strap the scale to the bin. Do not put the scale in the bin and call that one item. A photo of the pair is useful. A sentence that says “the bathroom” is not a count.

A toilet, a cistern, a bidet, a sink, a shower screen or a bath is a fixture. This page does not remove it, and it does not tell you to disconnect water. If the scale is beside the pan, the pan stays. Write “scale beside the pan” and do not try to shift the pan to make a better photo. If the bin is under a sink, the sink stays. Write “bin under the sink” and do not unscrew the trap, the waste or the taps.

A plumbed towel rail, a shower hose and a tap are fittings. Leave them. A rail that will not lift off is not a bin and not a scale. Write “rail staying” and do not follow the steps below as if a pipe were a handle.

A mirror is not this pair, and it is not given a URL here. If a mirror is leaving, name it as a mirror on its own line and keep it out of these steps. Do not call a mirrored cabinet a scale because it hangs in the same room.

A medicine cabinet that is still fixed to the wall is a fitting. Write “cabinet fixed” and do not unscrew it to reach a scale. Medicine that you can already see, in the bin or on a shelf, comes out and stays with you. This page does not say where medicine goes.

A kitchen scale on a counter is not a bathroom scale. A luggage scale is not a bathroom scale. Write the name you can see and keep it off these steps. A set of suitcases is its own note. Do not put a luggage scale inside a case and call the result this page.

A laundry basket is not a bathroom bin. A stack of baskets, emptied, with the nest tried, is the [laundry-basket note](/blog/laundry-basket-stack-dubai). A pedal bin does not become a basket because both can hold clothes. Do not follow the nest steps on a bin.

A desk bin in a study is not this pair. There is no scale on a bathroom floor in that case. Name it as a desk bin and keep it off these steps.

A ceiling fan is not this page. Do not book a fan by calling it a bathroom scale.

If you are not sure, write the shape. “Flat pad on the floor, display at one end” is a scale. “Tub with a pedal” is a bin. A wrong name is worse than a plain description.

## Empty the bin, and look under the scale

Start with the bin, because that is where small things hide.

Look inside, not only at the lid. A liner comes out if it already lifts. Shake it over a surface you can see, not over the floor you will have to clean twice. Write “liner out” or “liner kept”. If the liner is clipped and the clips already open by hand, open them. If a clip will not move, leave the liner in and write “liner stuck”. Do not cut the liner out.

What comes out stays out. Cotton, a bottle, a tube, a razor, a toothbrush, a card, a key. A key and a card stay with you. A bottle you recognise as something you are already discarding can leave as its own small line, named, and not inside the bin. If you do not know what the bottle is, it does not go back in. Write “bottle, not identified”. Do not pour it into the sink, and do not pour it into the bin again to hide it.

Do not bag the contents and put the bag back in the bin and call the bin empty. The bag is a second object. The bin is the shell.

Do not hose the bin in the bathroom. Water has to go somewhere, and a wet bin is heavier and can leak in a corridor. If it smells, say so in plain words: “empty, smells, not washed”. Do not spray a chemical into a lidded bin and then shut the lid.

Then the scale. Lift it if it already lifts. Look at the floor under it, and at the back. A hair tie, a coin, a strip of paper. Those are not the scale.

Turn it over only if you can hold it. Look for a battery door that already has a catch you use. If the door opens by hand, you may take the batteries out and keep them with you. Write “batteries out” or “no battery inside”. If the door will not open, do not pry it with a knife and do not crack the case. Write “battery door shut”. This page does not name a place to take a battery, and it does not open a sealed pad to find one.

A dial scale with no door stays closed. Do not take the glass off to see the spring. Write “dial, case closed”.

If the top is glass and you can see a crack, write “glass cracked”. Do not pick pieces out to make it look whole. A piece that has already come off goes in a bag with the scale. Write “broken piece, bagged with scale”. Do not tape the crack as a repair. The photo has to show the scale you have.

Do not stand on the scale to “test it” for the photo. A reading is not a preparation step. This page does not say what a scale should read, and it does not ask you to prove it works.

A plug, if you can see one, can be unplugged. Most of these scales have no plug. If a lead runs into the wall and you cannot see a plug, write “lead, no plug visible” and do not cut it. A scale with a lead you cannot trace is still this note. It is an honest line.

## The lid that will not shut, and the pad that will not lie flat

What makes the pair awkward is a lid, a pedal, or a pad that no longer lies flat.

Try the lid by hand, the way you would close it after emptying it. If it shuts, shut it and write “lid shuts”. If it stops on the liner, the liner is not out. Go back one step. If the lid is bent and will not sit, write “lid will not shut” and leave it as it sits. Do not remove the lid by breaking the hinge. Do not stand on the lid to flatten it.

A pedal that already moves can be left as it is. Write “pedal moves” or “pedal stuck”. Do not pull the pedal off to make the bin shorter. A stuck pedal is part of the shape.

A swing lid that spins and will not stay open can be held open with your hand for the photo of the inside, then left as it rests. Do not tape it open for the journey. Tape is not a hinge.

The scale lies flat if it already does. If a foot has come off and the pad rocks, write “rocks, foot missing” and do not glue a coin to the corner to level it. If a soft mat is peeling, leave it. Write “mat peeling”. Do not cut the mat off to make a thinner pad. A cut edge is a different object from the scale you started with.

If you have already forced the lid and the hinge is split, stop. Write “hinge split” and do not press it back together.

Count after you have looked. “One scale, one bin” is a count. “The bathroom bits” is not a count. A second bin in another room is a second line. Do not add a kitchen bin into this note because it is also a bin. If you bring them together, count again and say which one was in the bathroom.

## Where they are standing

The pair is awkward because of the room, as well as because of the lid.

On a wet floor, do not slide the scale through the water to the door. Wipe only if you are already drying that floor for yourself. A wet pad is still the same scale. Write “floor wet” if that is why you have not moved it. Do not claim you dried a leak. This page does not deal with a leak, and it does not book wet furniture.

Behind the door, where the door hits the bin, move the bin only if it moves without forcing the door off its hinges. Write “door hits the bin” if it still hits. Do not take the door off.

Under a sink, among bottles you are keeping, move the bottles you are keeping, then look at the bin. If a pipe blocks the lift and you would have to loosen a fitting to get the bin out, stop. Write “pipe in the way, bin not forced”. The pipe stays. The bin, if it will not come out, is photographed where it is.

A scale half under a cabinet can be slid out if it slides by hand. If the cabinet is a fixed vanity and the scale is trapped, write “under the vanity, not pulled out”. Do not unscrew the vanity. Do not saw the plinth.

In a shared bathroom, do not leave the scale in the hall the night before to “get it out of the way”. The place you photograph is the place it leaves from. A bin you have not emptied does not belong in a corridor.

If the tenancy is ending and the question is the whole flat, the end-of-tenancy guide keeps the order: what leaves, what stays with permission, and what you have not decided. A scale and a bin are two lines inside that order. They do not replace the walk-through. If the inventory says the scale or the bin belongs to the landlord, leave it and write “not released”. This page does not read a tenancy for you.

## Ordinary junk, not a fixture

You can book [ordinary junk, not a fixture](/services/junk-removal) once the bin is empty or the stuck liner is named, the scale’s case is still closed or the batteries are already out, and the two lines are written as two lines. That page is a single pickup or a mixed household load. It is not a request to remove a toilet, a sink, or a rail, and it is not a request to open a sealed scale.

A project pile from a clear-out or a refit is a different load. If the scale and the bin are sitting in that pile, [leave both out of a project load](/services/waste-removal) and say in the message that they are separate lines. That page is the project. These two are not part of it because they were in the same room.

The acceptance guide already lists what a household load can include. Stay on that guide for the wider list. Stay on this page for the scale and the bin. Do not send a message that says “bathroom” and expect the guide to guess which two objects you mean.

What a household container will not take, including a chute, stays on the [bins guide](/blog/what-dubai-bins-wont-take). A rigid bin does not become chute waste because it is small, and a scale does not become a bag because you wish it would. Do not post either object into a chute to test the fit.

The bottles, the liner and the medicine stay off this note once they are out. A scale you are giving to someone who still wants it is not a collection. Arrange that handover yourself. Keep it out of the leaving photos until they have collected it.

The business removes items. It does not buy them, sell them or say what a scale is worth.

## Measure the two lines

Measure after you have emptied the bin, not before, and not with a guess.

The scale: length, width and the thickness of the pad, including a display that sticks up. If it rocks, say so, and measure it as it lies. The bin: height with the lid as it will travel, shut or not, and the width including a pedal that sticks out. If the lid will not shut, measure the height you actually have, and write “lid open in the measure”.

Then look at the opening they have to pass. The useful figures are the clear height and clear width of the bathroom door, with the door as open as it goes, and of any narrower door on the way out. A bin under a sink still has to pass that door if it is coming out. A washing basket or a towel rail can change the gap. Move what you are keeping, then measure if the gap changed.

If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. This note does not keep a standard size for a scale, a bin or a bathroom door.

Write one sentence you measured. “Scale flat, bin taller than the gap under the sink” or “both on the floor, not yet compared with the door” or “not measured”. Do not write “it will fit” if you have not compared the figures. Do not write “it will not fit” from memory of a different door.

Weight is not something to guess. A bin you cannot lift because it is still full is not a weight. Empty it first. If the scale is awkward only because the glass is cracked, write “cracked, carry flat”. Do not write a number you did not weigh.

The wider method for one object and the narrowest point on the way out stays with the [bulky-item note](/blog/bulky-item-pickup-dubai) if the corridor and the lift are the question. Stay on this page for the bin, the case and the two lines. Do not send two descriptions that disagree about whether the liner came out.

## One line in the booking

Say, in one message:

- a bathroom scale, the kind, and where it stands
- a small bin, the kind, and where it stands
- bin empty, or liner stuck and what you believe is inside
- batteries out, or battery door shut, or a dial with the case closed
- glass cracked, or not
- the height and the widths you measured, or “not measured”
- that the toilet, the sink and any rail are staying
- anything else leaving from the same room, as its own line

Do not write “bathroom” as the only word if the room also holds a fixture and a cabinet you have not decided about. Do not write “bin empty” if the liner is still clipped in. Do not ask for a pedal to be cut because the figure is larger than the gap. The figure is the report. A cut is not a step on this page.

If you have not emptied the bin, you are early. Empty it if the liner already lifts. If the clip will not move, the stuck liner is the honest line. Send that.

On the morning, put the scale and the bin where the photos put them. Check a liner has not gone back in overnight. Check a battery you removed is not sitting on the pad. A bottle dropped back inside is not an empty bin. Take it out before you treat the old note as current.

Point at the pad as the scale. Point at the shell as the bin. Point at a toilet, a sink or a rail only to say it is staying. The useful record is the sentence from the first check: a bathroom scale and a small bin, the bin emptied or the liner named, the case left closed, kept as two lines, written in plain words.
