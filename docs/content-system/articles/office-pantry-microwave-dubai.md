The item is one microwave in an office pantry. Name that unit, empty it, and keep it out of a sack of ordinary waste. A booking that says “the pantry” will be read as the cups, the kettle, the fridge and this microwave together. This note is the microwave only.

A microwave in a home kitchen, sitting on a list of unlike machines, stays on the [old-appliance register](/blog/old-appliance-removal-dubai). Come back here when the only machine is this pantry unit. A one-off load of office material, with this microwave as one line among many, stays on the [office-waste guide](/blog/office-waste-collection-dubai). A plumbed drinking-water cabinet in the same room is not this microwave. That cabinet has its own note. This page is the countertop unit in the pantry, and nothing else from that room unless you name it as a separate line.

## Name the unit you can see

Stand in the pantry and say what is leaving. “Microwave, office pantry, countertop” is a name. “The kitchen” is not. Write the floor and the room. If the office has more than one pantry, say which one: “pantry by the meeting rooms” is a name. “Staff kitchen” is a name if that is the only one.

Look at how the unit meets the shelf. If it sits on a shelf or a counter and you can lift it clear, write “countertop”. If it is screwed into a run of cabinets, or the door of a cabinet is the door of the microwave, write “fitted” and stop this note. A fitted unit is a cabinet question, or a question for the person who may release the fitting. Do not unscrew it to make this page fit. Do not pull a built-in front off a carcass.

Look at the plug. If you can see a plug in a socket, write “plug visible”. If the lead goes into the wall or into the cabinet and you cannot see a plug, write “plug unseen”. Do not cut the lead to find the plug. Do not open a wall plate. A lead you cannot trace is a fact to write down, not a reason to cut.

Write the colour and the door. A black door, a silver door, a handle on the right, a handle on the left. If the door is dented, write “door dented” and say which edge as you face it. If the door will not shut, write “door will not shut”. Do not tape it shut and then describe it as closed.

If two microwaves sit in the same pantry, count two. “Left microwave” and “right microwave” are names. “One microwave” is wrong if you can see two. If only one is leaving, say which, and move the one that is staying out of the photograph or label it in the note as staying.

A model plate on the door or the back is useful if you can read it without moving a fitted unit. Write the words you can read. If you cannot read them, write “plate unread”. Do not invent a model. This page does not keep a list of sizes.

## Unplug it and leave the cord whole

If the plug is visible and the socket is one you already use, switch the socket off if it has a switch, then pull the plug. Coil the lead and write “unplugged”. Do not cut the lead to make the coil smaller. Do not remove the plug from the lead. A cut lead is a different object from the one you are describing, and it hides whether the unit was free.

If the plug is unseen, leave the lead as it is and write “not unplugged, plug unseen”. Do not assume a crew will find a safe way to free a lead that disappears into a cabinet. Say what you can see. If a facilities person has already freed it, write “freed by facilities” only if that person has told you so. Do not write it because the unit looks loose.

Look at the lead for a nick, a tape repair, or a melted plug. Write what you see: “lead nicked”, “plug taped”, “plug melted”. Do not wrap a fresh tape over a melt and then describe the plug as sound. The person reading the note needs the condition you can see.

A shelf that the microwave sat on stays. If the shelf is part of the pantry joinery, it is not this booking. Do not book the shelf because the microwave left a ring on it. If a loose tray or a mat under the unit is leaving, name it. If it is staying, it leaves the photograph.

The wall socket stays. Do not unscrew a socket, a spur or a fused plate on this page. “Socket stays” is the right line when the unit is unplugged. A socket that looks damaged is a fact about the wall. Write “socket looks damaged” if that is what you can see, and leave the repair to the person who looks after the building. This note does not become that repair.

## Empty the cavity before you call it empty

Open the door. Take out the food, the mug, the cover and the paper towel. If something is staying, it leaves the photograph. If something is leaving and it is not the microwave, name it or bag it as its own line. Do not leave a bowl inside and then say the unit is empty.

The glass plate and the roller ring are part of the unit. If they lift out by hand, you may take them out, set them with the microwave, and write “plate out, ring out”. If a plate is cracked, write “plate cracked”. Do not throw the cracked plate into a waste sack and then describe the microwave as complete. Keep the pieces with the unit, or write “plate missing” if it is already gone. Do not tape a crack and then describe the plate as whole.

Look at the roof of the cavity for a stirrer cover or a light cover. If it is in place, write “cover in place”. If it is hanging, write “cover loose”. Do not pull a cover off to see the lamp. A lamp that still works, or does not, is not the booking. Write “light works” or “light does not work” only if you have tried the door and the power while it was still plugged in, and you have since unplugged it. If you have not tried it, write “light not checked”.

Grease on the walls is a condition, not a reason to take the unit apart. Write “grease inside” if you can see it. Do not scrape it with a knife. Do not run the unit to “clean it” on this page. A smell is a fact if you notice it. Write “smell of food” and leave it. Do not spray a chemical into the cavity and then book the unit as if the spray were part of the job. If a cleaner has already been used and the cavity is wet, write “cavity wet” and leave the door ajar so the note matches the photo. Do not claim it is dry.

A rack, a grill tray or a crisp plate that belongs to this unit stays with it. If you can see it, name it: “grill rack with unit”. If the rack is missing, write “rack missing”. Do not borrow a rack from a second microwave that is staying.

Look under the unit if you can lift it a little without straining a lead that is still unseen. Crumbs, a spoon, a torn mat. If you are keeping them, they leave the photograph. If the unit will not lift because it is fitted, you are on the wrong page. If it is heavy and the shelf would mark, write “not lifted” and say you have not seen the underside. Do not claim the underside is clear if you have not looked.

## Do not put it in a waste sack

The mistake this page exists to stop is the sack. A microwave is not a bag of cups, a bag of food, or a bag of paper from the pantry. Do not drop it into a black sack and write “pantry waste”. Do not nest it inside a carton of mixed rubbish and hope the photo is enough. The unit stands on its own in the note, with the door described and the plate named.

You [do not bag it as ordinary waste](/services/waste-removal). That page is a project or site load. It is not a request to hide this unit in the same sacks as the cups. If the office is also clearing a real pile of fit-out or clear-out material, that pile is its own line on the office-waste guide. This microwave is still named here, even if it leaves on the same visit.

A home microwave that is one line among a fridge, a washer and an oven is the old-appliance register. Do not use this pantry note to skip that register. If the only machine in the message is this office unit, you are in the right place. If the message is a kitchen of unlike machines, you are not.

Food that came out of the cavity is not the microwave. If the food is being discarded, bag the food on its own and say so, or leave it out of the booking if someone else is taking it. Do not put the food back inside to “keep it together”. A plate of food inside a microwave is the wrong photo.

Packaging from a new microwave that has just arrived is not this old unit. If shaped foam or a carton is the pile, that is a packaging note, not this page. Name the old unit if it is still here. Do not book the carton as the microwave.

## What else in the pantry stays off this note

A kettle, a coffee machine, a water boiler and a box of mugs are not this microwave. If one of them is leaving, give it its own line and its own photo. If it is staying, move it out of the frame. A booking that says “pantry appliances” will be read as all of them. This page will not carry that phrase as the only words.

A fridge or a bottle cooler in the pantry is a different machine. A cooler with a drinking-water pipe is the plumbed-cabinet note, and the pipe is that note’s question. Do not add the cooler to the microwave count because they share a worktop. A fridge with a door that dispenses water is still a fridge. Leave it on its own line.

A dishwasher, an oven or a cooker under a counter is not a countertop microwave. If you can see a fitted cooking appliance, you are early for this page. Name the microwave only if it is the countertop unit, and leave the fitted cooker where it is unless its own guide says otherwise.

Cups, plates, cutlery and open food are contents. Empty them from the photograph of the microwave. A box of unwanted cups can be its own line if someone has decided they are leaving. Do not hide the box inside the microwave and then say the unit is empty.

A table and chairs in a staff room next to the pantry are not this unit. If the chairs are a counted stack from a waiting room, they have their own note. Do not add a stack of chairs to a microwave booking because the same person is arranging both. Two notes, two counts.

A whole office being cleared is the office-waste guide. Use that guide when the inventory is the floor. Use this page when you have come back to the one microwave and the open question is how to name it without bagging it. Do not turn this note into the floor.

## The shelf, the socket and the route

Measure the unit as it will travel. Width, height and depth, including the handle and the plug if the plug sticks out. If the plate is out and travelling beside it, say so and measure the unit without pretending the plate is still inside. If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. This page does not keep a standard door size.

Walk the path to the door the unit has to pass. A microwave is often smaller than the door. Write that only if you have looked. A tight turn at a pantry door, a trolley that will not enter, or a sill you would have to lift over is still worth a sentence. “Pantry door is the pinch” is useful if that is what you can see. Do not solve a pinch by cutting the lead or the shelf.

If you have already been given a lift or loading note, add it. If you have not, write “no lift instruction yet”. Do not invent a lift size. Do not write a building rule you have not been given. If the pantry is on a working floor, say whether the corridor is in use. Do not leave the unit in that corridor overnight as a way to clear the shelf. Leave it in the pantry, empty, unplugged if the plug was visible, with the door described.

The person who may release the unit is the person who owns it or who has been told they may send it. In an office that may be the employer, the facilities contact, or a supplier who placed the unit. Ask that person. A scratched door is not permission. A tenancy list or an asset list that says the microwave stays means it stays off the booking. This page does not interpret a contract.

If a replacement microwave is arriving, say whether the old one is still here. A delivery of a new unit is not a promise that this one leaves with the delivery. If someone else is taking the old unit, write “old unit collected by the supplier” and do not book it twice. If it is still on the shelf, it is still this note.

## One line in the booking

You can add this unit to [a pickup for this one machine](/services/junk-removal) once it is named, the cavity is empty or the contents are written down, and the note says it is not in a sack. That page is a one-off load. It is not a request to clear the pantry, and it is not a request to cut a lead.

Say, in one message:

- microwave, office pantry, and which pantry if there are two
- countertop, or fitted — and if it is fitted, you are on the wrong page
- unplugged, or plug unseen
- plate and ring out, cracked, or missing
- door shuts, or door will not shut
- width, height and depth, or “not measured”
- anything that is not this unit: a kettle, a cooler, a fridge, a sack of cups, a stack of chairs
- the words “do not bag”

Do not write “pantry waste” as the only words. Do not write “unplugged” if the lead still disappears into the cabinet. Do not ask for the unit to be cut out of a fitted run. The figure and the plug are the report.

If the cavity is still full, you are early. Empty it, or name what is still inside. Send the microwave you can stand in front of and check.

A second microwave is a second line, with its own plug and its own plate. Do not write “microwaves” and hope the photo shows the number. Count them.

If the office is clearing the floor on the same day, keep this unit as its own line inside that larger list. The office-waste guide holds the floor. This page holds the microwave. The two notes can travel together. They do not become one sentence.

If you are keeping the microwave and only want the shelf cleared of other things, this page is the wrong page. Say that the unit is staying. Do not book a collection for an object you still want to use.

The old-appliance register remains the right page when the microwave is one of several unlike machines in a home. The sentence on that register is the list. The sentence on this page is one unit in an office pantry, kept out of a sack.
