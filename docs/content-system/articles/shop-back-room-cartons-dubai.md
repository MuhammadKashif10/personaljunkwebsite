The pile is a carton stack in the back room of a shop that is still trading. Count the dry empty cartons, keep stock and returns out of the stack, and book that stack with the rest of the load. That is the job. A shop being closed is not. A flat full of delivery boxes is not. Empty product cartons as a question of what was for sale are not. Cardboard a leak has soaked is not.

This note is the stack while the shop is open. A unit being handed back, with stock, fixtures and a last trading day, is the [shop-clearance guide](/blog/shop-clearance-dubai). Packaging left by a house move, and the test for dry board against board that is food-soiled or plastic-coated, is the [packing-material guide](/blog/packing-material-disposal-dubai). Cartons filling a flat someone still lives in are the [shipping-carton plan](/blog/shipping-cartons-filling-a-flat-dubai). Empty cartons that held goods for sale, when the question is what is still stock, are the [product-box note](/blog/product-boxes-not-moving-boxes-dubai). Board a leak has soaked is the [wet-cardboard note](/blog/wet-cardboard-after-a-leak-dubai). Come back here when the shop is trading, the stack is in the back room, and the question is the cartons that have built up since the last time the room was cleared.

## Make sure it is the back-room stack

A back-room stack, for this note, is cardboard cartons standing or lying in the room behind the sales floor: the stockroom, the corridor behind the counter, or the cage the shop uses for deliveries. The shop is open, or it will open again on the next trading day. The cartons are the packaging from goods that have already been put on the shelf, or packaging that arrived and was not taken away. Write the room and the rhythm. “Back room, behind the counter, this week’s deliveries, shop open.” “Stockroom cage, two weeks of cartons, flattened on the left, intact on the right.” “Service corridor the shop is allowed to use, one row, dry.”

The sales floor is not the back room. A carton left by a display for an hour is not this stack. If the cartons have spread onto the floor customers walk, say so, and still count them as this pile only when they are packaging you have already decided is leaving. Do not call a window display a back-room stack.

A shop being emptied is not this stack. The shop-clearance guide is the register: what transfers, what returns to a supplier, what the landlord keeps, and what is approved for disposal. A last trading day, a fit-out handback and a strip of counters are that guide. Do not use this page to clear a unit. If the shop is closing, stop and use that guide. A stack that remains after those decisions, and only the stack, can come back here.

A fit-out is not this stack. Shelving, counters, signage, timber offcuts and a floor that is coming up are the [retail fit-out guide](/blog/retail-fit-out-waste-removal-dubai). Do not bag a counter and call it cartons. Do not put screws and offcuts inside a carton so the photo looks like board.

A house move is not this stack. The packing guide is the move. A flat filling with boxes from things the household bought is the shipping-carton plan. If you live above the shop and the cartons are in the flat, you are on that plan. Stay on this page only when the cartons are in the shop’s back room.

Product boxes, as a decision about stock, are not this page’s job. The product-box note is whether a carton still holds something for sale, an open order or a return. Do that check. Then, if the shop is still trading and the empty board is the weekly stack in the back room, the stack is this page. Do not keep two notes that say the same pile is both a stock decision and a volume decision without saying which cartons are which.

Wet board is not this stack. If a leak, a spill or a drip has soaked the bottom layer, the wet-cardboard note is the separation. Pull the dry cartons off the wet ones before you use this page for the dry stack. Do not flatten wet sheets into the dry bundle.

A warehouse being emptied is not a shop back room. A warehouse has aisles, retained stock and a loading shutter. That unit stays on its own guide. Do not call a shop stockroom a warehouse because the stack is tall.

Office waste, a pallet of tiles, and a microwave in a pantry are not this stack. They are not given a URL from this page. If one of them is in the back room, name it as what it is and keep it out of the carton count.

If you are not sure, write the room and whether the shop is still trading. “Stockroom, shop open today, empty cartons from this week’s delivery” is enough. “Unit being handed back on Friday” is a shop clearance, not this note.

## Keep stock, returns and goods out of the stack

A carton is not waste while it still holds something the shop has not released. Open it before it joins the outgoing stack. Look for the product, a spare part, a price label you still use, an invoice and a returns note. A sealed carton with a barcode is a reason to stop, not a reason to flatten faster.

Stock that is going back on the shelf stays on the shelf side of the room. Do not stand it against the empty cartons. A crew cannot tell a full carton from an empty one if both are brown and shut. Write “stock, not in the stack” and show that side in a separate photo, or move it to a shelf you then leave out of the outgoing photo.

A return the supplier is still due to collect stays intact. Leave that carton out of the stack and write “supplier collection, not included”. Do not flatten it because the stack looks tidier without the odd size. If the supplier has said in writing that they will not take the packaging, that carton can join the stack once it is empty. If they have not answered, it stays out.

A customer return that is still open is not waste. Finish the return, or decide it is not happening, before the carton is flattened. A returns label is not something a collection can judge.

Personal papers, a staff list, a till receipt bundle and a supplier account do not ride inside a carton that looks empty. Lift them out. This page does not decide which paper the business must keep. If you have not decided, put the paper in a box that is not the outgoing stack and write “paper, not decided”.

Food, a leaking bottle, a chemical, a battery and a gas canister are not packaging. Take them out. Do not tape a carton shut to hide a doubtful item. The shop-clearance guide already says not to hide controlled goods in ordinary cartons. The same line applies while the shop is still open. Name the item and keep it off this stack.

A wooden pallet under the cartons is not a carton. Photograph it, including broken boards and nails. Do not slide it under a photo of flattened board and hope it is included. A pallet is not given a URL here. It stays its own line.

## Dry board, and the other materials in the same delivery

Once a carton is empty and yours to discard, it can sit with the other empty board. Flatten it if you can do that without burying a carton you have not opened. The packing guide is how to flatten, bundle and describe dry board, and when wet or food-soiled board should stay off a recycling container. Follow that guide. Do not invent a second set of bin rules on this page. This page is the stack in the back room. Flattened board can leave with the other junk. It is not a recycling contract, and it is not a separate carton service.

Foam corners, plastic film, polystyrene and a strap do not become cardboard because they arrived with the cartons. Pull them out and give them a line of their own. A hanger, a tissue insert and a plastic collar from a garment delivery are not board. Bag them, or say they are loose, and do not hide them inside a flattened bundle.

A carton that got wet from a fridge drip, a mop bucket or a roof leak should be kept off the dry stack. Say that it is wet, and use the wet-cardboard note for that layer. The packing guide’s note on wet board stays the recycling test. Wet board is also heavier and it collapses when it is carried. Show it so it is not priced as a neat dry bundle.

Intact cartons and flattened sheets are different volumes. Intact cartons are mostly air. Flattened sheets take less space and are easier to carry, and they can still fill a back room if there are enough of them. The [load guide](/blog/how-much-fits-in-one-load-dubai) is why those two shapes are not the same volume. You do not need a tonnage figure. You need the wide photo and the words “intact” or “flattened” for each pile.

Do not break cartons down onto the sales floor during opening hours if that is the only place you can stand. Flatten in the back room, or after the shop has closed, and say which you did. A customer route full of sheets is not a tidy stack.

Count what you can see without pulling the stack apart. If you cannot see the back row, say the photo is the front only. A hidden second row is how a quote describes half the load. Do not climb a stack to count the back. Describe the limit of what you can see.

## Where the stack is standing

The stack is awkward because of the room it has filled, as well as because of the number of cartons.

Write which door the stack blocks. The door to the sales floor, the door to the service corridor, the door staff use for a delivery. Leave the door the shop still needs. Do not build the stack against the only exit from the back room. If the stack is already against that door, say so, and do not add more in front of it.

A cage or a shelf of cartons is still a count. “One cage, front full, back not seen” is a better line than “the stockroom”. A shelf of stock with empty cartons on the bottom is two things. Point at the empty cartons. Do not book the shelf.

The path from the back room to the loading point is part of the note. Say whether that path is inside the shop, a service corridor, a lift, or a door onto a street. If you have been given a time when deliveries may use that path, write the time you were given. Do not invent a mall rule, a lift size or a loading-bay hour. If you have not been given one, write “no access time given yet”.

Do not leave the stack in a shared corridor overnight so it is “ready”. A shared corridor is not the back room. Keep the cartons in the room the shop controls until the collection is the next thing that happens. If the building has told you the cartons cannot stay in the back room past a named hour, write that hour as it was told to you, and do not add a penalty or a fine you have not been shown.

A stack beside a working fridge, a kettle or a staff microwave needs a gap. Do not lean cartons on a hot appliance. Do not cover a switch. If a drip from the fridge has already marked the bottom layer, that layer is the wet note, not this stack.

If the shop has a second pile on the sales floor from today’s delivery, say so. One request that shows only the back room, when the sales floor is also holding cartons you want gone, describes half the job. If the sales-floor cartons are still full of stock, they are not this stack. Say that too.

## One stack, not a clearance

Daily or weekly cartons are a repeating pile. This page is one clear-out of the pile you can see now. It is not a contract for every week, and it is not a shop closure.

Book the stack when any of these is true:

- Staff cannot use the back room because of the cartons.
- A delivery is due and there is nowhere left to stand it.
- The building has told you not to keep the stack where it is.
- The cartons are mixed with other shop waste you also want gone, and you can name that waste.
- A date is fixed for a count, a visit or a new delivery, and the pile will still be there.

A single carton from one courier is not this page. The page starts when the cartons, together, have become the obstacle in the back room.

Do not promise the sales floor will be clear, or that every carton will be flat before the collection, until you have looked at the whole back room. A cage you forgot is still part of the load.

Ask to book [a cardboard collection with the rest of the load](/services/junk-removal). In the same message, list the other items that are actually leaving: a broken display, a bag of film, a pallet you have photographed. The junk-removal page says flattened cardboard can ride in a junk load, and that the page is not a cardboard recycling route. There is no separate carton contract on this page. Do not describe the stack as a baling job or a recycling contract. You are booking the cartons you have counted, with whatever else you have named.

If the shop-clearance guide is also open because the unit is closing, do not send this stack as a substitute for that register. Send the register there, and name any leftover empty cartons here only after the stock decision is made.

## Photos of the stack, not the shopfront

Take the photos the request needs.

- One wide photo from the back-room door, with the floor and the remaining walking space.
- Intact or flattened, visible in that frame, or a second frame if one pile is behind the other.
- Any wet, stained or crushed carton, close enough to see it, kept out of the dry pile.
- Stock and returns that are staying, either out of the frame or pointed at so they are not loaded.
- The other waste in the same visit, if there is any.

A photo of the shopfront, a logo, or a single neat carton does not show volume. A photo of the sales floor, when the stack is in the back, does not show the stack. If the door is blocked, photograph from the side you can reach and say the door is blocked. Do not climb the stack.

If another delivery arrives after you send the photos, send a new wide shot before the day. Do not wait until the vehicle is loaded to mention a second cage.

## One line in the booking

Say, in one message:

- the shop is still trading, and the pile is the back room
- how many cartons you can see, and whether the back row is unseen
- intact or flattened, for each pile
- dry or wet, if any board has been soaked
- stock, returns and supplier cartons that must stay
- film, foam and a pallet as their own lines, if they are there
- the path out, and any access time you have actually been given
- that this is not a shop closure, not a flat of delivery boxes, and not a fit-out

Do not write “cardboard” as the only word. Do not write “the shop” if the unit is closing and the register is unfinished. Do not write “all dry” if you have not lifted the bottom layer.

If you have not opened the sealed cartons, you are early. Open the ones you are allowed to open. Leave the supplier’s carton intact. If the bottom layer is wet, you are also on the wet-cardboard note for that layer. The dry stack can still use this page once it is separated.

On the morning, walk the back room again. A delivery that arrived overnight is part of the count if it is empty and leaving, and it is not part of the count if it is still stock. Check the door staff need. Check the carton you set aside for the supplier. It should still be there.

The useful record is one back-room stack, shop still trading, stock kept out, dry board named as intact or flat, booked with the rest of the load, written in plain words.
