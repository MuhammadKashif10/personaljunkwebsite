The item is a set of suitcases that will not be used again. Empty each one of papers and anything you still need, try the zip or the clasp it already has, and keep the set as its own line. That is the job. The rest of a move out of the home is not. A plastic pet carrier in a store is not. A pile of cartons from the unpack is not.

This note is the cases. A departure, with the rooms, the dates and everything else that has to leave, is the [leaving checklist](/blog/leaving-dubai-clearance-checklist). A packed store, with shelves and a path to the door, is the [storage-room guide](/blog/storage-room-clearance-dubai). Cartons, film and foam from a move stay on the [guide to sorting packing material](/blog/packing-material-disposal-dubai). Come back here when the question is the cases you have decided not to travel with again, and what is still inside them.

## Make sure it is a suitcase

A suitcase, for this note, is a hard or firm case with a shell, made to hold clothes for a trip, with a zip, a clasp or a combination of both, and usually a handle. It may be one case. It may be a set of two or three that nested when they were new. It may have wheels, or a strap, or a lock. Write which ones you have, and how many. “Two hard shells, one zip stuck, both with wheels.” “One case, clasps, no wheels, will not shut.” “Three nested, the smallest will not come out.”

A plastic pet carrier is not a suitcase. A carrier has a door for an animal, a grille or a flap, and vents. The [pet-carrier note](/blog/pet-carrier-removal-dubai) is that box and that door. A suitcase has no animal door. Do not call a carrier a suitcase because both were used for a trip, and do not call a suitcase a carrier because both have a shell.

A soft duffel, a backpack, a tote or a garment bag with no firm shell is not this set. Write “soft bag” and keep it off these steps. It can leave in the same visit only as its own line. Do not call it a suitcase because you want one note for every bag in the store.

A cardboard carton is not a suitcase. If the pile is boxes from a move, stay on the packing guide. Do not put a case inside a carton and call the result packing, and do not describe a case as a box because both are containers.

A trunk that is a piece of furniture, with a lid, sitting as a table or a chest, is not this set. If it is furniture, name it as furniture. This page does not empty a furniture chest and call it luggage.

A briefcase, a laptop bag or a cabin bag you are still using for work is not a case you have finished with. If you are keeping it, it leaves the photograph. This page is the set that will not be used again.

A case that is packed for a flight you are still taking is not this page. Close it, move it off the leaving stack, and write “still travelling”. Do not book the case that holds the clothes you are wearing next week.

If you are not sure, write “firm shell, zip around the edge, not sure it is a suitcase”. A label you can read is better than a guess.

## Empty it before you try the zip

Open the case if it already opens. Take out documents, a passport, medicine, a charger, keys, a card and any letter. Those stay with you. A case that is leaving does not carry them, even if they were in a pocket you forgot.

Look in the lid pocket, the shoe pocket and under the lining if the lining already lifts. A lining that is torn can hide a small thing. If the lining will not lift, do not cut it. Write “lining not lifted” if you could not see behind it.

Clothes come out. Shoes come out. This page does not book the clothes, and it does not tell you where they should go. If you are keeping them, put them where you will still find them, off the photograph. If you have not decided, put them in one open pile that is not the leaving set, and write “clothes, not decided”. A case that still holds a wardrobe is not an empty case.

Do not fill a case with other rubbish so that it “does one job”. Do not put pillows, a duvet or kitchen waste inside and zip it shut. A case full of bedding is a shell you have not emptied. Bedding that is leaving has its own decision, off this note.

A wash bag, a bottle and a tube come out. If you know the bottle is ordinary and you are already discarding it, it can leave as its own small line, named, and not inside the case. If you do not know what it is, it does not go back in. Write “bottle, not identified”. Do not pour it into another case.

Do not hose a case in the store. Water has nowhere to go, and a wet shell is heavier and can stain the floor when it is tipped. This page does not include a wash. If the case smells, say so in plain words: “empty, smells, not washed”. Do not spray a chemical inside and then force the zip shut.

If the case will not open, do not cut the shell to empty it. Write “will not open” and what you believe is still inside, from the last time you used it. If you do not know, write “contents not known”. Do not add anything through a gap.

A lock you have the key or the code for can be opened, and then the key stays with you, not in the case. Write “lock open”. A lock you cannot open stays locked. Write “locked, no key”. Do not drill the lock, and do not smash the shell to avoid the lock. A locked case is still this note. It is an honest line, not a failure you have to repair before you send the photo.

## The zip, the wheels and the handle

The zip is the part that decides whether the case is a closed shell or an open one.

If the zip already runs by hand, the way it did on the last trip, run it. Write “zip closes” or “zip opens, will not close”. A zip that stops halfway stays halfway. Do not pull with pliers. Do not cut the zip out to make the case lighter. Write “zip stuck” and leave the teeth as they are.

A clasp that already turns or snaps by hand can be fastened or left open. Write which. If a clasp is bent, write “clasp bent” and do not hammer it flat.

Wheels come off only if they already come off by a button or a pin you can move by hand, and only if you are keeping the wheels with the case in a bag you will not lose. It is usually clearer to leave the wheels on. Write “wheels on”. A wheel that is already missing gets the words “one wheel missing”. Do not fit a random screw to replace it.

A trolley handle that already slides down by its button can be pushed in. Write “handle in”. If the button will not move, leave the handle where it is and write “handle stuck out”. Do not saw the handle off to make the case shorter. A cut tube is a sharp end, not a smaller case.

A strap that is loose can come off if you want it with the case or in a separate bag. Write “strap off” or “strap on”. Do not cut a strap that is still stitched on just to make the photo neater.

A case that is split along the seam, or whose shell is cracked, stays in that shape. Write “shell cracked”. Do not tape it and then forget to say it is cracked. You may tape it only to hold a piece that is already loose, and the words stay in the note. Do not rebuild the case.

If two cases were sold as a set and the smaller one already slides inside the larger one by hand, you may nest them only when both are empty, and write “two nested, both empty”. If the inner one will not come out, or will not go in, write “will not nest” and leave them separate. Do not stand on a case to drive another one inside. Do not kick them apart.

Count after you have opened what will open. “Three cases: two empty and shut, one locked” is a count. “Some luggage” is not a count.

## Where the set is sitting

The cases are awkward because of where they were put.

On top of a wardrobe, do not pull them down by the handle if you cannot see the edge. Empty the shelf or the top until you can lift the shell, or leave them up there and write “on the wardrobe, not brought down”. A case that falls splits, and a split shell is a different shape from the one you measured. If you do bring one down, put it on the floor clear of the door.

In a store, under boxes, move the boxes you are allowed to move. The storage-room guide keeps the decision about the rest of the room. This note is the cases once you can see them. Do not call the corner “the suitcases” if the corner is a wall of cartons you have not decided about. If you cannot reach a case without unpacking that wall, write “blocked by boxes” and photograph it where it is.

Behind a door, a case that stops the door is part of this note. Open the door as far as it will go without forcing the case. If the door hits the shell, stop. Write “door hits the case”. Do not keep pushing.

A case used as a step, or with books stacked on it, is still a case. Take those things off. They are not the shell. If a book is leaving, it is not this page.

Tied to a shelf with string, untie the string if it is loose. Write “string off”. If the case is screwed or strapped to a fitting that is part of the room, write “fixed” and do not unscrew the building. This page starts when the case is already loose.

In a parking-bay locker, with its own key, the locker is its own note if that is the door you are clearing. This page stays the cases. Do not describe the locker as a suitcase.

A set spread across two rooms is still one decision only if you bring them together and count them in one place. If you leave them where they are, say how many are in each room. Do not write one number that covers a room you did not open.

## One set, not the move

The leaving checklist already walks the home, the dates and the groups of what is kept, given away or cleared. Stay on that list when the question is the departure. Stay on this page when the question is the cases that will not be used again. You can still use these steps on each case in a larger clear-out, one at a time, so each one is empty or the lock is named.

A case you are shipping with you, or that a mover is packing into a consignment that is still yours, is not a collection. Keep it out of the leaving photos. Write “still yours, not in this load”. This business removes items you have decided to discard. It does not relocate what you are keeping, and it does not pack a flight.

The storage-room guide keeps the inventory and the path. A set of cases is one group inside that room, or it is this page when the cases are the only job. Do not send two descriptions that disagree about the count.

The packing guide keeps the cartons. A suitcase is not a carton. If both are leaving, they are two lines. Do not flatten a case, and do not describe the set as boxes.

A second set, from another person in the home, is a second line unless that person has said these cases can go. A flatmate’s case, a guest’s case and a child’s school bag are not yours because they were in the store. If you do not have an answer, write “not released” and keep that case out of the booking.

Do not put documents back in “for safekeeping” after you have photographed an empty case. The case is leaving. Anything inside it leaves with it.

A case you are giving to someone who still wants it is not a collection. Arrange that handover yourself. Keep it out of the leaving photos until they have collected it. A message that says “a relative might collect it” is not a handover.

The business removes items. It does not buy them, sell them or say what a case is worth.

A cot, a car seat and a pet carrier are not this page. Say the name of the object you actually have. A live animal is not a line on this note.

## Measure the set as it will travel

Measure after you have tried the zip and the handle, not before, and not with a guess.

Length, width and height of each shell, including wheels and a handle that is stuck out. If the handle is in, say so and measure without the extra height. If two cases nested, measure the pair as it is, and say that you did. If they did not nest, measure each one.

Then look at the opening they have to pass. The useful figures are the clear height and clear width of the store door or the room door, with the door as open as it goes. A case on a wardrobe still has to pass that door if it is coming down. A box or a table leg can change the gap. Move what you are keeping, then measure if the gap changed.

If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. This note does not keep a standard size for a suitcase or for a store door.

Write one sentence you measured. “Three cases, handles in, the largest wider than the store door” or “one locked, not nested, not yet compared with the door” or “not measured”. Do not write “it will fit” if you have not compared the figures.

Weight is not something to guess. A case you cannot lift because it is still full of books is not a weight. Empty it first, or write “still full, not a weight”. If an empty case is still awkward, write “awkward to lift”. Do not write a number you did not weigh.

The wider method for one object and the narrowest point on the way out stays with the [bulky-item note](/blog/bulky-item-pickup-dubai) if the corridor and the lift are the question. Stay on this page for the zip, the contents and the count. Do not send two descriptions that disagree about whether a case was locked.

## Photos of the cases, not the checklist

The picture is a checklist notebook and stacked moving boxes. It does not show a suitcase, a zip, a wheel, a lock or a handle. Use it only as a reminder that the departure list and the cartons are a different job. Your own photos still have to show this set.

Send these views.

- The whole set, standing, with each case in the state you are leaving it.
- The zip or the clasp that will not move, close enough to see it.
- The inside, empty, or the words “will not open” or “locked, no key”, and what you believe is still in it.
- Wheels and the handle, in or stuck out.
- A cracked shell, if there is one.
- The door only if that opening is why a case is stuck.
- A second person’s case only if it is in the same photo, and then say whether it is released.

Add the room. Add the sentence about the zip. Add a lift or loading note if you have been given one. If you have not, write “no lift instruction yet”. Do not invent a lift size, and do not write a building rule you have not been given.

## One line in the booking

You can [collect the cases with other junk](/services/junk-removal) once each case is empty or the lock is named, and the note says they will not be used again. That page is a single pickup or a mixed household load. It is not a request to cut a zip, drill a lock, or pack what you are still taking with you.

Say, in one message:

- how many suitcases, and where they sit
- empty, or “locked, no key”, or “will not open”, and what is still inside
- zip closes, zip stuck, or clasp bent
- handles in, or handles stuck out
- the length, width and height you measured, or “not measured”
- anything else leaving from the same shelf, as its own line
- that it is not a pet carrier, not a carton, and not a case you are still travelling with

Do not write “luggage” as the only word if the store also holds a carrier, a wall of boxes and a bag you have not decided about. Do not write “they are empty” if you have not opened the lid pocket. Do not ask for a handle to be cut because the figure is larger than the gap. The figure is the report. A cut is not a step on this page.

If you have not tried the zip, you are early. Try the movement the case already has. If it will not move, the stuck zip or the missing key is the honest line. Send that.

On the morning, put the cases where the photos put them. Check nobody has used one overnight as a box for something else. A towel pushed back inside is not an empty case. Take it out before you treat the old count as current. Check the case you are still travelling with is still off the stack.

Point at the shells as the suitcases. Point at a carrier, a carton or a soft bag only if that object is its own line. The useful record is the sentence from the first check: a set of suitcases that will not be used again, emptied or the lock named, kept as its own line, written in plain words.
