The item is one bean bag that will not compact. Empty the cover, or name what is still inside it, and keep that bag as its own line. That is the job. A sofa is not. An armchair is not. A mattress is not. A room you are emptying is not.

This note is the bag. A sofa that will not turn a doorway is the [sofa doorway guide](/blog/sofa-wont-fit-through-door-dubai). An armchair with arms, a back and a mechanism is the [armchair guide](/blog/armchair-removal-dubai). A mattress, with its own size and its own route, is the [mattress guide](/blog/mattress-disposal-dubai). A small flat, and the order in which large pieces leave it, is the [decluttering guide](/blog/decluttering-small-dubai-apartment). Come back here when the question is one soft bag, what it is filled with, and why it will not squash into the shape you hoped.

## Make sure it is a bean bag

A bean bag, for this note, is one soft seat or one soft lounger with no wooden frame, filled with beads, foam chips or a shredded fill, and closed by a zip, a flap or a stitched seam. It may be a chair shape. It may be a long lounger. It may be a small floor cushion that is still a filled bag, not a flat scatter cushion. Write the shape you can see. “One chair-shaped bag, zip on the back, beads, living room.” “One long lounger, seam split, foam chips on the floor.” “One small bag, cover off, liner still shut.”

The cover is not a second item when it belongs to this bag. If the cover is on, they are one line. If the cover has come off and the liner is beside it, they are still one line when that liner is the bag. Write “cover on” or “cover off, liner beside it”. Do not book the cover as a cushion and the liner as rubbish. Do not book the beads as a separate sack unless they have already left the bag and you have gathered them. A handful of loose beads on the carpet is part of this line. A room of other soft things is not.

A sofa is not a bean bag. A sofa has a frame, arms or a back that stays shaped when nobody sits on it, and often feet or a sectional joint. That piece is the sofa doorway guide, and the sofa page is the booking for a sofa. Do not squash a bean bag into a photo of a sofa and call them one seat. Do not take the feet off a sofa and call the sofa a bag. If both are leaving, they are two lines.

An armchair is not a bean bag. An armchair has a built seat, arms, and often a reclining part or a swivel. The armchair guide is that chair. Do not unzip a bag and follow armchair steps. Do not call a bean bag a wingback because it has a high back when someone is sitting in it. When the person stands up, a bean bag slumps. An armchair does not.

A mattress is not a bean bag. A mattress has a labelled size, a stitched face, and a route of its own. The mattress guide is that piece. A thin pad on a camp bed is not this bag either. Do not roll a bean bag and call it a mattress. Do not wrap a mattress and call it a bag because both are soft.

Bags of duvets and pillows that will not go down a chute are bedding, not a bean bag. The [bedding note](/blog/duvets-that-will-not-fit-a-chute-dubai) is those bags. Do not tip a duvet into a bean bag cover and call the result one item. Do not call a bean bag bedding because both are soft and both refuse a chute. The bins page keeps what a chute is for. This page is the bag.

A floor cushion with no fill you can hear or feel moving, and no zip that opens onto beads, is a cushion. Name it as a cushion. Do not follow the steps below to empty a cushion that was never a bag. A pouffe with a rigid base, a wooden disc or a metal ring inside is not this bag. Write “rigid base” and keep it off these steps. This note starts when the object slumps, and the fill is loose inside a liner.

A child’s bean bag and an adult bean bag are the same kind of object. Write the size you see. Do not invent a second page because one of them is smaller. A whole children’s room, with more than this bag leaving, is not this page. The decluttering guide is the order for a small flat. A room being emptied as a room stays a room. One bag in that room can still use these steps.

A hanging egg chair, a hammock and a playpen are not this bag. They are not given a URL here. If one is in the room, name it as what it is and keep it out of the photograph of the bag.

If you are not sure, write the shape and the fill. “Grey fabric, chair shape, zip, beads move when I press it” is enough. “Blue liner, cover missing, foam chips, split along the seam” is enough. A wrong name is worse than a plain description.

## Empty the cover, or say why you stopped

Set the bag where you can see the opening, if it already sits on the floor. Do not drag it onto a sofa to make the photo look tidy. Do not stand it on end if it will not stay. A bag that falls over is still one bag. Write “will not stand” and leave it in the shape it has.

The cover comes off only if it already comes off by the zip or the flap it has. Write “cover off” when you have looked inside. If the zip will not move, leave the cover on and write “zip stuck”. Do not cut the cover to prove it is empty. A cut cover spills the fill, and the fill is then a second problem on the floor.

Look inside before you call it empty. A remote, a charger, a child’s toy, a set of keys and a coin purse sit in the crease. Those come out and stay with you. Anything you still need does not ride inside a bag that is leaving. If you cannot get a hand in because the zip has failed, write “inside not seen” and do not claim the bag is empty.

The liner is the part that holds the fill. If the liner is sound, leave the fill inside it. Write “liner shut, fill inside”. If the liner is split and beads or chips are already on the floor, gather what you can into a bag you can point at, and write “split, fill partly out”. Do not sweep the beads into a corridor, a lift or a chute. Do not open the split further so the bag looks flatter. A flatter bag that has shed its fill across a room is a worse job than a full bag with a named split.

Foam chips and polystyrene beads are not the same line as the shaped foam from an appliance. That foam is its own note. Here, chips that belong inside this bag stay with this bag. Do not tip them into a carton and call them packaging.

Do not wash the bag. Water in a bead fill has to go somewhere, and a wet bag is heavier and can stain the floor. If it smells, say so in plain words: “dry, smells, not washed”. Do not spray a chemical into the liner and then sit it back against a wall.

A label with a size or a care line can stay on. Do not scrape it off as a preparation step. The label is not the job.

If the bag is so full that the zip will not close again after you have looked, leave it as open as it is and write “zip will not close”. Do not sit on it, kneel on it, or strap it with a belt to force the zip. A forced zip breaks, and the fill comes out on the route.

## A bag that will not compact

What makes this bag a problem is that it keeps its volume. Pressing it with a hand does not make it a size that will go down a chute, into a car boot, or through a gap it does not already fit.

Do not sit on the bag to squash it for a measurement. The size you need is the size it has when nobody is on it. Write the length, the width and the height as it sits. If it changes shape when you let go, measure again after it has settled, and use that figure. Do not write the size you hope a crew can compress it to.

Do not force it into the building’s chute. The [bins guide](/blog/what-dubai-bins-wont-take) is what a chute and a bin are for. This page does not add a rule. If the building has already told you the chute will not take this bag, write “chute refused” and leave the bag in the flat. If you have not been told, do not test the chute with the bag. A bag that sticks in a chute is no longer a bag in a room.

Do not load it into a car boot by kneeling on it until the boot lid latches. If the boot will not close on the bag as it is, the boot is the wrong route. Write “will not fit the boot” and stop. A split liner in a car park is beads on a shared floor.

A strap you already own can go around the bag if the bag is already a size the strap can hold without crushing the seam. Write “strap on” only if the seam stayed shut. If the strap cuts into a split, take the strap off and write “strap off, seam split”. Do not add tape around a leaking liner and call that sealed. Tape hides the leak. It does not put the fill back.

A bag that has already been cut open is not made whole again on this page. Write “cut open” and keep the fill with the shell if you can do that without spreading it. Do not sew it as a preparation step. Do not staple the cover.

If two bags are leaving, they are two lines. Do not stack them and write one size. A stack of soft bags looks smaller in a photo than the two bags will be when they are carried. Count them on the floor, side by side, before you stack anything for the camera.

A bean bag with a rigid insert you can feel through the cover is not “will not compact” in the same way. Write “hard piece inside” and do not keep pressing until you know what the hard piece is. A book, a tray or a broken zip pull is one answer. A wooden base is another, and that base takes the bag off these steps, as the first section said.

## Where the bag is sitting

The bag is awkward because of where it was left, as well as because of the fill.

In a living room, write the seat it is beside. “Beside the sofa, not the sofa.” “In the corner, clear of the door.” If it is against the only exit, move it only if you can do that without the seam opening. If you cannot, write “against the exit, not moved”. Do not drag a leaking bag across a tiled hall.

In a bedroom, a bag used as a chair is still this page. The bed, the mattress and the wardrobe are their own lines if they are leaving. Do not photograph the bag on the bed and call the bed part of the bag. Put the bag on the floor for the photo if the floor is where it will be collected from. If it will be collected from the bed because you cannot lift it down, write “on the bed, staying there until collected” and say the bed itself is staying.

On a balcony, sun and a drip both matter. A bag that has baked is still a bag. Write “balcony, dry” or “balcony, wet underneath”. Do not hose it. Do not hang it over the rail. If rain has soaked the cover, say the cover is wet and keep it off a dry sofa. A wet bag is heavier. Show the wet patch in the photo.

A child’s room with one bag and a floor of toys is this page for the bag only. Toys you have decided are leaving are their own line. The decluttering guide is the decision about what else in a small flat has to go. Do not rename the toys as the bag.

A store room with one bag behind boxes is this page when the bag is the object. The boxes stay on their own note if the boxes are the pile. Say which you are booking. A photo that shows only boxes, when the bag is behind them, describes the wrong object.

The entrance door needs enough space for a person to pass. Leave the lock side clear. Do not stand the bag in the doorway overnight so it is “ready”. A bag in a shared corridor is not ready. It is in the way. Keep it inside until the collection is the next thing that happens.

If the route is stairs with no lift, say so. The [walk-up stairs guide](/blog/walk-up-stairs-no-lift-dubai) is the flights. A light-looking bag can still be an armful, and a split on a stair drops fill on every step. Do not start the carry yourself to prove it fits.

## One bag, not the room

A bean bag that has taken the corner of a small flat is one object. The decluttering guide is how you decide what else in that flat is costing floor space. Stay on that guide for the order of the rooms. Come back here for the bag. Do not book “the living room” because the bag is the thing you are tired of.

If the sofa, the armchair and the bag are all leaving, list them as three lines. The sofa guide and the armchair guide keep those pieces. This page keeps the bag. A request that says “seating” will be read as a sofa. Say the bag.

If the only other things leaving are ordinary household items, they can travel in the same visit once you have named the bag. Ask to [book a junk collection](/) for the visit, and put the bag in the same message as its own line. Do not use that sentence to mean a sofa booking, a mattress booking, or a whole-home clear-out. Those stay on their own pages.

A full room, every cupboard, and the balcony as well, is a clearance. This page does not become that clearance because a bean bag is in the pile. Name the room on the clearance page you are actually using, and name the bag here so it is not lost inside the word “contents”.

Do not promise the bag will be compressed on the vehicle. You do not know the load. You know the bag as it sits. The [load guide](/blog/how-much-fits-in-one-load-dubai) is why a soft shape and a rigid shape are not the same volume. You do not need a tonnage figure. You need the wide photo and the words “will not compact”.

## Measure the bag as it sits

Measure after the bag has settled, not while you are pressing it.

- Length, at the longest point on the floor.
- Width, at the widest point.
- Height, from the floor to the highest point of the slumped bag.

If a zip pull or a handle sticks out, include it. If the cover is off and the liner is smaller, measure the liner, and say the cover is separate in the same line. Do not measure a cushion you have folded on top and add that to the bag.

Write the figures in the note, or write “not measured”. A guess that says “about the size of a chair” is how a sofa gets booked. A chair-shaped bag is not a chair with legs. The figures, or the honest gap, are the report.

Compare the widest point with the doorway only if you have looked at the doorway. The [bulky-item note](/blog/bulky-item-pickup-dubai) is the photo and the route for a single awkward object. Use it for the way out. Stay on this page for the fill, the zip and the fact that the bag will not compact. Do not let that note turn the bag into a wardrobe or a framed picture.

If the bag will pass the door only when someone compresses it, write “needs compressing to pass the door” and do not do that compression on the stairs. That sentence is the warning. It is not an instruction to jump on the bag.

## Photos of the bag, not the sofa

Take the photos the request needs.

- One wide photo with the bag and the floor around it, so a sofa in the same room is visible if the sofa is staying or leaving.
- The zip, the split or the loose fill, close enough to see it.
- The cover off, if the cover is off, with the liner in the same frame.
- The doorway only if the bag is the thing that will not pass it.

Do not send a close-up of a logo and nothing else. Do not send a photo of the sofa and write “and a bean bag” with no view of the bag. A bag behind a sofa is invisible. Move the camera, or move the bag if the seam will allow it, until the bag is the object in the frame.

If beads are on the floor, include them. A photo of a tidy bag with a swept floor, when the beads are in a cupboard you forgot, describes half the job. Point at the gathered beads.

If you have two bags, photograph them apart, then say the count. A pile of two looks like one in a wide shot.

## One line in the booking

Say, in one message:

- one bean bag, the shape, and where it sits
- cover on or off, zip shut, zip stuck, or split
- fill inside, or fill partly out and gathered
- will not compact, and the three figures, or “not measured”
- anything else leaving from the same room, as its own line
- that it is not a sofa, not an armchair, and not a mattress

Ask to [book a junk collection](/) when the bag is the piece you want taken, with or without other household items you have already named. The decluttering guide stays the decision about the rest of a small flat. The sofa, the armchair and the mattress stay on their own guides.

Do not write “the seating” as the only words. Do not write “it will squash” if you have not seen it squash without a split. Do not ask for the bag to be cut open because a boot would then close. A cut is not a step on this page.

If you have not looked inside, you are early. Open the zip if it already moves. If the zip will not move, that stuck zip is the honest line. Send that. If the object has a rigid base, you are on the wrong note. Name the base and leave these steps.

On the morning, look at the seam again. A split that opened overnight is still part of the line. Gather the fill before you treat yesterday’s photo as current. Point at the bag as the bag. Point at a sofa only if the sofa is its own line.

The useful record is one bean bag, cover emptied or the reason written, fill named, will not compact, kept as its own line, written in plain words.
