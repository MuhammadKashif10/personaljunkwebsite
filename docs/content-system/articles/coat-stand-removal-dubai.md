The item is one coat stand, standing in the entry, and it is in the way of the door it has to leave through. Empty it, name its parts, and measure it against that door before you book. A coat stand looks like the smallest thing in the flat. In an entry it is often the tallest, and the hooks at the top are the part that catches.

This note is that one stand. A whole flat of furniture is the [apartment furniture guide](/blog/apartment-furniture-removal-dubai). One large piece with its own route problem, such as a wardrobe or a sofa, is the [bulky-item guide](/blog/bulky-item-pickup-dubai). A floor lamp that is too tall for the doorway is the [floor-lamp note](/blog/floor-lamp-taller-than-doorway-dubai). Come back here when the only object in the question is a freestanding coat stand by the front door. A narrow table against the hall wall, with a top for keys, is the [console-table note](/blog/console-table-removal-dubai).

## Take everything off it first

Lift every coat, bag, scarf, hat and umbrella off the stand. Put them somewhere that is not the entry. A stand with three jackets on it is not the stand you are describing. The jackets hide the hooks, add width, and make the photo useless for measuring.

Check the pockets of anything you lift off. Keys, a building access card, a parking remote and a wallet are the things that live on a coat stand. They are also the things that leave the flat by accident when a stand is carried out with a coat still on it. Put them in one place inside the flat. Write “stand emptied, pockets checked” in your note when you have done it.

Look at the base. Many stands have a ring or a tray near the floor for umbrellas, or a small shelf for shoes. Take out the umbrellas, the shoes, the dog lead and the bag of shopping bags that collects there. If the tray holds water from a wet umbrella, empty it and wipe it. Write “tray emptied” or “no tray”.

Some of what you took off may also be leaving. A broken umbrella or a worn-out bag is a separate line, not part of the stand. Put those items in their own pile and name them. Do not hang them back on the stand so they leave together. A stand that arrives at the door with items hanging from it is a stand that has not been emptied.

If someone else in the flat uses the stand, ask them before you empty it. Their coat is not yours to move into a cupboard, and their keys are not yours to collect. Empty only what you may touch. If a coat on the stand belongs to someone who is not there, write “one coat not mine, still on stand” and leave it. The stand is not ready until that coat is off.

## Name the kind of stand you have

A coat stand, for this note, is a freestanding piece whose job is to hold coats in an entry. It stands on the floor. It is not screwed to the wall as its main support. Write what you can see, in plain words.

The common shapes are these.

- A single pole: one upright, a ring of hooks at the top, and a base of legs or a weighted disc at the floor.
- A tree: a trunk with branches that are themselves the hooks, often wood, sometimes bent metal.
- A frame: two uprights joined by bars or a top rail, with hooks along the rail. Often wider than it is deep.
- A hall stand: a frame that also carries a mirror, a small bench, a shoe rack or a drawer.

Write which one. “Metal pole, six hooks, round base” is a description. “Coat stand” is a start. “Hall thing” is not.

Count the hooks. Write the number, and say whether they point out in a ring or in one direction. A ring of hooks makes the top of the stand wider than the pole. That width is part of the measurement, and it is usually the widest point.

Look at the material. Wood, metal, or wood with metal hooks. If the wood is split, write “split” and say where: “split at the base joint”. If a metal hook is bent, write “one hook bent”. Do not straighten it to make the photo neater. If a hook is missing, write “hook missing” and say whether it left a screw or a hole.

A hall stand with a mirror is a different object from a pole. The mirror is glass, and glass changes how the piece is carried. Write “mirror, fixed” or “mirror, lifts off”. If you can see that the mirror is cracked, write “mirror cracked” and do not lift it. Mirrors that stand with a dressing table stay on the [bedroom furniture guide](/blog/bedroom-furniture-removal-dubai). A mirror on a hall stand is still part of this stand.

If the stand has a bench, open its lid if it has one. Benches in an entry fill with shoes, school bags and sports kit. Empty it like the base. Write “bench emptied”. If the bench is a separate piece that only sits next to the stand, it is a second line. Do not join two pieces into one description because they stand together.

## Why the entry is the snag

The stand is usually in the narrowest part of the flat. Many entries are a short corridor with the front door at one end and a cupboard, a shoe cabinet or a kitchen opening along one side. The stand was put in the one corner that was left. Now it has to come out through the door it stands beside.

Two things make that harder than the size of the stand suggests.

The first is the hooks. A pole stand is narrow at the floor and wide at the top. When you tilt it to pass a door, the ring of hooks swings in an arc. The arc catches the frame, the light switch, the door closer and the top of the opening. A stand that is narrower than the door can still fail to pass it because of where the hooks travel when it tips.

The second is the door. A front door in a flat often has a closer arm at the top, a chain, a viewer and a deep frame. The clear opening is smaller than the door leaf. A front door also tends to close on its own. If it swings back while the stand is tilted through it, the stand takes the hit, and so does the frame.

Write both facts. “Ring of eight hooks at the top. Front door has a closer.” Those two lines tell the person reading the booking more than a photo of the stand on its own.

## Measure the stand, then the door

Measure the stand standing up. Height from the floor to the top of the highest hook. Width across the widest point, which is usually the hooks. Width of the base at the floor. If it is a frame or a hall stand, add the depth from front to back.

Then measure the front door. Open it as far as it opens. Measure the clear gap from frame to door edge, not the width of the door leaf. Measure the height from the floor to the lowest thing across the top: the frame, the closer arm, or a light. If there is a mat in the opening, lift the mat or write “mat in opening”.

If the stand has to pass another door first, such as a glass door between the entry and the living room, measure that one too. Name the tighter door. That door limits the stand.

If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. A guess written as a measurement is worse than no measurement, because it will be trusted. This note does not keep a standard door size, and it does not keep a standard coat-stand size.

A stand that is taller than the front door has to tip to leave. Write “taller than the door, has to tip”. Then look at what is above the door on the inside. If a light, a smoke detector or a shelf sits close above the frame, write it down. A tipping stand reaches up before it reaches out.

Check the ceiling height in the entry. Some entries have a lowered ceiling or a bulkhead that hides pipes or ducts. A tall stand may have been carried in before a ceiling was boxed in, or assembled in place. Write “low ceiling in entry” if the top of the stand is close to it.

## What already comes off, and what stays on

Many pole stands come apart. The top section, with the hooks, screws into the pole. The pole screws into the base. Some hooks screw into the top section one by one. This is the same kind of fact the dismantling guide decides on a larger piece, and on a coat stand it is often simple.

If the top unscrews by hand, and you already do that when you move the stand, you may unscrew it. Write “top off, by hand”. Put the top and any loose hooks in one bag or tie them together, and keep them with the pole. If the base unscrews by hand, the same rule applies. Write “base off, by hand”.

If a section is stiff, do not force it with pliers or a wrench you do not normally use on it. A stuck thread on an old stand can strip, split a wooden pole, or leave a sharp end. Write “top does not unscrew” and leave it on. A stand that leaves in one piece is fine, so long as the note says so.

Do not cut a stand down. Do not saw the top off to make it shorter than the door. Do not snap the hooks off. If the stand will only leave whole and tipped, that is the report. The decision on what to take apart, on furniture built to come apart where the screws are the work, stays on the [dismantling guide](/blog/furniture-dismantling-dubai). A pole stand whose sections already unscrew by hand is not that job.

A hall stand with a mirror is different. If the mirror lifts off a bracket by hand, and you already do that when you clean it, you may lift it off. Lean it face in against a wall, away from the route, and write “mirror off, leaning, glass facing wall”. If the mirror is screwed on, leave it. Do not unscrew a mirror and then carry the glass on its own without a plan. Write “mirror screwed on, left on”.

Keep every small part with the stand. Screws, plastic caps from the feet, a loose hook, the bolt that joins a frame. Put them in one bag. Tape the bag to the pole or put it on the bench. Write “parts bagged, with stand”. A coat stand that arrives as three separate pieces and a missing hook has become a different job from the one you described.

## Is it fixed to the wall?

Look behind the top of the stand. Some tall stands, and many hall stands, have a strap, a bracket or a screw that ties them to the wall so they do not tip. This is common in homes with children. It may have been added after the stand was bought.

If you can see a strap or bracket, write “tied to the wall”. Say where: “strap at the top, into the wall behind”. Then decide who is allowed to undo it. In a rented flat, the screw and the plug in the wall are part of the wall. The stand is yours. The hole may not be. This note does not decide what a tenancy says about holes in a wall, and it does not repair one.

If the strap releases by hand, with a clip or a buckle, you may release it. Write “strap released, by hand, screw still in wall”. If it needs a tool, leave it tied and write “still tied, needs tool”. Do not pull the stand forward to break the strap. A stand that is tied to the wall and then pulled loose takes plaster with it.

A rail of hooks screwed flat to the wall is not a coat stand. Neither is a hook board, a row of pegs, or a fitted hall cupboard. Those are fixed. If one is leaving, it is a fixture, and its removal is a decision for the owner of the wall. This note does not give a method for taking a fixed rail down. Write “fixed rail, not leaving” or leave it out of the photo.

## Who decides that the stand goes

In a furnished rental, the stand may belong to the landlord. Many furnished flats come with a coat stand in the entry, and it is easy to forget it was on the inventory. Check the inventory before you book. If it lists “coat stand” or “hall stand”, the stand stays unless the landlord or the agent says, in writing, that it may go. This note does not interpret that inventory.

In a shared flat, the stand may belong to one flatmate who has already left. The [note on furniture a flatmate left](/blog/flatmate-left-furniture-dubai) covers that question. Use it before you send this stand away on someone else’s behalf.

If the stand is yours, and you are keeping the flat, the question is only whether it leaves. Write “mine, leaving”. That line removes a lot of doubt for the person reading the booking.

## What is not this stand

A clothes rail on wheels, the kind used for drying or for an overflow wardrobe, is not a coat stand. It is longer, it is often in a bedroom, and it is not given a URL of its own here. Name it as a clothes rail on the [apartment furniture list](/blog/apartment-furniture-removal-dubai) if it is leaving. Do not call it a coat stand to use this note.

A shoe cabinet in the entry is not this stand. It is a cabinet with doors and shelves, and it has contents. If it is leaving, it is the [shoe-cabinet note](/blog/shoe-cabinet-removal-dubai). A console table, a key bowl, a mirror on a hook, a bench that stands alone and an umbrella bucket are each their own line. Do not stand them together and write “the hall” as if it were one piece.

A floor lamp is not a coat stand, even when it is the same height and stands in the same corner. A lamp has a shade, a cable, a bulb and a switch. Use the floor-lamp note for it. Do not hang a coat on a lamp to make it fit here.

A wardrobe in the entry is a wardrobe. The [wardrobe guide](/blog/wardrobe-removal-dubai) keeps it, even when it holds coats. A built-in cupboard in the entry is part of the flat and is not leaving.

A whole hallway of furniture, cleared for a handover, is part of a larger job. Use the apartment furniture guide when the entry is one room among several. The stand can still be one line inside that list. Keep it as one line with its own words, so it is not lost inside “hall furniture”.

Add the floor and the building. Add the measurements, or “not measured”. Add “stand emptied, pockets checked”. Add “top off, by hand” or “left whole”. Add a lift or loading note if you have already been given one. If you have not, write “no lift instruction yet”. Do not invent a lift size, and do not write a building rule you have not been given.

Take the photos in the light the entry already has. Entries are often dark. If the hooks cannot be seen, open the front door for light, or say “dark entry, hooks hard to see”. A dark photo with a confident description does not help.

## Clear the route before the visit

On the day, the stand should be standing where it is, or moved a short step out of the corner, empty. Do not lay it on the floor of the entry. A stand on the floor blocks the door and is easy to step on. If it has to be laid down, put it along a wall in the living room, with the hooks away from the walkway.

Keep the front door clear. Take the shoe pile, the bags and the mat out of the opening. If the door has a closer that pulls it shut, be ready to hold the door. Do not wedge it with a shoe. Do not prop it with the stand.

Keep children and pets out of the entry while the stand is moved. A tilted stand with a ring of hooks at head height is the reason. If the stand was tied to the wall because of children, the children are probably still in the flat.

If the corridor outside the flat is shared, do not stand the coat stand in it to wait. Leave it inside. A tall stand in a shared corridor is in the way of everyone else, and it may tip if someone brushes it.

## One line in the booking

You can [take the stand with other furniture](/services/furniture-removal) once it is empty, named, measured or marked “not measured”, and the front door is described. That page is the furniture leaving the flat. It is not a request to unscrew a stuck thread, to take a fixed rail off the wall, or to repair the plaster behind a strap.

If the coat stand is the only thing leaving, it can still be booked on its own. The [junk removal](/services/junk-removal) page is for a single pickup as well as a full load. Say that it is one stand, and say whether anything else from the same pile is going.

Say, in one message:

- one coat stand, and its shape: pole, tree, frame or hall stand
- the number of hooks, and whether they point out in a ring
- emptied, with pockets checked
- the height, the widest point and the base, or “not measured”
- the clear width and height of the front door, and of a tighter door, or “not measured”
- a door closer, a light or a detector close above the frame, if you can see one
- top off by hand, base off by hand, mirror off, or left whole
- tied to the wall, released by hand, or still tied
- anything that is not this stand: a clothes rail, a shoe cabinet, a floor lamp, a fixed rail

Do not write “coat stand” as the only words. Do not write “small item” because the stand is light. Do not ask for the top to be cut off. The hooks and the door are the report.

If the stand belongs to a landlord’s inventory, leave it off the note until the release is in writing. If the stand is tied to a wall you do not own, say so, and say who you have asked.

The apartment furniture guide remains the list when the whole flat is the job. The bulky-item guide remains the method for a single piece whose route is the problem. This page is the coat stand. Use it so the stand is emptied before it is counted, and so the hooks are measured before they meet the door.
